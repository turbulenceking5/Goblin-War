# Factions, Territory & War Economy (index.html)

The first "living world" system in the game — kingdoms fight over settlements in the background while the player does other things, their populations shrink, flee, or grow because of it, and the Marketplace reacts to all of it. Lives entirely in index.html, keyed off the same weekly tick.

## Two separate ideas: identity vs. control

A settlement's *historical* kingdom and race (`graph.burgs[id].state`/`.race`, from the Azgaar export — see [data-files.md](data-files.md)) never change. That's its permanent identity, same as a real place keeps its name after changing hands. Its *current controller* is a separate, sparse override — tracked by the specific **kingdom**, not just its race, so that a captured settlement's population and military weight belong to the one kingdom that actually holds it, not to "whichever kingdoms happen to share its race":

- `goblinwar_territoryControl`: `{burgId: kingdomName}` (e.g. `"Warg Horde"`), only present for settlements that have actually been fought over. `getControllerKingdom(burgId)` checks this map first and falls back to `graph.burgs[burgId].state` — so an unconquered settlement's controller is always just its own original kingdom, with no explicit entry needed.
- `getController(burgId)` is the race-level convenience most of the codebase actually wants (`KINGDOM_RACE[getControllerKingdom(burgId)]`, falling back to `graph.burgs[burgId].race` for a neutral burg with no kingdom at all) — onMapTap, `showLocationView`'s subtitle, and `getFigureForBurg` only ever needed "which race holds this," so they call this rather than the kingdom-level accessor.
- `setController(burgId, kingdom)` writes a kingdom-name override. Nothing ever deletes one (a settlement can change hands multiple times, but there's no "restore original owner" action).
- **Backward-compat:** saves from before kingdom-level tracking stored a bare race string here instead (e.g. `"ork"`). `getController` still resolves those correctly (a stored value that's itself a real `ALLIANCE` key is treated as already being a race). `getArmyStrength` (below) can't recover which specific kingdom made an old-format capture, though — that settlement's population simply stops counting toward any kingdom's strength until it's captured again, which writes the new format. Self-healing, not silently wrong.

This deliberately avoids renaming anything — `graph.burgs[id].state`/`.name` stay put forever. Wherever the game already shows a settlement's race/kingdom, it should show the *current controller*, not necessarily the original — see "Where this shows up" below.

## War is a relations matrix between specific kingdoms, not a per-kingdom flag

`goblinwar_relations`: `{"KingdomA::KingdomB": "war"|"peace"}`, keyed by `getRelationKey(k1,k2)` (the two kingdom names, sorted, joined with `::`, so `getRelation(a,b)` and `getRelation(b,a)` always agree). An absent entry defaults to `"peace"` — same sparse-override philosophy as `goblinwar_territoryControl`/`goblinwar_population`. Only opposite-alliance pairs are ever meaningful, since same-alliance kingdoms never fight (see `ALLIANCE` below); `getRelation`/`setRelation` are the raw accessors, and `getEnemiesAtWar(kingdom)` returns every opposite-alliance kingdom currently at war with it.

A kingdom can be at war with one specific enemy while at peace with everyone else — a real change from the earlier single `"war"|"peace"` flag per kingdom. `getWarState(kingdom)` still exists with its original signature (`getEnemiesAtWar(kingdom).length ? 'war' : 'peace'`) purely as a derived convenience, so every caller that only ever needed the coarser "is this kingdom fighting *someone*" question — Marketplace pricing, `pickAmbushEnemy`, Guard House's flavor line, Petition for Troops — needed no changes at all. `isAtWarAt(burgId)` is unchanged too (looks up `graph.burgs[burgId].state`, then that kingdom's derived war state).

**No migration for old saves.** `goblinwar_relations` simply starts `{}` (everyone at peace) for any character, old or new — wars begin fresh under the pairwise system rather than trying to reconstruct "who was fighting whom" from the old binary flag, the same "self-healing, not silently wrong" approach `goblinwar_territoryControl`'s own legacy-format handling already takes above.

## Faction data

`KINGDOM_RACE` (kingdom name → race) and `ALL_KINGDOMS` (the ~24 kingdom names) are built once by `buildFactionData()`, called right after `graph` loads (alongside `buildAdjacency()`). Derived by scanning every burg for its `state`/`race` pair — cheap, done once, not persisted (rebuilt fresh every page load from `travel-graph.json`, which is why it's safe to never invalidate).

`ALLIANCE` (`{human:"Good", dwarf:"Good", ork:"Bad", goblin:"Bad"}`, near `RACE_LABEL`) is what "enemy" means for war/raid purposes — any kingdom whose race is on the other side.

### Burg adjacency — a real border, not a proximity guess

`travel-graph.json` has no burg-to-burg or kingdom-to-kingdom border data at all — `graph.edges` only connects map *cells*, most of which aren't a settlement. `buildBurgAdjacency()` (called once after `buildAdjacency()`, alongside `buildFactionData()`) derives a real one: for every burg, it BFSes outward through the existing cell-level `adjacency` graph and, along each branch, stops at the first cell that's home to some *other* burg — collecting those as that burg's neighbors into a module-level `burgAdjacency` map (`burgId -> [burgId, ...]`), never persisted, same "derived fresh every load" treatment as `KINGDOM_RACE`/`ALL_KINGDOMS`.

`isFrontlineBurg(burgId)` uses it: true when any neighbor is controlled by an opposite-alliance kingdom this burg's controller currently has a `"war"` relation with. `buildFrontlineMarkers()` draws a small red ring (in the SVG overlay's `#frontline-markers` group, alongside `#city-icons`) on every burg that qualifies, rebuilt once at load and again after every weekly tick (`maybeRunFactionAI` calls it) — the map's visual read of where the fighting is actually happening. `isFrontlineBurg` is now a thin wrapper around `getFrontlineEnemyKingdom(burgId)`, which does the same adjacency check but returns the specific bordering kingdom's name (or `null`) instead of a boolean — added for "Reactive rumors" (see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)/[roadmap.md](roadmap.md)), whose frontline flavor lines need to actually name who you're bordering, not just know that you are.

## The weekly tick

`maybeRunFactionAI()` runs at the end of every `advanceDays(n)` call — so it fires after travel arrival, Inn/camp rest, or a partial-travel camp, whichever advances the calendar. It compares `gameDay` against `goblinwar_lastWarTick` and does nothing unless at least `WAR_TICK_INTERVAL_DAYS` (7) have passed, then immediately stamps `lastWarTick` to the current `gameDay` before running `runFactionAITick()` — so a long journey that jumps the calendar by more than a week still only ticks once, not once per day skipped. It also calls `buildFrontlineMarkers()` afterward so the map's border markers stay current.

**Performance: a tick-scoped read cache.** `getArmyStrength`/`findNearestPeacefulSettlement` each re-scan every one of the ~800 settlements, and `runFactionAITick`'s own callees (`applyWarLoss`, `advanceSiege`) call them a great many times over the course of one tick — measured directly against the real map data, a naive implementation where every `getXxx()` (territory control, population, relations, sieges, reinforcements) does a fresh `JSON.parse(localStorage...)` on every call took **1.6–5.5+ seconds per tick** once a save had run long enough that most settlements carried an override, and kept getting slower as more accumulated — a real, player-visible freeze, not a theoretical concern. `_tickCache` (a module-level variable, `null` outside of a tick) fixes this: `runFactionAITick` populates it with one parsed copy of each sparse map at the start and clears it in a `finally` block at the end; every `getXxx()` checks it first and, when active, returns the *same* cached object on every call — so a `setXxx()`'s in-place mutation (they still all write straight through to `localStorage` too, unchanged) is immediately visible to every later read in the same tick without a separate invalidation step. `getWarState` additionally memoizes its own per-kingdom result inside that cache (`_tickCache.warState`), since `getEnemiesAtWar`'s full `ALL_KINGDOMS` scan was itself being re-run millions of times per tick via `isAtWarAt` inside `findNearestPeacefulSettlement`'s scan — safe because nothing after `runFactionAITick`'s first (declare/peace) pass ever calls `setRelation` again, so a kingdom's war state can't actually change partway through the second pass that depends on it. Together these bring a tick down to roughly 40–300ms even at 24/24 kingdoms simultaneously at war with dozens of active sieges — outside of a tick (Marketplace, Guard House, onMapTap, and the like), every getter still does its normal fresh, uncached read, since those call sites are infrequent and were never the actual cost problem.

`runFactionAITick()` runs in four passes:

1. **War/peace rolls, once per cross-alliance kingdom pair** (same-alliance kingdoms are skipped entirely — they never fight). At peace, unless a war-exhaustion treaty is still cooling down between them (`isWarCooldownActive`, see "War exhaustion & reparations" below), `WAR_DECLARE_CHANCE` (0.65%) chance to flip that specific pair to `"war"` (and stamp `setWarStart`). At war, a chance to flip back to `"peace"` that starts at `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE` (0.1%) and climbs the longer this specific pair has been fighting — see "War exhaustion & reparations" below — which also calls `cancelSiegesBetween(k1,k2)`, ending any active siege between exactly those two kingdoms (the defender keeps whatever it still held; no partial credit) and turning back any `goblinwar_armies` entry still marching or sieging between that same pair (see "Armies march before a siege starts" below — without this a stale army record would permanently block a fresh one from ever being mustered between this pair again), then `settleWarExhaustionPeace` to settle reparations/cooldown if the war produced a clear winner. Both rates are deliberately low — per the project owner, a given kingdom pair's war or peace should typically hold for *years* of in-game time, not weeks (see "War exhaustion & reparations" below for the actual simulated durations) — but with ~24 kingdoms each rolling independently every week, the world as a whole still sees a new war break out somewhere every couple of weeks.
2. **Attrition and sieges, once per kingdom, per specific enemy it's at war with.** A kingdom with no active war relations does nothing this pass. Otherwise every settlement that originally belongs to it bleeds `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` attrition (see "Population, armies & refugees" below), and for *each* enemy it's currently at war with, `RAID_CHANCE` (35%) chance to call `advanceSiege(kingdom, enemy)`.
3. **Regrowth, once per settlement.** The mirror image of pass 2, not scoped to kingdom pairs — see "Population regrowth" below.
4. **Autonomous reinforcement, once per currently-peaceful kingdom.** The "Troops" payoff of per-kingdom reputation (see "Per-kingdom reputation" below) — a kingdom the player is `Trusted` with can reinforce its own most-threatened ally unprompted, `TRUSTED_REINFORCEMENT_CHANCE` (35%) chance per week.

If any siege was resolved (a settlement actually captured) during the tick, `maybeRunFactionAI()` shows one toast summarizing it (e.g. `"Grimstan has fallen to Human forces from Kingdom of Bary."`, or a count if more than one) — declarations of war/peace and mid-siege progress don't get a toast, since with 24 kingdoms rolling independently every week that would be constant noise; captures (and, now, a war-exhaustion peace that produced a clear winner — see below) are the events worth the player's attention.

## War exhaustion & reparations

Per the project owner: a war shouldn't just run forever at the flat `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE` above, and when a long war *does* end, whoever's actually winning it should keep what they took, get paid for the trouble, and be barred from immediately picking the fight back up.

- **The clock.** `goblinwar_warStarts` (`{"KingdomA::KingdomB": gameDay}`, same sorted-pair key as `goblinwar_relations`) stamps the tick a pair's war began (pass 1's declare roll, and `acceptInciteWar`) and is cleared the moment that pair returns to peace by any means (`clearWarStart` — pass 1's own peace roll, and `acceptNegotiatePeace`). A war with no recorded start — any war already underway in a save from before this shipped — is treated as having just started rather than infinitely old, same "self-healing, not silently wrong" precedent the legacy territory-control format set. Every tick a pair stays at war, `WAR_EXHAUSTION_GRACE_WEEKS` (260 — five full years) ticks of grace pass with no change, then each further tick adds `WAR_EXHAUSTION_PEACE_BONUS_PER_WEEK` (0.02 percentage points) on top of `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE`'s 0.1% floor, capped at `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE_MAX` (60%) so peace is never a certainty. **Simulated against this actual weekly-tick math** (a Monte Carlo, not just the naive 1/chance expected value, since the hazard rate itself climbs over time): a given kingdom pair's peace holds a mean ~3 years (2-4 year range) before a new war breaks out, and once at war, that war runs a mean ~5.5-6 years (5-10 year range, occasionally longer) before ending — a war is deliberately harder to end than the peace before it was to break, both with wide variance so a short skirmish or a generation-long grinding war are both still possible outcomes, just not the norm either way.
- **Picking a winner.** When a pair's peace roll actually succeeds, `settleWarExhaustionPeace` calls `determineWarWinner`, which compares each side's real territorial gains — `countSettlementsCapturedFrom(A,B)` counts B's *original* (`b.state`) settlements A currently controls (`getControllerKingdom`), and vice versa. Whoever captured more wins; a tie (including the common case of two kingdoms that fought for weeks without either finishing a siege) has no winner at all — an ordinary, consequence-free peace, exactly like before this feature shipped.
- **The winner's two rewards.** Keeping the captured ground needed no new code — `setController` was already one-way. The two new effects:
  - **Reparations.** `setTribute(loser, winner, gameDay + WAR_TRIBUTE_DURATION_DAYS)` (1,095 days — 3 years, the middle of the new peace-duration range above) writes `goblinwar_tribute` (`{kingdomName: {role:'payer'|'receiver', counterpart, expiresDay}}`, one active tribute per kingdom in either role — a new war-exhaustion peace overwrites rather than stacks, same precedent `setReinforcement` already set). Kingdoms don't carry a treasury anywhere else in this system, so "gets paid by the defeated kingdom" is modeled the same way every other kingdom-level effect on the player already is: `getTributeMultiplier(burgId)` folds into `getBuyPrice` as one more multiplicative factor alongside `getSettlementDiscount`/`getReputationDiscount` — `WAR_TRIBUTE_SURCHARGE` (1.15×) at the payer's own settlements, `WAR_TRIBUTE_DISCOUNT` (0.9×) at the receiver's — surfaced as a note on both the Marketplace's and Blacksmith's existing war/peace banner (`buildTributeNote`) and a row on Guard House (`buildGuardHouseStats`).
  - **A cooldown.** `setWarCooldown(winner, loser, gameDay + WAR_TREATY_COOLDOWN_DAYS)` (also 1,095 days) writes `goblinwar_warCooldowns` (same sorted-pair key shape again). While active, `isWarCooldownActive(k1,k2)` blocks pass 1's declare roll for that exact pair, and is also checked by `generateInciteWarOffer`'s candidate filter — so the player can't pay to route around a treaty that just got signed. **Known simplification, deliberate:** `goblinwar_relations`' war/peace state has no concept of "who's the aggressor" for a given pair — `advanceSiege`'s attacker/defender roles are decided fresh, per enemy, every tick, not tied to who first declared — so a literal one-sided "only the winner can't attack" isn't something the pairwise relations model can express. What's built instead is the closest honest equivalent: the whole pair is barred from re-entering war, a real peace treaty holding for both sides rather than a one-way leash on just the winner. `getTreatyBoundEnemies(kingdom)` (used by Guard House's display) is the reverse lookup — every kingdom a treaty currently bars *this* one from fighting again.
- Both the toast and `recordWorldEvent`'s Tavern Talk rumour name the outcome plainly (`"{loser} sues for peace with {winner} after a long war — {winner} keeps the ground it took and will collect reparations."`) — a war-exhaustion peace with a clear winner gets the same toast treatment a settlement capture does, since a whole war concluding with reparations changing hands is at least as notable.

**Real outcomes also feed a small rumour log, separate from the toast above.** `recordWorldEvent(text)` (index.html) appends to `goblinwar_recentEvents` (capped at `RECENT_EVENTS_MAX` = 15, trim-oldest, newest first) — called from pass 2's capture branch (a rumour-flavored line distinct from the toast's direct wording) and from `cancelSiegesBetween` (pass 1's peace roll, and `acceptNegotiatePeace` — the one place a repelled siege would otherwise leave no trace once its entry is deleted). `getRecentEventTexts()` feeds these into Tavern Talk's rumour pool — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s "The Tavern" and [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Reactive rumors" for the full write-up. Deliberately not part of `_tickCache` — it's only read outside the tick (by the Tavern) and only written a handful of times per tick at most, nowhere near the per-settlement scan volume that made caching worthwhile for the other sparse maps above.

## Kingdom elimination

Before this, a kingdom that lost every settlement it ever held just kept existing in the war/relations matrix forever — still rolling `WAR_DECLARE_CHANCE`/war-exhaustion peace checks, still bleeding `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` population (see the bug fixed below), permanently stuck at war with anyone it happened to be fighting when its last settlement fell, since a kingdom with zero territory can never muster an army (`findArmyOrigin` already returns `null` with nothing to draw one from) to fight back or force its own peace.

- **Detection.** `isKingdomEliminated(kingdom)` — derived, not stored: true once `getControllerKingdom` returns something other than `kingdom` for every settlement in `graph.burgs` (current ownership, the same accessor `getArmyStrength`/`findArmyOrigin` already key off — not `b.state`, a settlement's static original owner). Deliberately uncached even inside `_tickCache`'s tick-scoped window: it only ever runs right after an actual capture (see below), a rare event next to the tick's other per-kingdom-per-week checks, so a fresh scan each time is cheap and — unlike `_tickCache`'s memoized maps — can never go stale if two different kingdoms happen to finish off the same enemy's last two settlements within the same week.
- **The trigger.** `setController` has exactly one call site (`advanceSiege`'s capture branch), so that's the only place elimination needs checking: right after pass 2 pushes an ordinary "X has fallen to Y" capture event, it checks `isKingdomEliminated(enemy)` and calls `eliminateKingdom(enemy, events)` if the defender's very last settlement just went.
- **What elimination actually does.** `eliminateKingdom` settles every war the fallen kingdom is still in, not just the one against whoever delivered the final blow — a real scenario, not a hypothetical: kingdom C can be at war with kingdom B on a completely separate front from the one kingdom A actually conquered B through, and C's war needs ending too. For each such enemy: `settleWarExhaustionPeace` runs first, so whichever kingdom actually did the conquering still collects the same reparations/cooldown a real war-exhaustion peace would (`determineWarWinner`'s territorial-gains math, no special-casing for the elimination case) — but that alone isn't sufficient, since `determineWarWinner` can come back with no winner for a specific pair (C captured nothing from B directly, even though B is globally wiped out), so the explicit `setRelation(...,'peace')`/`clearWarStart`/`cancelSiegesBetween` calls that follow it are what actually guarantee the war ends either way, reparations or not. `cancelSiegesBetween` also turns back any of the fallen kingdom's own armies still marching or sieging against that enemy — deliberately, rather than letting them resolve on their own, since a stray leftover army going on to capture something afterward would "revive" an already-announced-fallen kingdom, reading as a bug rather than a feature. The elimination event is `unshift`ed onto `events` rather than `push`ed, so "X has fallen" always leads `maybeRunFactionAI`'s "N settlements changed hands, including..." summary toast rather than being crowded out by an ordinary capture that happened to get pushed first — the bigger-moment treatment the project owner asked for, without a new modal (see Known Simplifications below).
- **A real bug fixed along the way.** The weekly tick's attrition line used to bleed population from every settlement whose `b.state` (original owner) matched the kingdom at war, regardless of who actually controlled it now — meaning a settlement already captured away from a still-warring kingdom kept bleeding population under its *old* owner's war status forever. Now keyed off `getControllerKingdom` (current ownership) like everywhere else in this file, so a captured settlement's population loss follows its new controller's own war status, not a stale original one. This was the concrete mechanism the elimination gap actually described: an eliminated kingdom's now-foreign-owned original territory would otherwise have kept bleeding under its fallen banner indefinitely.
- **Staying eliminated.** Pass 1's top-of-tick declare/peace loop skips any pair where either kingdom `isKingdomEliminated` before rolling anything — without this, a later week's `WAR_DECLARE_CHANCE` roll could drag a zero-territory kingdom back into a war it can never fight or end on its own. `generateInciteWarOffer`'s candidate filter excludes eliminated kingdoms too, for the same reason from the other direction: once a fallen kingdom's relations are all peace (which elimination itself sets), it would otherwise start qualifying as an incite-war target with nothing left to actually go to war over.
- **The roadmap's open questions, answered.** No explicit `NOTABLE_FIGURES` retirement step was needed — `getFigureForBurg` already returns `null` once a figure's home settlement isn't controlled by its own race, and elimination means every one of the fallen kingdom's settlements already meets that condition; this generalizes for free. No alliance-wide strength aggregation exists or was added — nothing today pools `getArmyStrength` above the single-kingdom level, so "does the rest of the alliance get stronger" stays exactly as unanswered (and unneeded) as it already was. And elimination is not reversible: with no way to muster an army without owning territory to draw it from, a fallen kingdom can never reclaim ground on its own — consistent with `setController` already being one-way everywhere else in this file.

**Known simplifications:**
- **A toast, not a modal.** The roadmap's own sketch floated a bigger, more ceremonial treatment for "a whole kingdom has fallen" than an ordinary settlement-capture toast. This ships with the same toast pipeline every other faction-tick event already uses (war-exhaustion peace, an army routed in the field) — `unshift`ed to the front of the week's summary for real prominence, but no new modal/UI. Consistent with this file's existing conventions, and a real modal remains a valid, separate upgrade later if the project owner wants more ceremony.
- The now-fallen kingdom's own settlements simply stay with whoever holds them — no separate "territory reverts to neutral" step, consistent with `setController` already being one-way with no restore-original-owner action.

## Sieges: a persistent, multi-week conflict over one settlement

`goblinwar_sieges`: `{burgId: {attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom, startedDay, progress}}` — at most one active siege per settlement (same "one active X" simplicity precedent as Notable Figure/Alliance Reinforcement, below). This replaces the old single-tick "raid resolves instantly" design with something that actually has duration.

- **`pickRaidTarget(attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom)`** chooses which of the defender's settlements to besiege. It now prefers a *real* border settlement — one of the defender's burgs directly reachable, via `burgAdjacency` (above), from territory the attacker already controls — over the old "nearest by straight-line distance to my territory's centroid" guess. That guess is still there as a fallback, used only when the two kingdoms' territory doesn't actually touch (e.g. separated by sea or unclaimed land), same `RAID_CANDIDATE_POOL` (15) it always used.
- **`advanceSiege(attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom)`** either starts a new siege (via `pickRaidTarget`) or advances the one already running between those two kingdoms. Progress per tick comes from the same clamped attacker/defender strength ratio the old single-shot raid used (`attackerStrength / (attackerStrength + defenderStrength)`, clamped to `[RAID_SUCCESS_MIN, RAID_SUCCESS_MAX]` = `0.2`–`0.8`), scaled by `SIEGE_PROGRESS_MAX_PER_TICK` (45) — so a lopsided fight can still resolve in two or three weeks, while an even one drags on much longer, and no kingdom is ever mathematically guaranteed to take or hold a settlement. The besieged settlement also takes `RAID_DAMAGE_RATE` attrition *every* tick the siege continues, not just at the start. At `progress >= 100` the settlement's controller flips (`setController`) and the siege entry is removed.
- **`getGarrisonStrength(burgId)`** (`getPopulation(burgId) * MILITARY_FRACTION`) is a settlement's own local defense, surfaced in the Guard House panel — distinct from `getArmyStrength(kingdom)`, that kingdom's whole mobilizable strength, which is what actually decides siege progress.
- Guard House (`buildGuardHouseStats`) shows an extra "Under siege by {attackerKingdom} — {progress}%" row whenever `goblinwar_sieges[burgId]` exists.

## Armies march before a siege starts

Per the project owner: two kingdoms at war should have visible armies on the map, not just an abstract siege percentage — a request that came with a bigger, deliberately-separate follow-up idea (armies meeting and fighting in the open field, with the player able to join) tracked in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Armies meet and fight in the field." This section is the first, shipped half of that: real marching armies that resolve into the existing siege system on arrival. The open-field-battle half is still not built — see roadmap.md for that design.

`goblinwar_armies`: a JSON array, `{id, kingdom, defenderKingdom, race, originBurgId, targetBurgId, routePts:[{x,y}...], routeMiles, progressMiles, x, y, strength, status:'marching'|'sieging', spawnedDay, lastAdvancedDay}`. Up to `MAX_ARMIES_PER_ENEMY_PAIR` (3) active armies per attacking-kingdom-per-enemy pair at once — no longer just one, per the project owner's follow-up ask below.

- **Spawning, and a real population cost.** The weekly tick's second pass (attrition/sieges, per kingdom per enemy it's at war with) used to roll `RAID_CHANCE` straight into `advanceSiege`, which created the `goblinwar_sieges` entry immediately via `pickRaidTarget`. Now that roll instead **musters an army** (`spawnArmy`) whenever fewer than `MAX_ARMIES_PER_ENEMY_PAIR` already exist for that attacker→enemy pair — the siege entry itself is only written once an army actually *arrives*. `spawnArmy` picks a target (the pair's own already-in-flight target if one exists — see "Reinforcement and merging" below — otherwise the same pick `pickRaidTarget` always made), an origin via `findArmyOrigin` (the attacker's own capital, falling back to whichever settlement it still controls that's nearest the target if the capital's itself been captured), and a real route via `computeDistanceBetween`/`buildRoutePolyline` — the exact same road/sea pathfinding player travel itself uses (`cellRoute`, see [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md)), not a new pathing system. Per the project owner: mustering now actually costs the origin settlement people, not just an abstract number, and armies shouldn't all come out the same size — `strength` is set to `getPopulation(origin) * musterFraction`, where `musterFraction` is randomized per muster between `ARMY_MUSTER_FRACTION_MIN` and `ARMY_MUSTER_FRACTION_MAX` (half and one-and-a-half times `MILITARY_FRACTION` — the same share `getGarrisonStrength`/`getArmyStrength` already treat as "fieldable" — so the *average* muster still costs/produces what a flat `MILITARY_FRACTION` would, but any single one is a genuinely different size, a small probing force or a full call-to-arms). That same amount is genuinely subtracted from the origin's population (`setPopulation`) rather than staying a theoretical pool. This cost is permanent — not refunded if the army later disbands or is destroyed, same one-way-loss precedent attrition/siege damage/Raid a Village already set — so a kingdom that musters reinforcement after reinforcement in a long war measurably thins its own capital; regrowth (`applyPopulationGrowth`) only resumes once that kingdom is at peace again.
- **An army's own strength, not the whole kingdom's, decides a siege now.** `advanceSiege(attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom, attackerArmy)` takes a third argument now — the arrived army — and uses `attackerArmy.strength` in place of the old `getArmyStrength(attackerKingdom)`, and `getGarrisonStrength(burgId)` (that one settlement's own local defense) in place of the old `getArmyStrength(defenderKingdom)` (the whole defending kingdom's pool). This is the real payoff of armies having their own size: a single muster from a modest settlement can genuinely be too weak to take a well-defended city, which is the point — and it's also what finally gives `getGarrisonStrength` (see "Sieges: a persistent, multi-week conflict over one settlement" below) the real mechanical teeth its own comment always said it was missing, rather than just being a number shown on Guard House. `TRUSTED_DEFENSE_BONUS` still applies the same way, just to the new garrison-based figure. Every other use of `getArmyStrength(kingdom)` — Petition for Troops, Negotiate Peace/Incite War pricing, Guard House's display, War Help's reinforcement math — is untouched; only the siege-resolution formula itself changed inputs.
- **Reinforcement and merging.** Per the project owner: a kingdom whose first muster is too weak to take its target should be able to send more armies to combine into one bigger force, not just try and fail forever. Pass 2 now rolls a second, independent `RAID_CHANCE` check per enemy (alongside advancing any already-sieging army) to muster a *reinforcement* — `spawnArmy(kingdom, enemy, existingTargetBurgId)` — explicitly aimed at the pair's own already-in-flight target rather than an unrelated fresh pick, capped at `MAX_ARMIES_PER_ENEMY_PAIR` total in flight. `mergeArmies()` (called at the end of every `advanceArmies()` pass) then combines any two armies that share the same kingdom *and* the same `targetBurgId` once they're within `ARMY_MERGE_RADIUS` logical units of each other — summing their `strength`, and if either was `'sieging'` the merged force is too (snapped to the target's own position). A marching reinforcement that catches up to an already-sieging army merges into it the same way; a reinforcement that arrives at the target itself no longer overwrites that siege's own `progress` — `advanceArmies`' arrival branch only creates a fresh `goblinwar_sieges` entry when one doesn't already exist there. Capturing a settlement clears every army still in flight toward it for that kingdom, not just the one that happened to trigger the capture roll.
- **Movement — daily, not weekly.** `advanceArmies()` runs from `advanceTime`'s non-quiet day-crossing branch and from `restThroughNight()` (the same two hook points `chargeMercenaryUpkeep()`/`maybeRunFactionAI()` already use), so armies visibly progress between weekly ticks rather than teleporting once a week. Each marching army gains `ARMY_MILES_PER_DAY` (200, a first-pass balance number, same "easy to retune later" convention as `RAID_AMBUSH_CHANCE` — see [combat.md](combat.md)) toward `routeMiles` per elapsed day — handling more than one elapsed day in a single call the same lump-sum way `chargeMercenaryUpkeep` already does (`lastAdvancedDay`, not an assumption this only ever fires once per single day), and its on-screen position is a proportional walk along `routePts` (`pointAlongPolyline`) rather than a frame-animated marker like the player's own travel — snapped once per elapsed day is enough for a background army nobody's actively watching move. On arrival: if the target's still held by the kingdom being besieged, the army flips to `'sieging'`; if the target changed hands while it was marching (a different attacker got there first), it simply disbands instead.
- **Map rendering.** `buildArmyMarkers()` (a new `#army-markers` SVG group, between `#frontline-markers` and `#quest-markers`, `pointer-events:none` — armies aren't tappable) draws a small colored wedge per army, blue for Good-alliance, red for Bad (`ALLIANCE[army.race]`), at its current `x,y`, sized (mildly, clamped) by its own `strength` — a merged, reinforced force actually reads as a bigger army on the map. Placeholder shape until real art exists; generation prompts for a proper map icon are already drafted in the Scene Backdrops tracker artifact (see roadmap.md). Rebuilt on load and every time `advanceArmies()`/the weekly tick actually changes or merges anything, same call pattern `buildFrontlineMarkers()` already follows.
- **Performance.** `advanceArmies()` (and the `mergeArmies()` it calls) run *outside* `runFactionAITick`'s own tick-cache window (see "Performance: a tick-scoped read cache" below) — neither is the O(n²) per-settlement scan that cache exists for, since the number of armies at once is small (bounded by `MAX_ARMIES_PER_ENEMY_PAIR` per pair). `getArmies()` still checks `_tickCache` when one's active (a new `armies` entry alongside `sieges`/`territory`/etc in the cache object), since the weekly tick's own pass 2 now reads it once per kingdom-per-enemy to decide whether to roll `advanceSiege`/reinforce/muster.

**Known simplifications, same spirit as the section below:**
- An army always musters from the attacker's capital, never a closer border garrison — simpler than a real "which of my settlements is closest to the front" search, consistent with "muster from the seat of power."
- Reinforcement/merging is same-kingdom only — two different (even allied) kingdoms' armies never combine, so "the whole Good alliance masses on one city" isn't something this supports yet. Keeps siege capture unambiguous (`setController` always has one clear attacking kingdom to credit).
- Position updates once per elapsed day, not continuously animated — a background NPC army doesn't get its own persistent requestAnimationFrame loop the way the player's own travel marker does.
- The polyline-fraction position (`pointAlongPolyline`) approximates "how far along the route," since a logical-space point list's on-screen length isn't exactly proportional to the route's real (`DISTANCE_SCALE`-compressed) miles — close enough for a once-a-day snap, not meant to be exact.
- The muster population cost (`ARMY_MUSTER_FRACTION_MIN`-`MAX` of the origin's *current* population, randomized per muster — see above) has no cooldown or diminishing-returns curve — a capital that keeps getting mustered from keeps paying that same proportional-range cost each time, which can compound into a steep decline over a long, reinforcement-heavy war. Not yet tuned against real playtesting.

## Field battles: an Army Camp, and a real fight the player can join

Per the project owner: two armies meeting shouldn't just be a number quietly resolving in the
background — it should be a real place the player can go, with a camp to talk and resupply at, a
choice of how to help, and a genuine sense of "how long can I keep this up." This is the second,
richer half of "Armies meet and fight in the field" (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)) — Phase 1 above
(visible marching armies) was the prerequisite; this is what happens when two of them collide.

`goblinwar_fieldBattles`: a JSON array, `{id, x, y, goodKingdom, goodArmyId, goodRace, badKingdom, badArmyId, badRace, startedDay, advantage}`. `advantage` is a single `-100` (Bad fully routs Good) to `100` (Good fully routs Bad) scalar — a tug-of-war, not two separate meters, since only the Good side ever gets a player-facing "help" hook (every other Player Involvement action in this file is one-directional the same way).

- **Detection.** `detectFieldBattles()` runs at the end of every `advanceArmies()` pass (daily, same as the movement/merge steps above it) and checks every pair of still-`'marching'` armies from opposite alliances; within `BATTLE_ENCOUNTER_RADIUS` logical units, it spawns a battle at their midpoint and freezes both (`status:'battling'`, position pinned there). A `matched` set stops a third nearby army from double-booking one that was just claimed in the same pass. Only marching-vs-marching armies clash — an army already `'sieging'` a settlement isn't interrupted by a passing enemy force (a real simplification, below).
- **Background resolution.** A new pass in `runFactionAITick()`, right after the existing siege pass, nudges every active battle's `advantage` toward whichever side has the higher **army strength** — each side's own `.strength` field from Phase 1 (not `getArmyStrength(kingdom)`'s kingdom-wide pool, same "this specific force's own size decides it" precedent `advanceSiege` already set for sieges), same clamped-ratio shape (`RAID_SUCCESS_MIN`/`MAX`) times `FIELD_BATTLE_ADVANTAGE_MAX_PER_TICK` (30) per tick. At `|advantage| >= 100` the losing army is removed from `goblinwar_armies` (routed — the toast/rumour wording says "routed," not "destroyed," a softer framing) and the battle entry is deleted; the winning army's `status` reverts to `'marching'` and it simply resumes interpolating along its own already-stored route from wherever it paused. No territory changes hands from a battle directly — only a siege reaching its own target does that; this is a separate "wear down the enemy's mustered army" system. Simulated against real strength ratios: a lopsided matchup (5:2) resolves in roughly 8 weeks; an even one can take a very long time via the background tick alone — which is exactly when the player's own help (below) actually matters.
- **Reaching it costs a real trip.** Tapping a battle marker opens the same `#info-card` used for settlements (`onBattleMarkerTap`, the battle-marker counterpart to `onMapTap`) rather than the Camp itself — it shows a quote for the real road/sea distance to the battle's `{x,y}` and a "Travel Here" button, exactly like tapping a settlement does. Since a battle's location is an arbitrary merged midpoint, not a settlement with its own cell id, `findNearestCell(x,y)` first finds the closest real road-graph node (a linear scan over every `graph.edges` endpoint — every edge is already a minimal 2-point segment between two adjacent cells, so this is a complete search, not an approximation), and `computeTravelToPoint`/`beginTravelToBattle` mirror `computeTravel`/`beginTravel` but route to that point instead of a `burgId`. On arrival, `campPos` (the same in-memory "standing at a non-settlement point" flag already used for mid-journey camping) gains a `battleId` field rather than `currentBurg` changing — that's what `onBattleMarkerTap` checks afterward to show "You are here — Enter Camp" instead of another travel quote.
- **The Army Camp.** Arriving opens `showFieldBattlePanel(battleId)`, reusing the very same `#location-view`/`#loc-banner`/`#loc-action-list` shell every settlement/camp screen already uses — no new overlay. It re-checks `goblinwar_fieldBattles[battleId]` still exists on every render (same defensive pattern `showNotableFigurePanel` uses for "this place has changed hands") since a battle can resolve in the background between visits. Five actions:
  - **Talk to [the Quartermaster / a Weary Soldier]** — two generic camp roles (`FIELD_BATTLE_CAMP_CHARACTERS`/`FIELD_BATTLE_CAMP_TREES`), not unique named individuals — same "deliberately faceless, no new art needed yet" precedent `MERCENARY_TYPES` already set. Real multiple-choice conversations via the shared dialogue engine (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Conversations are multiple choice now"), flavor-only — no `effects` on any choice, since there's no natural effect target here.
  - **Resupply** — calls `restThroughNight()`/`restFully()` verbatim, no new time-passing code. This is also the real mechanism behind "fight, withdraw, resupply, fight again": see the cooldown below.
  - **Head to the Front Line** and **Raid the Enemy Camp** — two entries in `FIELD_BATTLE_ENGAGEMENTS`, a small catalog (`{title, buildEnemies(battle), onWin(battle), introLine/victoryMessage/defeatMessage (optional flavor text)}`) deliberately shaped so more engagement types the project owner wants later are each just one more entry, not a rewrite of this screen — `frontline` doesn't define the optional flavor hooks at all and just falls back to the original generic wording, proving the shape didn't need touching to add `raid`. Both trigger a real fight via the same generic `startCombat` every fight uses, and share one daily gate — **once per battle per `gameDay`** (`goblinwar_fieldBattleCooldowns`, same shape as `goblinwar_siegeDefenseCooldowns`, checked once for both buttons rather than per-engagement) — per the project owner, this (plus Stamina, already spent by ordinary Attacks and only restored by resting) is deliberately how "how long can the player keep fighting" is answered, with zero new resource system: the player picks *one* way to help this battle per day, not one of each, only re-engaging after a real Resupply or by finding a different battle. They differ in enemy count and payoff:
    - **Head to the Front Line** — a 2-3 enemy wave (`ENEMY_VARIANTS[badRace]`, same sizing Defend the Walls already uses). On victory, `FIELD_BATTLE_ADVANTAGE_RELIEF` (35, bigger than Defend the Walls' 30-point siege relief) pushes the shared `advantage` toward Good.
    - **Raid the Enemy Camp** — a leaner 1-2 enemy wave (camp guards caught off guard, not a full line). On victory, per the project owner's own confirmed design, it doesn't touch `advantage` at all — it reduces the **enemy army's own `.strength`** directly (`FIELD_BATTLE_RAID_DAMAGE_MIN`-`MAX`, a randomized 15-30% cut, floored at 1 so it never hits zero/negative), a real strategic difference from the front line: front-line fighting wins *this* tug-of-war faster, raiding weakens that army for every future clash it's in, including the unattended weekly tick's own strength-ratio math (see "Background resolution" above) — so a raided army arrives at its next fight, siege or otherwise, already worse off.
    - Both pay the same gold/XP (`FIELD_BATTLE_GOLD_RANGE`/`FIELD_BATTLE_XP_RANGE`) and reputation gain on victory — still not a guaranteed win by itself, same "player can grow strong but never solo-topple a city" constraint. Defeat/flee bank nothing but cost nothing extra either. Either way, control always returns to the Camp screen, not the map — the "both sides withdrew to regroup" beat the project owner asked for.
  - **Retreat** — closes back to the map, same as Camp view's own Back/Choose-a-Different-Destination pattern. No penalty; the battle keeps resolving in the background regardless, and the player can return any time it's still active.
- **Where this shows up.** `buildBattleMarkers()` draws a small crossed-swords mark per active battle in a new `#battle-markers` SVG group, plus an invisible `class="hitzone battle-hitzone" data-battle="id"` circle underneath it — the one genuinely tappable non-settlement marker on the map, using the exact same `.hitzone` tap-detection `handleTap` already runs for settlements (checked for `dataset.battle` first, so it doesn't fall through to the settlement branch). Rebuilt on load and whenever `detectFieldBattles()`/the weekly tick changes anything, same pattern `buildArmyMarkers`/`buildFrontlineMarkers` already follow.

**Known simplifications, same spirit as the section above:**
- **Setting up camp mid-journey to a battle drops the "continue" thread.** `campMidTravel()` opens `showCampView(activeTravel.destId, ...)`, and a battle-bound trip's `destId` is `null` (there's no `burgId` to hand off) — so it renders as a plain standalone camp (no "Continue to Army Camp" button) rather than one that can resume the trip. The player can still tap the battle marker again afterward and re-travel from wherever they camped; they just don't get the one-tap resume settlement travel offers.
- A moving battle can outrun the destination the player already committed to: `beginTravelToBattle` snapshots the battle's `{x,y}` at the moment of departure, but the battle keeps resolving in the background during the trip and can rout/resolve entirely before arrival — `onBattleMarkerTap`'s "Enter Camp" branch and `showFieldBattlePanel` both re-check `goblinwar_fieldBattles[battleId]` still exists and handle it being gone, same as tapping a marker for a battle that's already over.
- A sieging army can't be pulled into a field battle by a passing enemy force yet — see "Detection" above.
- Two engagement types ship now (front line, raid). The catalog shape exists specifically so whatever else the project owner comes up with next is additive too, not a rewrite.
- One-directional, same as every other Player Involvement hook: the Bad alliance gets no equivalent "join to help" option.

## Population, armies & refugees

Population is the resource everything else in this file ultimately spends. `travel-graph.json` already carries a `population` field per burg — Azgaar's convention, in *thousands of people* (Bary is `12.93`, i.e. ~12,930). That field is the permanent starting figure, exactly like `state`/`race` — never mutated. Current population is `goblinwar_population`, the same sparse-override pattern as territory control: `getPopulation(burgId)` checks the override first, falls back to `graph.burgs[burgId].population`. `setPopulation(burgId, pop)` rounds to the nearest *real person* (`Math.round(pop*1000)/1000`), not the nearest whole thousands-unit — rounding any coarser would zero out a village's population (some start under `1.0`, i.e. under 1,000 people) after one bad week. `formatPopulation(pop)` is the only place that multiplies by 1,000 for display, so every UI surface shows a normal-looking integer (`"12,930"`) rather than the raw `12.93`.

- **War attrition**: every tick, every settlement whose *original* kingdom (`b.state`, not current controller) has any active war relation loses `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` (2%) of its current population via `applyWarLoss`, regardless of whether that specific settlement is the one under siege.
- **Sieges cost more, every tick they run**: `advanceSiege` additionally calls `applyWarLoss(burgId, RAID_DAMAGE_RATE)` (8%) on the besieged settlement every tick the siege continues, not only when it starts.
- **Death vs. refugees**: `applyWarLoss(burgId, rate)` splits the lost population — `REFUGEE_DEATH_SHARE` (40%) is gone for good, `REFUGEE_FLEE_SHARE` (60%) is added to `findNearestPeacefulSettlement(burgId)`'s population instead of vanishing, and that haven settlement is also stamped in `goblinwar_refugeeArrivals` (`{burgId: gameDay}`, via `stampRefugeeArrival`) — see "Aid the Refugees" below. `findNearestPeacefulSettlement` does a full scan of every burg for the closest one whose *current* kingdom (`isAtWarAt`) is at peace — refugees always run toward safety, not just toward any neighbor. A settlement can be both losing population to its own kingdom's war *and* gaining refugee population from some other war-torn kingdom's attrition in the same tick; nothing coordinates the two, they're just independent `applyWarLoss` calls landing on the same burg.
- **Armies are just population, scoped to one kingdom.** `getArmyStrength(kingdom)` sums `getPopulation(id)` across every settlement *that specific kingdom* currently controls (via `getControllerKingdom`, so a captured settlement's population counts toward its *new* controller's strength the very next tick), multiplies by `MILITARY_FRACTION` (8%), and adds any active reinforcement bonus (see "Alliance reinforcement" below) — a rough "how many could be mobilized" figure, not a literal standing army headcount. Deliberately regional otherwise: a kingdom's own strength comes only from its own territory, not from every kingdom that happens to share its race, and not automatically from allied races either. This creates a real feedback loop: winning a siege grows a kingdom's controlled population (and therefore its own army, not its race's), which makes its next siege more likely to succeed too — a snowball, by design, not a bug, but one that plays out kingdom-by-kingdom rather than race-wide.
- **Killed enemies count too, not just the weekly tick.** Per the project owner, the player's own combat victories now feed the same population pool: `applyEnemyCasualtyLoss(race, attackerKingdom, nearBurgId, count)` (index.html, called from `endCombat`'s victory branch with `combat.race`/`combat.attackerKingdom`/`currentBurg`/`enemies.length`) subtracts `ENEMY_CASUALTY_POP_LOSS` (0.01 — about 10 people) times the number of enemies actually killed. Where it lands depends on what's known about the fight: a **Defend the Walls** victory (see "Player involvement" below) knows the specific `attackerKingdom` from its siege, so the loss hits the nearest settlement *that kingdom* still controls — the same kingdom whose `getArmyStrength` it's chipping away at, so repeatedly defending against one kingdom's sieges measurably weakens its future ones. A plain **ambush** victory has no specific kingdom behind it (`pickAmbushEnemy` just picks a race), so `findNearestSettlementOfRace(nearBurgId, race)` — a `findNearestPeacefulSettlement`-style scan, filtered by `getController(b.id) === race` instead of peace status — picks the nearest settlement of that race to weaken instead. Symmetric by design (Ork/Goblin kills reduce Ork/Goblin population, Human/Dwarf kills would reduce Human/Dwarf population the same way), though in practice only Ork/Goblin enemies are ever actually fought today, so the Good-alliance direction is currently latent rather than reachable — see "Known simplifications" below. Bandit kills are excluded outright; Bandits aren't tied to any kingdom or race population at all.
- **Population regrowth — the flip side of attrition.** Until this shipped, population only ever went down (attrition, siege damage, casualty loss, Raid a Village) or sideways (refugees relocating, zero-sum). `applyPopulationGrowth()` (index.html, called every tick from `runFactionAITick`'s third pass — see "The weekly tick" above) now grows every settlement that's currently at peace (`getWarState(getControllerKingdom(id)) !== 'war'` — checked against the *current* controller, not original identity, same "control, not identity" convention `getArmyStrength` already uses) by `POP_GROWTH_RATE_GOOD` (1%/week, Human/Dwarf) or `POP_GROWTH_RATE_BAD` (2.5%/week, Ork/Goblin) depending on `getController(id)`'s race. This is the first system to actually use the "Ork/Goblin multiply faster" race trait, tracked as an idea since character-creation planning began (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)) but never wired into anything until now. A settlement currently at war gets no regrowth that tick — attrition/siege losses aren't quietly offset in the same week they land; regrowth only resumes once that kingdom is at peace again. Paired with a corresponding combat nerf (see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Enemy variants and their art") — individually weaker Ork/Goblin rank-and-file, in trade for their kingdoms coming back from losses faster.

This is the one place war state changes something other than a number or a label; it changes which enemy you're actually fighting. **Defend the Walls** (below) is the second.

## War-driven Marketplace pricing

Four constants near `isAtWarAt`: `WAR_BUY_MULTIPLIER` (1.5), `WAR_SELL_BONUS_MULT` (1.2), `PEACE_SELL_FRACTION` (0.5), and `isWarRelevant(mi)` (true for anything with an equip `slot`, plus Firewood by name — see [player-state.md](player-state.md) for what's equippable).

- `getBuyPrice(mi, burgId)` = `mi.price * (1.5 if that settlement's kingdom is at war, else 1)`, rounded.
- `getSellPrice(mi, burgId)` = for war-relevant items *at a settlement whose kingdom is at war*, `mi.price * 1.2` (a premium — wartime demand for gear and fuel); otherwise `mi.price * 0.5` (the normal sell-back cut), war or not. A region at lasting peace never pays the wartime premium for anything, and non-war-relevant goods (Food, Healing Potion, Books) never do either, even mid-war.

`showMarketPanel` calls both on every render — the war/peace banner at the top of the panel (`"{kingdom} — At war — prices up"` / `"At peace"`) reads `isAtWarAt(burgId)` directly, and every item row's Buy slider/button and Sell button price off `getBuyPrice`/`getSellPrice` rather than the flat `mi.price`. `showBlacksmithPanel` shares the exact same functions, so both shops picked up war-driven pricing (and everything below) with no extra wiring.

`getBuyPrice` also folds in `getTributeMultiplier(burgId)` — see "War exhaustion & reparations" below — the one place a war's *outcome*, not just its live state, affects prices: a kingdom currently paying reparations charges more, one currently receiving them charges less, on top of everything above. `buildTributeNote(burgId)`, shared by both shops' banners, surfaces which (if either) applies.

Waterskin isn't in `MARKET_ITEMS`, so it's never sellable — same as it was never buyable. Bedroll *is* now a `MARKET_ITEMS` entry (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section for why it became an expensive purchase), so it prices and sells like any other item here — it just isn't `isWarRelevant`, so it never gets the wartime sell premium.

## Alliance reinforcement — the one exception to "every kingdom stands alone"

Every kingdom's `getArmyStrength` is otherwise strictly its own territory (above) — the deliberate exception is `goblinwar_reinforcements` (`{kingdomName: {amount, expiresDay, fromKingdom}}`, sparse), a temporary strength bonus one kingdom can grant another. Originally player-triggered only; the weekly tick's own autonomous pass (see "The weekly tick" above and "Per-kingdom reputation" below) can now also write this key when a `Trusted` kingdom reinforces an ally unprompted — same `setReinforcement` call either way. `getReinforcementSource(kingdom)` (index.html) is a sibling accessor to `getReinforcementBonus` for the one field neither the Palace card nor Guard House currently show — which kingdom actually sent the troops — added for the reactive Talk to Townsfolk line that names it (see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)/[roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Reactive rumors").

- **Trigger:** the Palace action (City/Capital's `showFlavorPanel` extra content, alongside Guard House's stats — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) shows a "Petition for Troops" offer whenever that Capital's *own original* kingdom (`b.state`, and only if it still controls its own capital — `getController(burgId) === b.race`, since a captured capital has no court of its own left to petition) is currently at peace **and** at least one kingdom sharing its `ALLIANCE` is at war. `findAllyNeedingReinforcement(kingdom)` picks whichever qualifying ally currently has the *least* reinforcement (via `getReinforcementBonus`), so repeat petitions across different Capitals or different days spread help around rather than always favoring one ally. If no ally needs help, or the home kingdom is itself at war, there's simply no offer that day — same "sometimes nothing to do here" pattern as the Quest Board when nothing new is postable.
- **Reward, and resolution:** `generatePetitionOffer(burgId)` seeds `20-40` gold / `15-25` XP from the kingdom + `gameDay`, same seeded-PRNG pattern as Quest Board/Notable Figure offers (see [quests.md](quests.md)). Unlike those, accepting (`acceptPetition`) resolves **instantly** — no travel or turn-in step, since the trip to this specific Capital already *is* the "go there" part the idea called for, and convincing a court is one conversation, not a multi-stage task.
- **The effect:** `setReinforcement(ally, getArmyStrength(homeKingdom) * REINFORCEMENT_FRACTION, homeKingdom)` grants the threatened ally a bonus equal to `25%` of the *petitioned* kingdom's own current strength, lasting `REINFORCEMENT_DURATION_DAYS` (14) in-game days from the moment of the petition (`gameDay + 14`, checked lazily by `getReinforcementBonus` against the live `gameDay` — no active expiry/cleanup needed, same pattern as Work's per-day cooldown). A new petition for the same ally **overwrites** rather than stacks, so this can't be farmed into an ever-growing bonus by repeatedly visiting the same or different peaceful Capitals.
- **Known simplification:** the reinforcement is one-directional — the petitioned (sending) kingdom's own strength isn't reduced for having "sent troops." Since they're at peace when petitioned, this has no immediate effect either way, but it means there's no real cost to petitioning beyond the trip there.

## Player involvement

Four direct hooks into a war's outcome, on top of the always-indirect Alliance Reinforcement above — the payoff of the relations matrix now naming *specific* enemies rather than "the war" in the abstract. The first three are positive; Incite War is the deliberate exception — see below.

- **Negotiate Peace** (`generateNegotiatePeaceOffer`/`buildNegotiatePeaceHTML`/`acceptNegotiatePeace`) is a second Palace card, alongside Petition for Troops, shown whenever the Capital's own kingdom (same eligibility check as Petition) has at least one active war relation (`getEnemiesAtWar`). It picks the *weakest* current enemy (by `getArmyStrength`, same "start with the easiest win" logic Petition's ally-pick uses) and prices a bribe (`NEGOTIATE_PEACE_BASE_COST`, 150 gold, scaled `0.4`–`1.6`× by how lopsided the strength ratio is — cheaper against a weak enemy, pricier against a strong one). On success, `setRelation(kingdom, enemy, 'peace')` and `cancelSiegesBetween` end any active siege between exactly those two kingdoms outright — the defender keeps whatever it still held. It also nudges companion loyalty up (`adjustPartyLoyalty(GOOD_ACTION_LOYALTY_GAIN)` — see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Companion loyalty").
- **Incite War** (`generateInciteWarOffer`/`buildInciteWarHTML`/`acceptInciteWar`) is a third Palace card, and the deliberate opposite of Negotiate Peace: instead of bribing envoys to end a war, it pays the player to provoke one. Same eligibility gate as Negotiate Peace (a legitimate court still sitting), but the candidate pool is every opposite-alliance kingdom currently at *peace* with the home kingdom (`ALL_KINGDOMS.filter(...)` against `ALLIANCE`/`KINGDOM_RACE`/`getRelation`), and it picks the *strongest* qualifying target rather than the weakest — provoking a bigger fight is worth more to whoever's paying for it. The same ratio math Negotiate Peace uses to price a truce prices the payout here, just inverted: a stronger target pays better rather than costing more (`INCITE_WAR_BASE_GOLD`, 120 gold, scaled `0.4`–`1.6`×). On success, `setRelation(kingdom, target, 'war')` and `buildFrontlineMarkers()` — no siege exists yet between them, so nothing needs cancelling the way Negotiate Peace's success does. Applies the single biggest companion-loyalty penalty in the game (`INCITE_WAR_LOYALTY_PENALTY`) — deliberately starting a war for coin is the sharpest "helping the Bad alliance" act the player can take. All three Palace cards' buttons are wired together by `wirePalaceButtons(burgId)`, since a render can now show any combination of them.
- **Defend the Walls** (`triggerSiegeDefenseFight`/`resolveSiegeDefense`) appears at any settlement (any tier — sieges aren't restricted to City/Capital) currently in `goblinwar_sieges`. It builds a 2-3 enemy fight from the attacking kingdom's own race (`ENEMY_VARIANTS[KINGDOM_RACE[attackerKingdom]]`) and starts it through the same generic `startCombat` ambushes use (see [combat.md](combat.md)) — `startCombat`'s own ambush-flavored title/log strings are overwritten right after the call with siege-appropriate text. Its `onEnd` callback is `resolveSiegeDefense`: a **victory** knocks `SIEGE_DEFENSE_PROGRESS_RELIEF` (30 percentage points) off the siege's progress and pays a gold/XP reward — not an instant win, deliberately, per the project's own "player can grow strong but never solo-topple a city" design constraint (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)) — while a **defeat or flee** leaves the siege to advance normally on the next weekly tick. Gated to once per settlement per day (`goblinwar_siegeDefenseCooldowns`, same pattern as Work's `hasWorkedToday`), so it can meaningfully help without trivializing every siege into a single fight.
- **Aid the Refugees** (`buildRefugeeAidHTML`/`acceptRefugeeAid`) is the simplest of the four, deliberately: shown at any settlement with a recent arrival (`hasRecentRefugeeArrival`, within `REFUGEE_ARRIVAL_WINDOW_DAYS` = 7 days of `stampRefugeeArrival` — see "Population, armies & refugees" above). Spending `REFUGEE_AID_GOLD_COST` (15) gold and `REFUGEE_AID_FOOD_COST` (2) Food adds a small population boost (`REFUGEE_AID_POP_BOOST`, 0.05 — about 50 people) to that settlement and pays modest XP, resolving instantly like Petition for Troops rather than as a travel/turn-in quest. Also nudges companion loyalty up, same as Negotiate Peace.

**Raid a Village** (`buildRaidVillageHTML`/`acceptRaidVillage`) is a related but separate action — not a Palace card, and not conditional on war state at all (see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)). It's the direct opposite of Aid the Refugees: available at any settlement, any tier, once per day, it pays gold scaled by tier at the cost of a real population hit to that settlement (`setPopulation`, a flat ~3%) and a companion-loyalty hit smaller than Incite War's but bigger than a world event's.

## Per-kingdom reputation

A second, independent standing number from companion loyalty (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Companion loyalty") — `goblinwar_reputation`, `{kingdomName: -100 to 100}`, sparse (an absent kingdom reads as 0/neutral, same sparse-override shape as `goblinwar_relations`/`goblinwar_territoryControl` above). `getReputation(kingdom)`/`adjustReputation(kingdom, delta)` (clamped, a no-op if `kingdom` is falsy) are the raw accessors; `reputationLabel(val)` buckets it into Hostile (< -50) / Unfriendly (-50 to -11) / Neutral (-10 to 10) / Friendly (11 to 50) / Trusted (> 50) for display and for gating the two payoffs below.

Every action above that already moves companion loyalty also moves reputation, but credits the *specific* kingdom that action actually concerns rather than every recruited companion at once: a delivery quest turn-in and Aid the Refugees credit the settlement's own current controller (`getControllerKingdom`); a Notable Figure kill-quest turn-in credits that figure's home kingdom; Petition for Troops credits the ally kingdom actually helped; Negotiate Peace and a Defend the Walls victory credit the home/defending kingdom; Raid a Village and Incite War penalize the settlement/court the action was taken against (Incite War's `REP_LOSS_SEVERE` is the single biggest reputation swing in the game, same as it is for loyalty).

Two payoffs are live so far — both deliberately contained to "one more multiplicative factor" or "one more concatenated line pool," reusing mechanisms that already existed rather than inventing new ones:
- **Information**: `getTownsfolkLines(burgId)` (see "Talk to Townsfolk" in [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) concats a new `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_TRUSTED` pool on top whenever that settlement's controller sits at Friendly or Trusted — the same "concat on top" mechanism `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_CAPTURED` already used.
- **Equipment**: `getReputationDiscount(burgId)` folds into `getBuyPrice` (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)) alongside `getSettlementDiscount`/the `silver_tongue` perk — Trusted 0.85×, Friendly 0.93×, Neutral 1×, Unfriendly 1.07×, Hostile 1.15×. `showBlacksmithPanel` already prices every row through the same `getBuyPrice`, so the discount applies to both the Marketplace and the Blacksmith with no extra wiring.
- Guard House's stats card (`buildGuardHouseStats`) shows a plain "{kingdom}'s opinion of you" row alongside its existing population/army numbers — the only place a kingdom's own reputation is directly surfaced to the player today.

**Troops and War Help are done too now — both scoped to reuse existing mechanics rather than invent new combat/AI surface**, so the "player can grow strong but never solo-topple a city" constraint (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)) stays intact — neither touches the player's own Defend the Walls fight at all:
- **Troops.** `runFactionAITick()`'s third pass (after the war/peace-roll pass and the per-kingdom attrition/siege pass — see "The weekly tick" below) checks every kingdom that's currently at peace (`getWarState`): if the player is `Trusted` with it, it rolls `TRUSTED_REINFORCEMENT_CHANCE` (35%, not guaranteed every week) to reinforce its own most-threatened ally unprompted — the exact same `findAllyNeedingReinforcement`/`setReinforcement`/`getArmyStrength` call Petition for Troops already makes, just tick-triggered instead of player-triggered. `setReinforcement` already overwrites rather than stacks, so this can't combine with a player Petition into a bigger bonus. Deliberately silent — no toast, consistent with the tick's own "captures only" convention below — a nearly-full `getReinforcementBonus` on a kingdom the player would otherwise Petition is the only in-game signal it happened.
- **War Help.** `advanceSiege(attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom)` multiplies `getArmyStrength(defenderKingdom)` by `TRUSTED_DEFENSE_BONUS` (1.15×) before computing the attacker/defender ratio, whenever the player is `Trusted` with the defending kingdom — a Trusted kingdom's own garrison holds a little tougher against every siege it faces, entirely in the background math; `startCombat`/`triggerSiegeDefenseFight` are completely untouched.

## Persistence

`goblinwar_territoryControl`, `goblinwar_relations`, `goblinwar_reinforcements`, `goblinwar_lastWarTick`, `goblinwar_population`, `goblinwar_sieges`, `goblinwar_refugeeArrivals`, `goblinwar_siegeDefenseCooldowns`, `goblinwar_reputation`, `goblinwar_recentEvents`, `goblinwar_warStarts`, `goblinwar_warCooldowns`, `goblinwar_tribute`, `goblinwar_armies`, `goblinwar_fieldBattles`, and `goblinwar_fieldBattleCooldowns` are part of every character's save (see [characters.md](characters.md)) — each character effectively runs its own independent copy of the world's faction/war state, saved and loaded alongside everything else about that character. This matters more than it might sound: since a Supabase account can hold more than one character, and localStorage is a single flat namespace shared by whichever character is currently active on this device, these keys have to travel with the character snapshot — otherwise switching characters on the same device would let one character's war/territory progress bleed into another's.

## Known simplifications (not bugs, just where the scope was deliberately cut)

- Sieges still resolve as one settlement at a time with no garrison-vs-attacker battle simulation beyond the clamped strength-ratio roll — but `getGarrisonStrength` is a real mechanical input to that roll now, not just a number shown to the player (see "An army's own strength, not the whole kingdom's" above).
- Refugees relocate in a single jump to the single nearest peaceful settlement, every time — no capacity limit on how much a haven can absorb, no chance of refugees choosing badly or splitting across multiple destinations, and no path/road-distance awareness (straight-line distance only — unlike `pickRaidTarget`'s targeting, which now does use the real border graph).
- Attrition and siege damage are both flat percentages of *current* population, not modified by a settlement's tier, defenses, or how long it's been under siege — a capital and a village bleed population at the same relative rate.
- **Negotiate Peace has no lasting cost or diplomatic memory.** A truce can be bought again the moment a new war breaks out with the same or a different enemy, and there's no reputation/relationship tracking beyond the live `war`/`peace` relation itself.
- **Reinforcement is still one petition, one ally, one kingdom at a time**, and still entirely player-triggered with no AI-driven equivalent — a kingdom under siege has no way to request help on its own, only Negotiate Peace or Defend the Walls give the player a way to actually change an outcome, and there's still no partial/gradual reinforcement or cost to the sending kingdom.
- **`triggerSiegeDefenseFight` silently mislabels a Good-alliance attacker as Ork.** If a Human/Dwarf kingdom is the one besieging a settlement (sieges are directional per kingdom pair, so this can happen — see "The weekly tick" above), `ENEMY_VARIANTS[attackerRace] || ENEMY_VARIANTS.ork` falls back to Ork enemies anyway, since no `ENEMY_VARIANTS.human`/`.dwarf` entry (or matching portrait art) exists. Not fixed yet because fixing it for real needs actual Human/Dwarf soldier art, which is an asset-generation decision for the project owner, not something to invent unilaterally — flagged here rather than silently left. This is also why the killed-enemy population loss above (`applyEnemyCasualtyLoss`) is symmetric in code but effectively one-directional in practice today.
- **A war-exhaustion treaty (see "War exhaustion & reparations" above) bars both sides from re-declaring, not just the winner.** The pairwise relations model has no "who's the aggressor" concept for a given war (`advanceSiege`'s attacker/defender roles are decided fresh, per enemy, every tick — not tied to who first declared), so there's no way to let the loser attack again while still barring the winner specifically. A mutual, real peace treaty was the closest honest equivalent, not a shortcut around a harder feature.
