# Factions, Territory & War Economy (index.html)

The first "living world" system in the game — kingdoms fight over settlements in the background while the player does other things, their populations shrink, flee, or grow because of it, and the Marketplace reacts to all of it. Lives entirely in index.html, keyed off the same weekly tick.

## Two separate ideas: identity vs. control

A settlement's *historical* kingdom and race (`graph.burgs[id].state`/`.race`, from the Azgaar export — see [data-files.md](data-files.md)) never change. That's its permanent identity, same as a real place keeps its name after changing hands. Its *current controller* is a separate, sparse override — tracked by the specific **kingdom**, not just its race, so that a captured settlement's population and military weight belong to the one kingdom that actually holds it, not to "whichever kingdoms happen to share its race":

- `goblinwar_territoryControl`: `{burgId: kingdomName}` (e.g. `"Kingdom of Warg"`), only present for settlements that have actually been fought over. `getControllerKingdom(burgId)` checks this map first and falls back to `graph.burgs[burgId].state` — so an unconquered settlement's controller is always just its own original kingdom, with no explicit entry needed.
- `getController(burgId)` is the race-level convenience most of the codebase actually wants (`KINGDOM_RACE[getControllerKingdom(burgId)]`, falling back to `graph.burgs[burgId].race` for a neutral burg with no kingdom at all) — onMapTap, `showLocationView`'s subtitle, and `getFigureForBurg` only ever needed "which race holds this," so they call this rather than the kingdom-level accessor.
- `setController(burgId, kingdom)` writes a kingdom-name override. Nothing ever deletes one (a settlement can change hands multiple times, but there's no "restore original owner" action).
- **Backward-compat:** saves from before kingdom-level tracking stored a bare race string here instead (e.g. `"ork"`). `getController` still resolves those correctly (a stored value that's itself a real `ALLIANCE` key is treated as already being a race). `getArmyStrength` (below) can't recover which specific kingdom made an old-format capture, though — that settlement's population simply stops counting toward any kingdom's strength until it's captured again, which writes the new format. Self-healing, not silently wrong.

This deliberately avoids renaming anything — `graph.burgs[id].state`/`.name` stay put forever. Wherever the game already shows a settlement's race/kingdom, it should show the *current controller*, not necessarily the original — see "Where this shows up" below.

## War state belongs to kingdoms, not settlements

`goblinwar_warState`: `{kingdomName: "war"|"peace"}`, default `"peace"` for any kingdom not in the map. War/peace is a property of the *kingdom as a whole* — a kingdom that's currently fighting has every one of its settlements' Marketplaces charging wartime prices, regardless of which specific settlements have actually been captured. `getWarState(kingdom)` / `setWarState(kingdom, state)` are the accessors; `isAtWarAt(burgId)` is the one most call sites actually want (looks up `graph.burgs[burgId].state`, then that kingdom's war state).

## Faction data

`KINGDOM_RACE` (kingdom name → race) and `ALL_KINGDOMS` (the ~24 kingdom names) are built once by `buildFactionData()`, called right after `graph` loads (alongside `buildAdjacency()`). Derived by scanning every burg for its `state`/`race` pair — cheap, done once, not persisted (rebuilt fresh every page load from `travel-graph.json`, which is why it's safe to never invalidate).

`ALLIANCE` (`{human:"Good", dwarf:"Good", ork:"Bad", goblin:"Bad"}`, near `RACE_LABEL`) is what "enemy" means for war/raid purposes — any kingdom whose race is on the other side.

## The weekly tick

`maybeRunFactionAI()` runs at the end of every `advanceDays(n)` call — so it fires after travel arrival, Inn/camp rest, or a partial-travel camp, whichever advances the calendar. It compares `gameDay` against `goblinwar_lastWarTick` and does nothing unless at least `WAR_TICK_INTERVAL_DAYS` (7) have passed, then immediately stamps `lastWarTick` to the current `gameDay` before running `runFactionAITick()` — so a long journey that jumps the calendar by more than a week still only ticks once, not once per day skipped.

`runFactionAITick()` loops every kingdom in `ALL_KINGDOMS` and rolls independently:
- **At peace**: `WAR_DECLARE_CHANCE` (6%) chance to flip to `"war"`.
- **At war**: `RAID_CHANCE` (35%) chance to call `attemptRaid(kingdom)`, and separately (not mutually exclusive with a raid in the same tick) `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE` (15%) chance to flip back to `"peace"`.

`attemptRaid(attackerKingdom)` doesn't pick a target uniformly at random — it computes the centroid of every settlement *that one attacking kingdom* currently controls (`getControllerKingdom(b.id) === attackerKingdom` — not every kingdom sharing its race), finds every enemy-alliance-controlled settlement (race-level check, via `getController`, since alliance is a race concept), sorts by distance to that centroid, and picks randomly among the nearest `RAID_CANDIDATE_POOL` (15). This is a proximity *bias*, not a real adjacency graph (the game doesn't have kingdom border data — see "War frontline tracker / territory border detection" in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)), but it's enough to stop a kingdom from raiding all the way across the map. Success is no longer a flat coin flip — see "Population, armies & refugees" below for what actually decides it. Either way, `applyWarLoss(target.id, RAID_DAMAGE_RATE)` costs the target population before the outcome is even decided — a raid hurts whether or not it succeeds — and a success flips the target's controller to the attacking *kingdom* via `setController`.

If any raid actually succeeded during the tick, `maybeRunFactionAI()` shows one toast summarizing it (e.g. `"Grimstan has fallen to Human forces from Kingdom of Bary."`, or a count if more than one) — declarations of war and peace deals don't get a toast, since with 24 kingdoms rolling independently every week that would be constant noise; captures are the events worth the player's attention.

## Population, armies & refugees

Population is the resource everything else in this file ultimately spends. `travel-graph.json` already carries a `population` field per burg — Azgaar's convention, in *thousands of people* (Bary is `12.93`, i.e. ~12,930). That field is the permanent starting figure, exactly like `state`/`race` — never mutated. Current population is `goblinwar_population`, the same sparse-override pattern as territory control: `getPopulation(burgId)` checks the override first, falls back to `graph.burgs[burgId].population`. `setPopulation(burgId, pop)` rounds to the nearest *real person* (`Math.round(pop*1000)/1000`), not the nearest whole thousands-unit — rounding any coarser would zero out a village's population (some start under `1.0`, i.e. under 1,000 people) after one bad week. `formatPopulation(pop)` is the only place that multiplies by 1,000 for display, so every UI surface shows a normal-looking integer (`"12,930"`) rather than the raw `12.93`.

- **War attrition**: every tick, every settlement whose *original* kingdom (`b.state`, not current controller) is at war loses `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` (2%) of its current population via `applyWarLoss`, regardless of whether that specific settlement was the one raided.
- **Raids cost more**: `attemptRaid` additionally calls `applyWarLoss(target.id, RAID_DAMAGE_RATE)` (8%) on whichever settlement it targets, win or lose — the immediate violence of being raided, on top of the kingdom's general wartime attrition.
- **Death vs. refugees**: `applyWarLoss(burgId, rate)` splits the lost population — `REFUGEE_DEATH_SHARE` (40%) is gone for good, `REFUGEE_FLEE_SHARE` (60%) is added to `findNearestPeacefulSettlement(burgId)`'s population instead of vanishing. That helper does a full scan of every burg for the closest one whose *current* kingdom (`isAtWarAt`) is at peace — refugees always run toward safety, not just toward any neighbor. A settlement can be both losing population to its own kingdom's war *and* gaining refugee population from some other war-torn kingdom's attrition in the same tick; nothing coordinates the two, they're just independent `applyWarLoss` calls landing on the same burg.
- **Armies are just population, scoped to one kingdom.** `getArmyStrength(kingdom)` sums `getPopulation(id)` across every settlement *that specific kingdom* currently controls (via `getControllerKingdom`, so a captured settlement's population counts toward its *new* controller's strength the very next tick), multiplies by `MILITARY_FRACTION` (8%), and adds any active reinforcement bonus (see "Alliance reinforcement" below) — a rough "how many could be mobilized" figure, not a literal standing army headcount. Deliberately regional otherwise: a kingdom's own strength comes only from its own territory, not from every kingdom that happens to share its race, and not automatically from allied races either. `attemptRaid` computes `attackerStrength / (attackerStrength + defenderStrength)` and clamps it to `[RAID_SUCCESS_MIN, RAID_SUCCESS_MAX]` (`0.2`–`0.8`) — so population advantage matters a lot, but no kingdom is ever a mathematically guaranteed win or loss no matter how lopsided the numbers get. This creates a real feedback loop: winning raids grows a kingdom's controlled population (and therefore its own army, not its race's), which makes its next raid more likely to succeed too — a snowball, by design, not a bug, but one that plays out kingdom-by-kingdom rather than race-wide.

## Where this shows up

- **`onMapTap`** (the tap-a-settlement info card): the race tag and its color now come from `getController(burgId)`, not `burg.race`, and a `(captured)` note (in the danger-red used elsewhere for warnings) appears whenever the current controller differs from the original race. The kingdom name shown next to it is still the original `burg.state` — identity, not control. A second line below shows that settlement's current population via `formatPopulation(getPopulation(burgId))`.
- **`showLocationView`**'s subtitle: same idea, `"{tier} · {Race}-controlled"` appended only when the controller differs from the original.
- **Guard House** (`buildGuardHouseStats`, City/Capital-only — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)): the one place a kingdom's own mustered strength is actually shown to the player (named by kingdom, e.g. "Kingdom of Warg forces" — not by race), alongside its usual random flavor line. `showFlavorPanel` grew an optional 5th `extraHTML` parameter just for this — Temple and The Palace still call it with nothing extra and stay pure flavor.
- **`showMarketPanel`**: see below — this is where war state actually has a mechanical effect, not just a label.
- **Combat**: `pickAmbushEnemy` (index.html, see [combat.md](combat.md)) reads `isAtWarAt(burgId)` directly — a Good-alliance settlement whose kingdom is at war has a chance of spawning a themed Goblin/Ork Raiders fight instead of a plain Bandit. This is the one place war state changes something other than a number or a label; it changes which enemy you're actually fighting.

## War-driven Marketplace pricing

Four constants near `isAtWarAt`: `WAR_BUY_MULTIPLIER` (1.5), `WAR_SELL_BONUS_MULT` (1.2), `PEACE_SELL_FRACTION` (0.5), and `isWarRelevant(mi)` (true for anything with an equip `slot`, plus Firewood by name — see [player-state.md](player-state.md) for what's equippable).

- `getBuyPrice(mi, burgId)` = `mi.price * (1.5 if that settlement's kingdom is at war, else 1)`, rounded.
- `getSellPrice(mi, burgId)` = for war-relevant items *at a settlement whose kingdom is at war*, `mi.price * 1.2` (a premium — wartime demand for gear and fuel); otherwise `mi.price * 0.5` (the normal sell-back cut), war or not. A region at lasting peace never pays the wartime premium for anything, and non-war-relevant goods (Food, Healing Potion, Books) never do either, even mid-war.

`showMarketPanel` calls both on every render — the war/peace banner at the top of the panel (`"{kingdom} — At war — prices up"` / `"At peace"`) reads `isAtWarAt(burgId)` directly, and every item row's Buy slider/button and (new) **Sell** button price off `getBuyPrice`/`getSellPrice` rather than the flat `mi.price`. Selling is new: any carried item that's also in `MARKET_ITEMS` gets a "Sell 1 for Ng" row once `getItem(mi.name).qty > 0` (checked fresh every `render()`, so it appears/disappears as your inventory changes) — `sellItem(mi)` is the one-way version of `buyItem`, using `consumeItem`/`setGold` instead of `addItem`/`setGold`. There's no bulk sell, only Sell 1 — deliberately simpler than the Buy slider, since selling is new and less central than buying.

Waterskin and Bedroll aren't in `MARKET_ITEMS`, so they're never sellable — same as they were never buyable.

## Alliance reinforcement — the one exception to "every kingdom stands alone"

Every kingdom's `getArmyStrength` is otherwise strictly its own territory (above) — the deliberate exception is `goblinwar_reinforcements` (`{kingdomName: {amount, expiresDay, fromKingdom}}`, sparse), a temporary strength bonus one kingdom can grant another, but only ever through the player: there's no autonomous AI reinforcement, the faction AI tick never touches this key.

- **Trigger:** the Palace action (City/Capital's `showFlavorPanel` extra content, alongside Guard House's stats — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) shows a "Petition for Troops" offer whenever that Capital's *own original* kingdom (`b.state`, and only if it still controls its own capital — `getController(burgId) === b.race`, since a captured capital has no court of its own left to petition) is currently at peace **and** at least one kingdom sharing its `ALLIANCE` is at war. `findAllyNeedingReinforcement(kingdom)` picks whichever qualifying ally currently has the *least* reinforcement (via `getReinforcementBonus`), so repeat petitions across different Capitals or different days spread help around rather than always favoring one ally. If no ally needs help, or the home kingdom is itself at war, there's simply no offer that day — same "sometimes nothing to do here" pattern as the Quest Board when nothing new is postable.
- **Reward, and resolution:** `generatePetitionOffer(burgId)` seeds `20-40` gold / `15-25` XP from the kingdom + `gameDay`, same seeded-PRNG pattern as Quest Board/Notable Figure offers (see [quests.md](quests.md)). Unlike those, accepting (`acceptPetition`) resolves **instantly** — no travel or turn-in step, since the trip to this specific Capital already *is* the "go there" part the idea called for, and convincing a court is one conversation, not a multi-stage task.
- **The effect:** `setReinforcement(ally, getArmyStrength(homeKingdom) * REINFORCEMENT_FRACTION, homeKingdom)` grants the threatened ally a bonus equal to `25%` of the *petitioned* kingdom's own current strength, lasting `REINFORCEMENT_DURATION_DAYS` (14) in-game days from the moment of the petition (`gameDay + 14`, checked lazily by `getReinforcementBonus` against the live `gameDay` — no active expiry/cleanup needed, same pattern as Work's per-day cooldown). A new petition for the same ally **overwrites** rather than stacks, so this can't be farmed into an ever-growing bonus by repeatedly visiting the same or different peaceful Capitals.
- **Known simplification:** the reinforcement is one-directional — the petitioned (sending) kingdom's own strength isn't reduced for having "sent troops." Since they're at peace when petitioned, this has no immediate effect either way, but it means there's no real cost to petitioning beyond the trip there.

## Persistence

`goblinwar_territoryControl`, `goblinwar_warState`, `goblinwar_reinforcements`, `goblinwar_lastWarTick`, and `goblinwar_population` are part of every character's save (see [characters.md](characters.md)) — each character effectively runs its own independent copy of the world's faction/war state, saved and loaded alongside everything else about that character. This matters more than it might sound: since a Supabase account can hold more than one character, and localStorage is a single flat namespace shared by whichever character is currently active on this device, these keys have to travel with the character snapshot — otherwise switching characters on the same device would let one character's war/territory progress bleed into another's.

## Known simplifications (not bugs, just where the scope was deliberately cut)

- War is binary and per-kingdom, not a relations matrix between specific kingdom pairs — a kingdom "at war" is fighting *someone*, not a tracked specific enemy. Simpler to reason about and to display, at the cost of not knowing (or being able to show) exactly who's fighting whom.
- Raids only ever move a settlement one hop of "current controller" — there's no concept of a siege, garrison, or multi-turn fight; `attemptRaid` resolves entirely within one tick. Success odds are population-driven (see above) but still just one clamped probability roll, not a real battle simulation.
- Refugees relocate in a single jump to the single nearest peaceful settlement, every time — no capacity limit on how much a haven can absorb in one tick, no chance of refugees choosing badly or splitting across multiple destinations, and no path/road-distance awareness (straight-line distance only, same simplification `attemptRaid`'s targeting already makes).
- Attrition and raid damage are both flat percentages of *current* population, not modified by a settlement's tier, defenses, or how long it's been at war — a capital and a village bleed population at the same relative rate.
- No autonomous player involvement — the tick runs whether or not the player is anywhere nearby, and there's no way to intervene *during* a raid itself (fight for a settlement, negotiate a peace, feed/shelter refugees, etc.). Alliance reinforcement (above) is the one exception, and it's entirely player-triggered rather than something kingdoms do on their own — there's still no AI-driven reinforcement, and a kingdom under threat has no way to request help itself. See "Living world" in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md).
- **Reinforcement is one petition, one ally, one kingdom at a time.** There's no way to petition on behalf of a specific ally (`findAllyNeedingReinforcement` always picks for you), no partial/gradual reinforcement, and no cost to the sending kingdom. A real diplomacy system (choosing who to help, straining relations with a kingdom asked too often, sending kingdoms bearing some risk) is unbuilt — this is a first, deliberately simple version.
