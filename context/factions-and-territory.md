# Factions, Territory & War Economy (index.html)

The first "living world" system in the game — kingdoms fight over settlements in the background while the player does other things, their populations shrink, flee, or grow because of it, and the Marketplace reacts to all of it. Lives entirely in index.html, keyed off the same weekly tick.

## Two separate ideas: identity vs. control

A settlement's *historical* kingdom and race (`graph.burgs[id].state`/`.race`, from the Azgaar export — see [data-files.md](data-files.md)) never change. That's its permanent identity, same as a real place keeps its name after changing hands. Its *current controller* is a separate, sparse override — tracked by the specific **kingdom**, not just its race, so that a captured settlement's population and military weight belong to the one kingdom that actually holds it, not to "whichever kingdoms happen to share its race":

- `goblinwar_territoryControl`: `{burgId: kingdomName}` (e.g. `"Kingdom of Warg"`), only present for settlements that have actually been fought over. `getControllerKingdom(burgId)` checks this map first and falls back to `graph.burgs[burgId].state` — so an unconquered settlement's controller is always just its own original kingdom, with no explicit entry needed.
- `getController(burgId)` is the race-level convenience most of the codebase actually wants (`KINGDOM_RACE[getControllerKingdom(burgId)]`, falling back to `graph.burgs[burgId].race` for a neutral burg with no kingdom at all) — onMapTap, `showLocationView`'s subtitle, and `getFigureForBurg` only ever needed "which race holds this," so they call this rather than the kingdom-level accessor.
- `setController(burgId, kingdom)` writes a kingdom-name override. Nothing ever deletes one (a settlement can change hands multiple times, but there's no "restore original owner" action).
- **Backward-compat:** saves from before kingdom-level tracking stored a bare race string here instead (e.g. `"ork"`). `getController` still resolves those correctly (a stored value that's itself a real `ALLIANCE` key is treated as already being a race). `getArmyStrength` (below) can't recover which specific kingdom made an old-format capture, though — that settlement's population simply stops counting toward any kingdom's strength until it's captured again, which writes the new format. Self-healing, not silently wrong.

This deliberately avoids renaming anything — `graph.burgs[id].state`/`.name` stay put forever. Wherever the game already shows a settlement's race/kingdom, it should show the *current controller*, not necessarily the original — see "Where this shows up" below.

## War is a relations matrix between specific kingdoms, not a per-kingdom flag

`goblinwar_relations`: `{"KingdomA::KingdomB": "war"|"peace"}`, keyed by `getRelationKey(k1,k2)` (the two kingdom names, sorted, joined with `::`, so `getRelation(a,b)` and `getRelation(b,a)` always agree). An absent entry defaults to `"peace"` — same sparse-override philosophy as `goblinwar_territoryControl`/`goblinwar_population`. Only opposite-alliance pairs are ever meaningful, since same-alliance kingdoms never fight (see `ALLIANCE` below); `getRelation`/`setRelation` are the raw accessors, and `getEnemiesAtWar(kingdom)` returns every opposite-alliance kingdom currently at war with it.

A kingdom can be at war with one specific enemy while at peace with everyone else — a real change from the earlier single `"war"|"peace"` flag per kingdom. `getWarState(kingdom)` still exists with its original signature (`getEnemiesAtWar(kingdom).length ? 'war' : 'peace'`) purely as a derived convenience, so every caller that only ever needed the coarser "is this kingdom fighting *someone*" question — Marketplace pricing, `pickAmbushEnemy`, Guard House's flavor line, Petition for Troops — needed no changes at all. `isAtWarAt(burgId)` is unchanged too (looks up `graph.burgs[burgId].state`, then that kingdom's derived war state).

**No migration for old saves.** `goblinwar_relations` simply starts `{}` (everyone at peace) for any character, old or new — wars begin fresh under the pairwise system rather than trying to reconstruct "who was fighting whom" from the old binary flag, the same "self-healing, not silently wrong" approach `goblinwar_territoryControl`'s own legacy-format handling already takes above.

## Faction data

`KINGDOM_RACE` (kingdom name → race) and `ALL_KINGDOMS` (the ~24 kingdom names) are built once by `buildFactionData()`, called right after `graph` loads (alongside `buildAdjacency()`). Derived by scanning every burg for its `state`/`race` pair — cheap, done once, not persisted (rebuilt fresh every page load from `travel-graph.json`, which is why it's safe to never invalidate).

`ALLIANCE` (`{human:"Good", dwarf:"Good", ork:"Bad", goblin:"Bad"}`, near `RACE_LABEL`) is what "enemy" means for war/raid purposes — any kingdom whose race is on the other side.

### Burg adjacency — a real border, not a proximity guess

`travel-graph.json` has no burg-to-burg or kingdom-to-kingdom border data at all — `graph.edges` only connects map *cells*, most of which aren't a settlement. `buildBurgAdjacency()` (called once after `buildAdjacency()`, alongside `buildFactionData()`) derives a real one: for every burg, it BFSes outward through the existing cell-level `adjacency` graph and, along each branch, stops at the first cell that's home to some *other* burg — collecting those as that burg's neighbors into a module-level `burgAdjacency` map (`burgId -> [burgId, ...]`), never persisted, same "derived fresh every load" treatment as `KINGDOM_RACE`/`ALL_KINGDOMS`.

`isFrontlineBurg(burgId)` uses it: true when any neighbor is controlled by an opposite-alliance kingdom this burg's controller currently has a `"war"` relation with. `buildFrontlineMarkers()` draws a small red ring (in the SVG overlay's `#frontline-markers` group, alongside `#city-icons`) on every burg that qualifies, rebuilt once at load and again after every weekly tick (`maybeRunFactionAI` calls it) — the map's visual read of where the fighting is actually happening.

## The weekly tick

`maybeRunFactionAI()` runs at the end of every `advanceDays(n)` call — so it fires after travel arrival, Inn/camp rest, or a partial-travel camp, whichever advances the calendar. It compares `gameDay` against `goblinwar_lastWarTick` and does nothing unless at least `WAR_TICK_INTERVAL_DAYS` (7) have passed, then immediately stamps `lastWarTick` to the current `gameDay` before running `runFactionAITick()` — so a long journey that jumps the calendar by more than a week still only ticks once, not once per day skipped. It also calls `buildFrontlineMarkers()` afterward so the map's border markers stay current.

**Performance: a tick-scoped read cache.** `getArmyStrength`/`findNearestPeacefulSettlement` each re-scan every one of the ~800 settlements, and `runFactionAITick`'s own callees (`applyWarLoss`, `advanceSiege`) call them a great many times over the course of one tick — measured directly against the real map data, a naive implementation where every `getXxx()` (territory control, population, relations, sieges, reinforcements) does a fresh `JSON.parse(localStorage...)` on every call took **1.6–5.5+ seconds per tick** once a save had run long enough that most settlements carried an override, and kept getting slower as more accumulated — a real, player-visible freeze, not a theoretical concern. `_tickCache` (a module-level variable, `null` outside of a tick) fixes this: `runFactionAITick` populates it with one parsed copy of each sparse map at the start and clears it in a `finally` block at the end; every `getXxx()` checks it first and, when active, returns the *same* cached object on every call — so a `setXxx()`'s in-place mutation (they still all write straight through to `localStorage` too, unchanged) is immediately visible to every later read in the same tick without a separate invalidation step. `getWarState` additionally memoizes its own per-kingdom result inside that cache (`_tickCache.warState`), since `getEnemiesAtWar`'s full `ALL_KINGDOMS` scan was itself being re-run millions of times per tick via `isAtWarAt` inside `findNearestPeacefulSettlement`'s scan — safe because nothing after `runFactionAITick`'s first (declare/peace) pass ever calls `setRelation` again, so a kingdom's war state can't actually change partway through the second pass that depends on it. Together these bring a tick down to roughly 40–300ms even at 24/24 kingdoms simultaneously at war with dozens of active sieges — outside of a tick (Marketplace, Guard House, onMapTap, and the like), every getter still does its normal fresh, uncached read, since those call sites are infrequent and were never the actual cost problem.

`runFactionAITick()` runs in two passes:

1. **War/peace rolls, once per cross-alliance kingdom pair** (same-alliance kingdoms are skipped entirely — they never fight). At peace, `WAR_DECLARE_CHANCE` (6%) chance to flip that specific pair to `"war"`. At war, `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE` (15%) chance to flip back to `"peace"` — which also calls `cancelSiegesBetween(k1,k2)`, ending any active siege between exactly those two kingdoms (the defender keeps whatever it still held; no partial credit).
2. **Attrition and sieges, once per kingdom, per specific enemy it's at war with.** A kingdom with no active war relations does nothing this pass. Otherwise every settlement that originally belongs to it bleeds `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` attrition (see "Population, armies & refugees" below), and for *each* enemy it's currently at war with, `RAID_CHANCE` (35%) chance to call `advanceSiege(kingdom, enemy)`.

If any siege was resolved (a settlement actually captured) during the tick, `maybeRunFactionAI()` shows one toast summarizing it (e.g. `"Grimstan has fallen to Human forces from Kingdom of Bary."`, or a count if more than one) — declarations of war/peace and mid-siege progress don't get a toast, since with 24 kingdoms rolling independently every week that would be constant noise; captures are the events worth the player's attention.

## Sieges: a persistent, multi-week conflict over one settlement

`goblinwar_sieges`: `{burgId: {attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom, startedDay, progress}}` — at most one active siege per settlement (same "one active X" simplicity precedent as Notable Figure/Alliance Reinforcement, below). This replaces the old single-tick "raid resolves instantly" design with something that actually has duration.

- **`pickRaidTarget(attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom)`** chooses which of the defender's settlements to besiege. It now prefers a *real* border settlement — one of the defender's burgs directly reachable, via `burgAdjacency` (above), from territory the attacker already controls — over the old "nearest by straight-line distance to my territory's centroid" guess. That guess is still there as a fallback, used only when the two kingdoms' territory doesn't actually touch (e.g. separated by sea or unclaimed land), same `RAID_CANDIDATE_POOL` (15) it always used.
- **`advanceSiege(attackerKingdom, defenderKingdom)`** either starts a new siege (via `pickRaidTarget`) or advances the one already running between those two kingdoms. Progress per tick comes from the same clamped attacker/defender strength ratio the old single-shot raid used (`attackerStrength / (attackerStrength + defenderStrength)`, clamped to `[RAID_SUCCESS_MIN, RAID_SUCCESS_MAX]` = `0.2`–`0.8`), scaled by `SIEGE_PROGRESS_MAX_PER_TICK` (45) — so a lopsided fight can still resolve in two or three weeks, while an even one drags on much longer, and no kingdom is ever mathematically guaranteed to take or hold a settlement. The besieged settlement also takes `RAID_DAMAGE_RATE` attrition *every* tick the siege continues, not just at the start. At `progress >= 100` the settlement's controller flips (`setController`) and the siege entry is removed.
- **`getGarrisonStrength(burgId)`** (`getPopulation(burgId) * MILITARY_FRACTION`) is a settlement's own local defense, surfaced in the Guard House panel — distinct from `getArmyStrength(kingdom)`, that kingdom's whole mobilizable strength, which is what actually decides siege progress.
- Guard House (`buildGuardHouseStats`) shows an extra "Under siege by {attackerKingdom} — {progress}%" row whenever `goblinwar_sieges[burgId]` exists.

## Population, armies & refugees

Population is the resource everything else in this file ultimately spends. `travel-graph.json` already carries a `population` field per burg — Azgaar's convention, in *thousands of people* (Bary is `12.93`, i.e. ~12,930). That field is the permanent starting figure, exactly like `state`/`race` — never mutated. Current population is `goblinwar_population`, the same sparse-override pattern as territory control: `getPopulation(burgId)` checks the override first, falls back to `graph.burgs[burgId].population`. `setPopulation(burgId, pop)` rounds to the nearest *real person* (`Math.round(pop*1000)/1000`), not the nearest whole thousands-unit — rounding any coarser would zero out a village's population (some start under `1.0`, i.e. under 1,000 people) after one bad week. `formatPopulation(pop)` is the only place that multiplies by 1,000 for display, so every UI surface shows a normal-looking integer (`"12,930"`) rather than the raw `12.93`.

- **War attrition**: every tick, every settlement whose *original* kingdom (`b.state`, not current controller) has any active war relation loses `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` (2%) of its current population via `applyWarLoss`, regardless of whether that specific settlement is the one under siege.
- **Sieges cost more, every tick they run**: `advanceSiege` additionally calls `applyWarLoss(burgId, RAID_DAMAGE_RATE)` (8%) on the besieged settlement every tick the siege continues, not only when it starts.
- **Death vs. refugees**: `applyWarLoss(burgId, rate)` splits the lost population — `REFUGEE_DEATH_SHARE` (40%) is gone for good, `REFUGEE_FLEE_SHARE` (60%) is added to `findNearestPeacefulSettlement(burgId)`'s population instead of vanishing, and that haven settlement is also stamped in `goblinwar_refugeeArrivals` (`{burgId: gameDay}`, via `stampRefugeeArrival`) — see "Aid the Refugees" below. `findNearestPeacefulSettlement` does a full scan of every burg for the closest one whose *current* kingdom (`isAtWarAt`) is at peace — refugees always run toward safety, not just toward any neighbor. A settlement can be both losing population to its own kingdom's war *and* gaining refugee population from some other war-torn kingdom's attrition in the same tick; nothing coordinates the two, they're just independent `applyWarLoss` calls landing on the same burg.
- **Armies are just population, scoped to one kingdom.** `getArmyStrength(kingdom)` sums `getPopulation(id)` across every settlement *that specific kingdom* currently controls (via `getControllerKingdom`, so a captured settlement's population counts toward its *new* controller's strength the very next tick), multiplies by `MILITARY_FRACTION` (8%), and adds any active reinforcement bonus (see "Alliance reinforcement" below) — a rough "how many could be mobilized" figure, not a literal standing army headcount. Deliberately regional otherwise: a kingdom's own strength comes only from its own territory, not from every kingdom that happens to share its race, and not automatically from allied races either. This creates a real feedback loop: winning a siege grows a kingdom's controlled population (and therefore its own army, not its race's), which makes its next siege more likely to succeed too — a snowball, by design, not a bug, but one that plays out kingdom-by-kingdom rather than race-wide.

## Where this shows up

- **`onMapTap`** (the tap-a-settlement info card): the race tag and its color now come from `getController(burgId)`, not `burg.race`, and a `(captured)` note (in the danger-red used elsewhere for warnings) appears whenever the current controller differs from the original race. The kingdom name shown next to it is still the original `burg.state` — identity, not control. A second line below shows that settlement's current population via `formatPopulation(getPopulation(burgId))`.
- **`showLocationView`**'s subtitle: same idea, `"{tier} · {Race}-controlled"` appended only when the controller differs from the original. Its action list also gains two conditional, any-tier actions: **Defend the Walls** whenever `goblinwar_sieges[burgId]` exists, and **Refugee Camp** whenever `hasRecentRefugeeArrival(burgId)` is true — see "Player involvement" below for both.
- **The map itself**: `buildFrontlineMarkers()` rings every `isFrontlineBurg()` settlement in red, alongside the existing City/Capital icons (`buildCityIcons`) — the visual counterpart to burg adjacency, above.
- **Guard House** (`buildGuardHouseStats`, City/Capital-only — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)): shows a kingdom's own mustered strength (named by kingdom, e.g. "Kingdom of Warg forces" — not by race) alongside its usual random flavor line, plus a conditional "Under siege by {kingdom} — {progress}%" row when applicable. `showFlavorPanel` grew an optional extra-HTML parameter just for this — Temple stays pure flavor.
- **`showMarketPanel`**: see below — this is where war state actually has a mechanical effect, not just a label.
- **Combat**: `pickAmbushEnemy` (index.html, see [combat.md](combat.md)) reads `isAtWarAt(burgId)` directly — a Good-alliance settlement whose kingdom is at war has a chance of spawning a themed Goblin/Ork Raiders fight instead of a plain Bandit. This is the one place war state changes something other than a number or a label; it changes which enemy you're actually fighting. **Defend the Walls** (below) is the second.

## War-driven Marketplace pricing

Four constants near `isAtWarAt`: `WAR_BUY_MULTIPLIER` (1.5), `WAR_SELL_BONUS_MULT` (1.2), `PEACE_SELL_FRACTION` (0.5), and `isWarRelevant(mi)` (true for anything with an equip `slot`, plus Firewood by name — see [player-state.md](player-state.md) for what's equippable).

- `getBuyPrice(mi, burgId)` = `mi.price * (1.5 if that settlement's kingdom is at war, else 1)`, rounded.
- `getSellPrice(mi, burgId)` = for war-relevant items *at a settlement whose kingdom is at war*, `mi.price * 1.2` (a premium — wartime demand for gear and fuel); otherwise `mi.price * 0.5` (the normal sell-back cut), war or not. A region at lasting peace never pays the wartime premium for anything, and non-war-relevant goods (Food, Healing Potion, Books) never do either, even mid-war.

`showMarketPanel` calls both on every render — the war/peace banner at the top of the panel (`"{kingdom} — At war — prices up"` / `"At peace"`) reads `isAtWarAt(burgId)` directly, and every item row's Buy slider/button and Sell button price off `getBuyPrice`/`getSellPrice` rather than the flat `mi.price`.

Waterskin and Bedroll aren't in `MARKET_ITEMS`, so they're never sellable — same as they were never buyable.

## Alliance reinforcement — the one exception to "every kingdom stands alone"

Every kingdom's `getArmyStrength` is otherwise strictly its own territory (above) — the deliberate exception is `goblinwar_reinforcements` (`{kingdomName: {amount, expiresDay, fromKingdom}}`, sparse), a temporary strength bonus one kingdom can grant another, but only ever through the player: there's no autonomous AI reinforcement, the faction AI tick never touches this key.

- **Trigger:** the Palace action (City/Capital's `showFlavorPanel` extra content, alongside Guard House's stats — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) shows a "Petition for Troops" offer whenever that Capital's *own original* kingdom (`b.state`, and only if it still controls its own capital — `getController(burgId) === b.race`, since a captured capital has no court of its own left to petition) is currently at peace **and** at least one kingdom sharing its `ALLIANCE` is at war. `findAllyNeedingReinforcement(kingdom)` picks whichever qualifying ally currently has the *least* reinforcement (via `getReinforcementBonus`), so repeat petitions across different Capitals or different days spread help around rather than always favoring one ally. If no ally needs help, or the home kingdom is itself at war, there's simply no offer that day — same "sometimes nothing to do here" pattern as the Quest Board when nothing new is postable.
- **Reward, and resolution:** `generatePetitionOffer(burgId)` seeds `20-40` gold / `15-25` XP from the kingdom + `gameDay`, same seeded-PRNG pattern as Quest Board/Notable Figure offers (see [quests.md](quests.md)). Unlike those, accepting (`acceptPetition`) resolves **instantly** — no travel or turn-in step, since the trip to this specific Capital already *is* the "go there" part the idea called for, and convincing a court is one conversation, not a multi-stage task.
- **The effect:** `setReinforcement(ally, getArmyStrength(homeKingdom) * REINFORCEMENT_FRACTION, homeKingdom)` grants the threatened ally a bonus equal to `25%` of the *petitioned* kingdom's own current strength, lasting `REINFORCEMENT_DURATION_DAYS` (14) in-game days from the moment of the petition (`gameDay + 14`, checked lazily by `getReinforcementBonus` against the live `gameDay` — no active expiry/cleanup needed, same pattern as Work's per-day cooldown). A new petition for the same ally **overwrites** rather than stacks, so this can't be farmed into an ever-growing bonus by repeatedly visiting the same or different peaceful Capitals.
- **Known simplification:** the reinforcement is one-directional — the petitioned (sending) kingdom's own strength isn't reduced for having "sent troops." Since they're at peace when petitioned, this has no immediate effect either way, but it means there's no real cost to petitioning beyond the trip there.

## Player involvement

Three direct hooks into a war's outcome, on top of the always-indirect Alliance Reinforcement above — the payoff of the relations matrix now naming *specific* enemies rather than "the war" in the abstract.

- **Negotiate Peace** (`generateNegotiatePeaceOffer`/`buildNegotiatePeaceHTML`/`acceptNegotiatePeace`) is a second Palace card, alongside Petition for Troops, shown whenever the Capital's own kingdom (same eligibility check as Petition) has at least one active war relation (`getEnemiesAtWar`). It picks the *weakest* current enemy (by `getArmyStrength`, same "start with the easiest win" logic Petition's ally-pick uses) and prices a bribe (`NEGOTIATE_PEACE_BASE_COST`, 150 gold, scaled `0.4`–`1.6`× by how lopsided the strength ratio is — cheaper against a weak enemy, pricier against a strong one). On success, `setRelation(kingdom, enemy, 'peace')` and `cancelSiegesBetween` end any active siege between exactly those two kingdoms outright — the defender keeps whatever it still held. Both Palace cards' buttons are wired together by `wirePalaceButtons(burgId)`, since a render can now show either or both.
- **Defend the Walls** (`triggerSiegeDefenseFight`/`resolveSiegeDefense`) appears at any settlement (any tier — sieges aren't restricted to City/Capital) currently in `goblinwar_sieges`. It builds a 2-3 enemy fight from the attacking kingdom's own race (`ENEMY_VARIANTS[KINGDOM_RACE[attackerKingdom]]`) and starts it through the same generic `startCombat` ambushes use (see [combat.md](combat.md)) — `startCombat`'s own ambush-flavored title/log strings are overwritten right after the call with siege-appropriate text. Its `onEnd` callback is `resolveSiegeDefense`: a **victory** knocks `SIEGE_DEFENSE_PROGRESS_RELIEF` (30 percentage points) off the siege's progress and pays a gold/XP reward — not an instant win, deliberately, per the project's own "player can grow strong but never solo-topple a city" design constraint (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)) — while a **defeat or flee** leaves the siege to advance normally on the next weekly tick. Gated to once per settlement per day (`goblinwar_siegeDefenseCooldowns`, same pattern as Work's `hasWorkedToday`), so it can meaningfully help without trivializing every siege into a single fight.
- **Aid the Refugees** (`buildRefugeeAidHTML`/`acceptRefugeeAid`) is the simplest of the three, deliberately: shown at any settlement with a recent arrival (`hasRecentRefugeeArrival`, within `REFUGEE_ARRIVAL_WINDOW_DAYS` = 7 days of `stampRefugeeArrival` — see "Population, armies & refugees" above). Spending `REFUGEE_AID_GOLD_COST` (15) gold and `REFUGEE_AID_FOOD_COST` (2) Food adds a small population boost (`REFUGEE_AID_POP_BOOST`, 0.05 — about 50 people) to that settlement and pays modest XP, resolving instantly like Petition for Troops rather than as a travel/turn-in quest.

## Persistence

`goblinwar_territoryControl`, `goblinwar_relations`, `goblinwar_reinforcements`, `goblinwar_lastWarTick`, `goblinwar_population`, `goblinwar_sieges`, `goblinwar_refugeeArrivals`, and `goblinwar_siegeDefenseCooldowns` are part of every character's save (see [characters.md](characters.md)) — each character effectively runs its own independent copy of the world's faction/war state, saved and loaded alongside everything else about that character. This matters more than it might sound: since a Supabase account can hold more than one character, and localStorage is a single flat namespace shared by whichever character is currently active on this device, these keys have to travel with the character snapshot — otherwise switching characters on the same device would let one character's war/territory progress bleed into another's.

## Known simplifications (not bugs, just where the scope was deliberately cut)

- Sieges still resolve as one settlement at a time with no garrison-vs-attacker battle simulation beyond the clamped strength-ratio roll — `getGarrisonStrength` exists but isn't yet a hard defensive multiplier, just a number shown to the player.
- Refugees relocate in a single jump to the single nearest peaceful settlement, every time — no capacity limit on how much a haven can absorb, no chance of refugees choosing badly or splitting across multiple destinations, and no path/road-distance awareness (straight-line distance only — unlike `pickRaidTarget`'s targeting, which now does use the real border graph).
- Attrition and siege damage are both flat percentages of *current* population, not modified by a settlement's tier, defenses, or how long it's been under siege — a capital and a village bleed population at the same relative rate.
- **Negotiate Peace has no lasting cost or diplomatic memory.** A truce can be bought again the moment a new war breaks out with the same or a different enemy, and there's no reputation/relationship tracking beyond the live `war`/`peace` relation itself.
- **Reinforcement is still one petition, one ally, one kingdom at a time**, and still entirely player-triggered with no AI-driven equivalent — a kingdom under siege has no way to request help on its own, only Negotiate Peace or Defend the Walls give the player a way to actually change an outcome, and there's still no partial/gradual reinforcement or cost to the sending kingdom.
