# Roadmap: Stubs Already Scaffolded in the Code

Every "coming soon" placeholder currently in the game, collected in one place so future work has a checklist instead of needing to grep for it. Each entry names the exact file/element so implementing it means replacing that stub, not guessing where it should live.

## Skills & Progression — character.html — in progress

**Built so far:** the whole loop is real. Combat victories grant XP (`ENEMY_VARIANTS`' `xpRange`, rolled in `endCombat`), leveling up grants a skill point plus a small Health/Stamina bump, and character.html lets a player spend each point on Strength/Agility/Intelligence, one of 9 passive perks, or one of 6 active special attacks — all sharing the same `goblinwar_skills` unlock storage and stat-threshold gating. The active moves show up as a "Specials" button in combat (`#combat-specials-panel`, `combatSpecial()` in index.html) alongside Attack/Flee, each usable once per fight. See [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s "Leveling & stats" for exactly where every perk and move hooks in.

The former naming collision is resolved: the active move is "Crushing Blow" (2x damage), distinct from the passive "Power Strike" perk (+2 flat damage, always on).

**Not built yet:** nothing — multi-enemy encounters (see below) closed the last gap here.

**Data model:** five new `localStorage` keys (see [player-state.md](player-state.md) once added) — `goblinwar_level` (default `1`), `goblinwar_xp` (default `0`), `goblinwar_skillPoints` (unspent points, default `0`), `goblinwar_stats` (`{str, agi, int}`, default all `0`), `goblinwar_skills` (`{skillId: true}` for unlocked perks/moves, default `{}`). All five need adding to the character snapshot shape in all three duplicated spots (index.html, settings.html, characters.html — see [characters.md](characters.md)) or leveling resets on save/switch.

XP curve: roughly `xpForLevel(n) = 40 * n^1.5`. Each `ENEMY_VARIANTS` entry (see [combat.md](combat.md)) gets an `xpReward` field alongside its existing `goldRange`, rolled in `endCombat('victory')` the same way gold is. Leveling up grants 1 skill point plus a small flat bump to `maxHealth`/`maxStamina` (e.g. +8/+4), so it feels good even before a point is spent.

**Passive perks** (spend a skill point, gated on a stat threshold so raw stats and perks stay entangled): Power Strike (Str, +2 flat dmg), Pack Mule (Str, +10 carry capacity — see `CARRY_CAPACITY` in [player-state.md](player-state.md)), Iron Skin (Str, −1 flat dmg taken), Evasion (Agi, 15% chance to fully dodge the enemy counter-attack in `combatAttack()`), Second Wind (Agi, 25% chance an attack costs no stamina), Sure Feet (Agi, −10% ambush chance, see `AMBUSH_CHANCE_PER_DAY`/`rollAmbush()` in [combat.md](combat.md)), Silver Tongue (Int, −10% Marketplace buy prices), Scavenger (Int, +20% gold from victories), Field Rations (Int, −10% Hunger/Thirst gained from travel/waiting, see `hoursToHungerGain()`/`hoursToThirstGain()` in [player-state.md](player-state.md)).

**Active special attacks** — built. Combat no longer stays "just hit Attack": a "Specials" button opens `#combat-specials-panel`, listing whichever of the 6 moves are unlocked, each costing more stamina than a basic Attack and usable once per fight (`combat.usedMoves`, reset in `startCombat`): Crushing Blow (Str, 2x damage to your target), Reckless Swing (Str, 2.5x damage to your target, but that one enemy's counter ignores armor *and* Evasion — see "Multi-enemy encounters" below for why it's scoped to just that enemy), Precise Shot (Agi — since the game has no miss chance to guarantee against, this instead always rolls max damage rather than random), Adrenaline Rush (Agi, a free extra attack with no stamina cost), War Cry (Int, cuts *every* enemy's damage 30% for the rest of the fight, deals no damage itself), Cleave (Str, higher tier — hits every alive enemy for 60% damage each).

**Multi-enemy encounters — built.** Goblin/Ork raiding-party ambushes now spawn 2-3 enemies at once (Bandit ambushes stay solo); `combat.enemies` is an array with click-to-target UI (`#combat-enemies`, `renderEnemies()`), a per-enemy HP bar, auto-retargeting off a killed enemy, and every alive enemy countering each round (`runEnemyCounters`) — the actual difficulty lever that makes a raid harder than a solo fight, not just longer. See [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Multi-enemy encounters" section for the full mechanics and the balance calls made (War Cry/Cleave hit every enemy; Reckless Swing's ignore-armor counter is scoped to only its one target).

**Still deliberately not part of this build:**
- **Talkable NPCs / dialogue** — already captured below as an unscoped idea; explicitly parked, unrelated system (NPC data model, conversation UI, settlement hooks).

**Moved off character.html onto a new "Skills" tab — done.** Per the project owner: Strength/Agility/Intelligence, the 9 perks, and the 6 special attacks now live on [skills.html](secondary-pages.md), a new top-level nav destination on index.html's toolbar alongside Inventory/Party/Hero/Settings, rather than inside the Character sheet. character.html no longer shows any of it — the Level/XP header, Character Stats box, Perks card, and Special Attacks card were removed from character.html entirely and rebuilt on the new page (`renderProgression`/`renderSkillList`, `PERKS`/`MOVES`), leaving character.html back to identity/Health/Stamina/Equipment/Accessories/Purse/basic info. The combat-facing half (`combatSpecial`, `MOVES`, `hasPerk`, etc.) was already in index.html and is unaffected. See [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md) for the full page breakdown.

**Perks + Special Attacks reworked into one Cyberpunk-2077-style skill tree — done.** Per the project owner, skills.html's flat Perks list and Special Attacks list read as two disconnected cards; they're now a single branching tree per stat (`ALL_SKILLS` merges `PERKS`/`MOVES`, grouped into tiered columns by `req`, connected node-to-node), with a shared detail panel below the tree showing whichever node was last tapped. Purely presentational — same shared skill points, same `goblinwar_skills` storage, same stat gating as always; see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s "Leveling & stats" for the full node-by-node breakdown of what didn't change.

## Quests — index.html — in progress

**Built so far:** four quest types across two sources — see [quests.md](quests.md) for the full mechanics. The Quest Board (any settlement, a fifth action alongside Inn/Marketplace/Work/Talk to Townsfolk) offers minor delivery jobs — a coin flip per slot between Deliver a Message and the heavier-paying Deliver Cargo — capped per settlement tier (village 1, town 2, city 2, capital 3) and reset once a real in-game month rather than daily, with each slot's actual count independently rolled from 0 up to that tier's cap. Delivery targets exclude enemy territory (alliance-relative, not kingdom-specific — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)) and reward scales with real travel distance from the giver, shown next to the quest as a travel-time estimate. A Notable Figure (a new sixth action, but only at the handful of specific settlements one of the 5 named `NOTABLE_FIGURES` calls home — not every City/Capital) offers one important kill-task at a time, capped globally so only one can ever be active: Deal with the Bandits at peace, or Drive Off the Raiders (the actual Goblin/Ork threat, paying more) once that figure's home kingdom is at war — reacting to real game state the same way Marketplace pricing already does. Figures are pinned to their home settlement rather than reassigned daily, and disappear (the action stops appearing, any accepted quest is unaffected) if that settlement is captured by another race — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md). This was a deliberate split from the project owner: important quests come from talking to a character, minor busywork from a board — see "Talkable NPCs" below for how the Notable Figure relates to that still-unscoped idea.

**Quests tab, map markers, and Abandon — done.** A dedicated [quests.html](quests.html) toolbar tab (see [context/secondary-pages.md](context/secondary-pages.md)) lists Active Quests (each with an Abandon button) and a new Recently Completed history (`goblinwar_completedQuests`, capped at 20 entries — the "no completed-quest history" gap below is resolved). Every active delivery quest's destination also gets a small flag marker on the map itself (`buildQuestMarkers()`), and the Quests nav icon carries a live count badge of active quests.

**Not built yet:** the tracker's bigger/structurally-different quest ideas — Protect a Caravan, Destroy a Goblin Nest, Train Villagers, Ask for News (overlaps with Talk to Townsfolk), a Faction Reputation Chain (multi-part, tied to standing with a race), and a fuller Randomised Bounty Board. [quests.md](quests.md)'s "Adding a new quest type" section is the pattern to follow for each — the delivery and kill families already proved it generalizes cleanly (each went from 1 type to 2 without touching the turn-in architecture), these remaining ones are just less well-suited to either existing family.

**Petition for Troops — built, but as a Palace action rather than a `goblinwar_quests` entry.** The idea (travel to a kingdom that isn't at war and convince its royalty to reinforce a threatened ally) turned out to fit the existing Palace flavor-panel better than the Deliver/Kill quest families: the trip to that specific Capital already *is* the "go there" part, and convincing a court is one conversation, not a multi-stage travel-then-turn-in task — so it resolves instantly on accept rather than needing a persisted active quest. See [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s "Alliance reinforcement" section for the full mechanic (`goblinwar_reinforcements`, `findAllyNeedingReinforcement`, `acceptPetition`) and [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md) for where it hooks into the Palace action. Reward is 20-40 gold / 15-25 XP, same seeded-offer pattern as the Quest Board/Notable Figure.

## Equipment — done; Accessories — done

Equipping is real: six core `MARKET_ITEMS` entries (Sword, Shield, Leather Cap, Leather Armour, Leather Boots, Lucky Charm) each carry a `slot` and a `dmg`/`def` combat bonus, `goblinwar_equipped` tracks what's in each of the 6 slots, inventory.html is where you equip/unequip (an Equip/Unequip button per equippable item row), character.html shows what's equipped and lets you unequip from there too, and `combatAttack()` (index.html) actually applies the bonus to damage dealt/taken — see [player-state.md](player-state.md) and [combat.md](combat.md) for the full mechanics.

**Accessories — done.** The 8-slot grid on character.html is no longer decoration: 8 more `MARKET_ITEMS` entries (Ring of Vigor, Band of Warding, Amulet of the Wilds, Clasp of the Wayfarer, Warbelt, Iron Bracers, Silver Earring, Sigil Charm) each carry their own `slot` id (`ring_left`, `ring_right`, `amulet`, `cloak`, `belt`, `bracers`, `earring`, `charm`) and a small `dmg`/`def` bonus, using the exact same `goblinwar_equipped`/`ITEM_STATS`/equip-button machinery as the original 6 gear slots — `EQUIP_SLOTS` (index.html, inventory.html) just grew from 6 entries to 14. character.html splits its own rendering into two grids off one shared `renderSlotGrid(gridId, slots)` helper (`equip-grid` for the original 6, `accessory-grid` for the new 8) so the visual 3×2/4×2 split stays. inventory.html no longer has a separate grid at all anymore (see "Inventory consolidated into one flat list" below) — all 14 slots' equip state shows inline on each item's own row instead. Buying, selling (with sell-the-last-copy auto-unequip), and the Marketplace listing all worked with zero special-casing, since both pages already drove every equip surface off the shared `EQUIP_SLOTS`/`ITEM_STATS` arrays rather than hardcoding the original 6.
- **Inventory consolidated into one flat list — done.** Per the project owner, inventory.html's separate Equipment & Accessories slot-grid (below the Carried Items list) was redundant — every equippable item already got its own Equip/Unequip button and highlight in that list. The grid (and its unused `SLOT_LABEL`/click-to-unequip code) is gone; character.html's own two grids are untouched (equip/unequip works differently there — display plus unequip-only — see [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md), and any further merging is bound up in the Hero Screen design conversation, see below). Item rows on inventory.html are also visually smaller now (tighter padding, a 19px icon instead of 26px), since a fuller bag was starting to make the page feel long.

**A Marketplace/Blacksmith equip hint, and Dropping an item — both done.** Every equippable row now shows an "Equips: {slot}" tag before you buy it (`EQUIP_SLOT_LABELS`, index.html — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)), so you no longer have to buy something and check inventory.html just to learn its slot. inventory.html also grew a Drop button on every row (`consumeItem`, auto-unequipping the last copy of an equipped item the same way selling does — see [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md)), the answer to the old "no way to destroy an item for no gold" gap.

**Equipment rarity/tiers — done, via a 4-tier item ladder per slot.** The 6 core Equipment slots each now have a Bronze/Iron/Steel/Mithril version (Bronze/Iron at the Marketplace, Steel at any City/Capital Blacksmith, Mithril at a Capital Blacksmith only — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s quality ladder and [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s "The Blacksmith"), replacing the old single fixed item per slot. Accessories (Warbelt and the other 7) are unaffected — still one fixed item apiece, deliberately out of scope since they're unique named items, not mass-produced tiered gear. What's still not built: stacking multiple bonus *sources* per slot (each slot holds exactly one item, as intended) or a per-instance upgrade system (inventory still stacks by name/qty, not individual item identity) — the 4-tier ladder is a real answer to "gear should vary in power," not to "this specific Sword should get better."

## Faction AI, territory & war economy — index.html

A real weekly tick exists — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md) for the full mechanics: a pairwise kingdom relations matrix (not a single per-kingdom flag), real burg/kingdom border data driving both raid targeting and a map frontline overlay, multi-week sieges with garrisons instead of a single-tick coin-flip, and three direct ways for the player to affect an outcome (Defend the Walls, Negotiate Peace, Aid the Refugees) beyond the existing Alliance Reinforcement. ## Talk to Townsfolk — index.html — done

A real `'talk'` action now, alongside Marketplace — reuses `showFlavorPanel` (the same pure-flavor panel Temple/Guard House/Palace share, see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)). `getTownsfolkLines(burgId)` assembles the eligible line pool per visit from `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_PEACE`/`_WAR` (via `isAtWarAt`), `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_VILLAGE`/`_CITY` (by `b.tier`), and `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_CAPTURED` (added on top whenever `getController(burgId) !== b.race`) — a settlement-flavored counterpart to the Inn's `RUMOURS`, deliberately scoped as flavor-only, same as the two ideas below it were kept separate from this.

## Talkable NPCs — idea only, nothing built yet

Loose idea, not scoped or scheduled: named NPCs the player can actually talk to, either standing around at a settlement's locations (extending Talk to Townsfolk's flavor text into real characters) or attached to a kingdom's army/military presence (tying into the faction war system — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)). No data model, no UI, no hook into any existing screen yet — this is purely a placeholder so the idea isn't lost, not a spec to build against. Also unscoped and separate: a real "Ask for News" quest type overlapping with Talk to Townsfolk (see the Quests section's "Not built yet" list above) — both were deliberately left out of Talk to Townsfolk's flavor-only build so it stayed small.

The Notable Figure quest-giver (see the Quests section above and [quests.md](quests.md)) is a small, narrow precedent toward this — a named NPC (`NOTABLE_FIGURES`, just `{name, title}`) with a single flavor line and one quest to offer, no portrait, no dialogue tree, no persistence beyond the quest itself. It was scoped deliberately small to unblock "important quests come from a character," not as a first slice of this bigger idea — but if Talkable NPCs ever gets properly scoped, `NOTABLE_FIGURES` and the panel pattern in `showNotableFigurePanel` are a reasonable starting point rather than a clean-sheet design.

**Notable Figure portraits — a smaller, already-scoped task, separate from the full idea above. What already exists toward the bigger idea: a growing roster of human and dwarf character art (knights, mages, rangers, dwarven warriors) cataloged with generation prompts in the "Character Forge" tracker artifact, separate from this repo — ask for its link if it's not at hand. Those entries are intentionally left unchecked for "Asset implemented in game," since none of them are wired into anything here yet. When this idea gets scoped for real, start there for character looks rather than commissioning fresh art.

**Natasha and Sophie — two named characters already designed, per the project owner.** Natasha is a princess of one of the major human kingdoms, meant to be a real talkable character once this system exists. Sophie is Natasha's companion, a large mythical dog, meant to appear alongside her in dialogue scenes. Both have portrait art in Character Forge now — Sophie has two expressions (Friendly/Neutral), the first entry there to use Character Forge's new `expressions` field (see below). Neither is wired into anything in the game yet — this is purely so the idea isn't lost before Talkable NPCs gets properly scoped, same as everything else in this section.

Sophie's Friendly/Neutral pair is the first use of it — the pattern is there for combat poses or dialogue frames once those get generated.

## Party / Companions — party.html

**The roster/portrait system is built, and recruiting is real now too.** index.html has a `COMPANIONS` catalog array (`{id, name, title, portrait, blurb, homeBurgId}` per entry, following the same one-array-entry-plus-a-PNG pattern as `ENEMY_VARIANTS`/`NOTABLE_FIGURES` — party.html duplicates a leaner copy without `homeBurgId`, per the no-modules convention — see [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md)) and a `goblinwar_party` `localStorage` key (JSON array of recruited companion ids, part of the character snapshot shape, see [characters.md](characters.md)) that drives which of them actually show up. `MAX_PARTY_SIZE` (`3`) caps how many can be active at once (companions and mercenaries share the cap), per the project owner — shown as a live "n / 3" counter on party.html.

**The three companions — Ser Brannoc Ward (human knight), Wren Sable (human mage), Thrain Emberforge (dwarf warrior)** — are original characters, deliberately distinct from both the existing unused knight/mage/dwarf roster in Character Forge and from Natasha/Sophie, which the project owner confirmed are staying out of the party system. All three have real generated art (`assets/party/brannoc-ward.png`, `assets/party/wren-sable.png`, `assets/party/thrain-emberforge.png`, set as each entry's `portrait`), also attached back to their Character Forge tracker entries (`hasImage: true`).

**Recruiting — done.** Per the project owner: "have them in random cities, but you have to talk to them to hire them — they give you their story and why they want to join, and you decide." Each companion is pinned to one home settlement (`homeBurgId`) spread across different cities than `NOTABLE_FIGURES` uses, and Thrain (a dwarf) sits in Dwarf territory rather than Human, matching his own race. A new "A New Face" settlement action (`getCompanionForBurg`, same disappears-if-captured/already-taken rule `NOTABLE_FIGURES` uses, city/capital tier only) opens `showCompanionPanel` — the companion's name, title, and `blurb` (their own spoken reason for wanting to join, reused verbatim), with **Not Now**/**Recruit** buttons rather than a mercenary's instant paid hire. Declining isn't remembered; the same companion is there to talk to again next visit. A full party shows that instead of a Recruit button. Recruited companions get their own **Part Ways** button on party.html (`partWithCompanion`) to free the slot back up, worded differently from a mercenary's Dismiss since they're not a paid employee.

**Combat participation — done.** Recruited companions now fight alongside the player exactly like hired mercenaries do (see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Party in combat") — `COMPANIONS` grew its own `combatHp`/`combatDmgRange` per entry, `buildCombatAllies()` merges `getParty()` in alongside `getMercenaries()`, and a companion who falls in a fight is removed from `goblinwar_party` for good, gated by the `permadeathCompanions` difficulty toggle (on by default) exactly the way `permadeathMercenaries` already gated mercenaries — the first thing that toggle has ever actually done, closing the gap the difficulty setting left open when it shipped. **Still not built:** the loyalty meter (see "Companion loyalty" below) — a recruited companion can now fight and die, but nothing yet tracks approval or reacts to player choices.

## UI Theme — pixel-art blue/gold reskin — in progress

Originally a loose, unscoped idea from the project owner: replace the dark-parchment palette (see [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)'s Conventions section) with a pixel-art "royal blue and gold" fantasy RPG look — reference is a mobile hero-sheet screen with a deep blue gradient background, thick ornate gold pixel-art panel borders, segmented pixel Health/Stamina bars, a level badge, a race pill, and locked/unlocked perk cards with an "UNLOCK" button. Generation prompts for each art asset are tracked in the standalone "UI Forge" tracker artifact (28 prompts, split out of Item Forge's old "UI Theme" section and reviewed for generation reliability) — ask for its link if it's not at hand. 27 of the 28 now have real generated art.

**Live in the game now, on every player page (`index.html` plus the 10 secondary pages — not the unused `game-map.html`):** the `:root` palette (`--ink`, `--water-dk`, plus every hardcoded dark-brown panel/header color) swapped from warm browns to a deep navy sampled directly from the generated art, and the Press Start 2P pixel font applied to every page's `h1`. `assets/ui/` holds the real art actually wired in: `bg-dither.jpg` (Background Dither Texture, blended behind every header), `sword-icon.png` (Top-Bar Sword Icon, on every page's Back button), `border-frame.png` (9-Slice Panel Border, via `border-image` — character.html's/skills.html's `.card`, plus the single standout panel on each of inventory.html (`.gold-card`), login.html (`.card`), settings.html (`.current-card`), and characters.html (`.char-card`, not the dashed `.create-card` — that dashed style deliberately reads as an "add new" affordance, not real content, same reasoning as character.html's dashed empty equip-slots), `health-track.png`/`health-fill.png`/`stamina-track.png`/`stamina-fill.png` (the real Health/Stamina bars — index.html's combat view, character.html's Hero sheet; enemy/ally combat bars are untouched, no art for those tiers yet), `race-pill.png` (character.html's "Human" tag), `level-badge.png` (skills.html's Level box), `unlock-btn.png` (skills.html's node-unlock button — the padlock art plus real "Unlock (1 point)" text on top, per the split done when this prompt was written), and `perk-*.png`/`move-*.png` (all 9 Perk + 6 Special-Attack skill-tree node icons on skills.html, full color once ready/unlocked and desaturated while still locked). The source images all arrived from chat as flattened JPEGs with a baked-in checkerboard standing in for transparency (JPEG has no alpha channel) — real alpha was recovered with a flood-fill script before any of it could be used as a sprite.

**Not wired in yet, though the art exists:**

Only 7 of the 24 tiered Equipment items have real art so far (all 4 Sword tiers, 3 of the 4 Shield tiers — Mithril Shield is still generic) — every other slot (Helm/Chestplate/Greaves/Charm) and every non-equipment item still shows the generic icon.

## Smaller/implicit gaps (not marked with a UI stub, but visible from the code)

- **Hunger & Thirst — done.** Replaced the old per-trip Food-cost gate entirely — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section for the full mechanic. Food and Waterskin are the game's two real consumables: inventory.html's item rows grow **Eat**/**Drink** buttons (`eat-food-btn`/`drink-waterskin-btn`) that consume one and relieve Hunger/Thirst by a flat amount, disabled once the relevant meter is already at 0 — the first "use a consumable" interaction in the game, so this is the pattern to extend if Healing Potion/Books/Firewood ever get one too. Neither restores Stamina anymore (an earlier build briefly had Waterskin do that; reversed at the project owner's direction once Hunger/Thirst existed as the real place for it to land). Both meters also rise passively from travel/waiting and from combat (win, lose, or flee alike — see [combat.md](combat.md)); left unattended, either overflowing past 100 converts the excess straight into Health damage, floored so starvation/dehydration alone can never be fatal. Bedroll is no longer a flavor-only possession: it's now an expensive (45g) Marketplace purchase that, carried, both multiplies the night-ambush roll by `BEDROLL_AMBUSH_MULT` (0.85, stacking with the Sure Feet perk's own 0.9) *and* grants a `BEDROLL_STAMINA_BONUS` (15) overcap on top of a full stamina refill after resting — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s Tavern/Camp rest write-ups. inventory.html still shows this as an informational note on Bedroll's row rather than a button, since both effects are passive.
- **The Pub — done, then merged into the Inn to become one Tavern.** Originally a sixth settlement action gated by tier (every City/Capital, ~40% of Towns via a seeded per-burg coin flip, no Villages) offering an instant "Meal & Drink" for 12g that fully relieves both Hunger and Thirst in one purchase. Per the project owner, having a settlement's rest stop and its food-and-drink stop be two separate locations didn't make sense — **The Inn** and **The Pub** are now **The Tavern**, one action at every settlement (no more tier/coin-flip gating on the meal option), offering Rest for the Night, Meal & Drink, and Tavern Talk together. See [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s "The Tavern" section.
- **No character customization.** character.html hardcodes "The Traveler" / "Human" / "Kingdom of Bary" — see "Character creation & race" below, where this ties into a bigger idea from the dev tracker.

- **World map rebuilt as a painterly render — done.** Two earlier approaches were tried and abandoned before landing here: first, a grid of large unique plates painted by Gemini and stitched together (dropped — Gemini couldn't hold a consistent style across that many big, independently-painted regions, even with reference images); then a small reusable pixel-art tileset stamped by a data-driven pipeline (dropped at the project owner's direction in favor of a softer, hand-drawn look — see the reference discussed in the session that built this). What shipped needed no external art generation at first — `scripts/build-world-raster/render-painterly.js` (a standalone offline Node tool, its own `package.json`, `sharp` for compositing, `node_modules` gitignored — not part of the game's runtime) renders `assets/world-raster.jpg` directly from `assets/game-map.json`'s real Voronoi cell geometry, biome, elevation, river, and state data, entirely vector-drawn — though four of those elements (forests, mountains, water, land grain) were later replaced with real generated sprite/texture art, see further down:
  - **Soft-blended terrain** — every land cell's polygon is filled with its biome color, rendered via SVG. Mountains are no longer one flat tone: `mountainBandColor(h)` blends between a brown/rocky low-elevation color and a cooler grey high-elevation one based on the cell's own height, closer to real banded rock than a single tint (snow caps still layer on top of the very highest peaks separately).
  - **Coastline** — a two-pass chamfer distance transform computes real per-pixel distance-to-nearest-land (not cell-hop distance, which produced blocky, wrong-looking water in an earlier pass). Colors are linearly interpolated between each `WATER_BANDS` entry by that distance (not bucketed into hard steps), and the whole water layer gets its own dedicated blur (`--water-blur`, independent of the tiny blur applied to everything else) — both were needed together to actually read as a soft fade from shore to open ocean rather than a stepped edge, plus a crisp dark coastline stroke drawn unblurred on top.
  - **Rivers** — traced from `pack.rivers`' actual cell sequences, Chaikin-smoothed (not Catmull-Rom — that extrapolates a tangent through each point and pinches into a cusp on a sharp turn; Chaikin only ever interpolates between existing points, so it can't loop or cusp), tapered from thin at each river's genuine headwater up to full width at its mouth (SVG can't vary stroke width continuously along one path, so this is stepped — a few overlapping sub-segments, each its own width), and extended to the *exact* shared boundary point between the last land cell and the sea (not that cell's centroid, which can sit visibly inland) so every river reaches the coast flush, no gap and no overshoot poking through the coastline stroke.
  - **Forests, mountains, water, and land grain now use real generated art, not vector shapes — done, superseding the vector-drawn versions of all four described just above (the surrounding terrain/coastline/river/pixel-art/sundown techniques are all still exactly as described).** The project owner asked for real image assets in place of the SVG circles/triangles/hachure-ticks/dot-scatter this file used to draw for those four specifically. A companion Claude Artifact ("Terrain Prompt Forge") holds the actual copy-ready image-generation prompts (a deciduous tree icon, a conifer/taiga tree icon, a mountain-peak icon, a seamless water texture, a seamless land-grain texture — each written to match this file's established pixel-art/golden-hour house style) and a per-asset status tracker. What came back and shipped:
    - **Icons were generated on a magenta (#ff00ff) chroma-key background** so real alpha transparency could be extracted, but partially-transparent edge pixels still carried un-premultiplied magenta in their stored RGB (a visible pink halo around every icon once composited over anything but magenta). Solving the standard "observed = alpha·true + (1-alpha)·bg" compositing equation backwards for `true` at the raw per-pixel alpha turned out to be numerically unstable at low alpha (confirmed — produced neon green/white noise, not a subtle tint), so the fix instead snaps the whole antialiased edge ramp to a hard cutout: alpha ≥ 0.6 gets de-fringed (stable in that range) and forced fully opaque, anything below that is fully transparent — which also happens to match the pixel-art house style (hard edges, not soft antialiasing) better than the original soft edge would have anyway.
    - **The land-grain texture came back as an opaque JPEG literally depicting a grey/white transparency checkerboard behind its speckle dots**, not a real alpha channel — a generator failure mode when asked for "transparent background" without the output format actually supporting alpha. Recovered by classifying each pixel as background (near-neutral/desaturated, matching the checkerboard) versus real content (the colored speckle dots), with alpha scaled by saturation for soft dot edges. A handful of oversized blotches in the source art (confirmed: a few ~20x20px solid blobs among the intended ~4-8px dots) read as an odd, evenly-spaced dark-red square once tiled across the whole map — fixed with a flood-fill connected-component pass over the recovered alpha mask that drops any blob bigger than a real dot should be.
    - **Trees place as small clumps of 2-3 overlapping sprites, not one icon per point** — a single isolated overhead tree icon read as too sparse/graphic at map scale (real forest cover is continuous canopy), so each per-cell seeded position is now a clump center with its own tight huddle of sprites (own size/rotation roll each), and the old single-icon-per-point density was roughly halved to compensate so total sprite count (and render time) stayed in the same ballpark.
    - **Mountain icons cluster tightly per cell** (radius capped at 60% of the cell's own radius) so overlapping icons read as one continuous range sitting on top of the still-present elevation-banded color fill, rather than scattered separate shapes.
    - **The water texture tiles across the whole canvas and blends over the existing shore-distance band-color raster** (`--water-texture-strength`, default 0.55) rather than replacing it — the band coloring still carries the correct shallow-to-deep hue transition, the tiled texture adds real ripple detail on top. The old small hand-drawn "~" wave arcs are gone, superseded by this.
    - **Compositing tens of thousands of individual tree/mountain sprite placements as one giant `sharp().composite()` overlay array turned out to be the real performance trap** — a first attempt stalled for a very long time (high CPU, ~3.8GB resident) and had to be killed well past when a render this size should reasonably finish. Fixed by pre-caching each sprite's raw RGBA pixels once per (size, rotation) combination, then hand-blitting each placement directly into a raw canvas buffer with plain per-pixel typed-array math (`blitInto`) instead of asking libvips to manage that many separate composite layers — full render time dropped from indefinite/hung to ~22 seconds.
    - **A follow-up pass fixed two things the project owner caught after seeing the first shipped version.** First, the mountain icon itself was the wrong idea entirely — an angled, front-facing triangular peak silhouette (with a bright, high-contrast pale summit) rather than a true top-down/aerial shape, and too small and too uniform (one repeated icon) to read as a real range. Second, the water texture showed an obvious repeating pattern once tiled — initially misdiagnosed as a boundary-edge seam (a real, if minor, edge mismatch was measurable and a classic "offset and heal" fix — roll the tile by half its width/height so the old edge mismatch relocates to the center, then blur-blend it away there — did shrink that specific number, but made the *visible* result far worse: the heavy centered blur read as an obvious repeating smear once tiled, worse than the original seam). The actual cause turned out to be a big, soft, low-frequency light/dark blob baked into the source art itself (visible even in one single un-tiled copy of the texture) — almost certainly the model rendering "warm light glinting" as one large soft highlight streak rather than fine, evenly-distributed detail. Fixed by flattening that large-scale gradient out directly (subtract a heavily-blurred copy of the image from itself, add back the global mean — standard uneven-lighting correction) rather than editing the prompt further or touching tile boundaries at all.
    - **Both fixes needed real regenerated art, not just a prompt tweak** — the project owner generated 4 new top-down mountain massif images (name them `mountain-peak.png` through `mountain-peak-4.png`; `listMountainSpritePaths()` auto-detects any file matching that pattern, so adding a 5th or 6th needs no code change) and a new water texture. Each mountain placement now also rolls a random *shape* (`pickShapeVariant`) in addition to size/rotation, so a cluster of icons doesn't look copy-pasted. `buildTiledLayer` also gained a `varyTiles` option (used for both water and grain) that assigns each grid tile a random flip (identity/horizontal/vertical/both) — flips preserve true tile-edge seamlessness (a mirrored seamless tile is still seamless against an unmirrored one) while breaking up the exact-repeat look; on its own this didn't fix the water issue (the real cause was the baked-in light blob, not a lack of flips), but it's cheap insurance against the same class of problem recurring, kept for both textures.
    - **Mountain size/density needed four rounds of tuning after the new shapes went in, because per-cell count and icon size were the wrong levers entirely at first.** `MOUNTAIN_ICON_SIZES` first went from `[55,75,95]` to `[90,130,170]` (too small to read as a real range) — but 3 placements per cell at that size just crowded the shapes into an overlapping, visually noisy mass (all their internal ridge-line detail crisscrossing on top of itself), not a clean range. Bumping size *again* (`[160,220,280]`) while cutting to 2/cell, then to exactly 1/cell, made the overlap *worse* each time, not better — the real problem was that literally every elevated cell rolled a placement regardless of how close it sat to its neighbors, so bigger icons on unchanged cell density always meant more overlap. The actual fix was real minimum-distance (Poisson-disc-style) thinning: shuffle all elevated cells into a random visit order, and only accept a placement if it's at least `MOUNTAIN_MIN_SPACING` (logical px) from every already-accepted one — the first attempt at a spacing value (90) rejected too much and left visible gaps of bare color-banded fill showing through; 45 landed as the workable middle ground, with icon size at `[220,300,380]`. That combination fixed the overlap-mess problem, but left visible bare color-banded-fill patches between shapes, which the project owner asked to have closed — `MOUNTAIN_ICON_SIZES` was bumped a fourth time (`[280,370,460]`) *and* `MOUNTAIN_MIN_SPACING` brought back down (45 → 25), landing on solidly-covered, cohesive range shapes with no bare gaps. Still read as "a bit weird" once genuinely gap-free, though — packing that many copies of only 4 shapes densely enough to close every gap makes the repetition itself the tell. Rather than keep tuning placement density, the project owner asked for a **prompt** fix instead: a companion "Terrain Prompt Forge" Claude Artifact prompt revision (v4) now asks for whole large, sprawling mountain-*range-segment* images (multiple connected ridgelines/valleys/spurs filling most of the frame) rather than one relatively compact massif per image, so far fewer placements are needed to cover a real range — regeneration pending as of this writing.
    - **Separately, the project owner asked to "blend the mountains together"** — with placements packed tightly enough to close every gap, each icon's own dark outline stroke was visibly cutting across the combined mass everywhere two placements overlapped, reading as distinct stamped shapes butted together rather than one continuous range. Fixed in `buildIconVariants` (mountains only, via a new `featherAlphaSigma` parameter trees don't use): blur the alpha channel at each icon's outer edge before caching it, so overlapping icons cross-fade into each other through `blitInto`'s existing partial-alpha blending instead of one icon's hard edge stamping flatly over another's interior. A first attempt at this blurred alpha only and produced solid black rectangles — every fully-transparent pixel is stored as RGB `(0,0,0)` (meaningless, un-premultiplied color), so once alpha-only blur gave those pixels partial opacity, they blended toward pure black instead of the sprite's real edge color. Fixed by premultiplying RGB by alpha, blurring all 4 channels together, then unpremultiplying — the standard way to blur something with transparency without black fringing. The feather amount itself also needed tuning down: `6px` softened the *entire* mass (at this placement density, most of the visible area is within a few px of some overlap), landing on `2.5px` as enough to smooth the worst seams while keeping ridge-line detail legible.
    - **All of the above — discrete icon placements, minimum-distance thinning, per-instance alpha feathering — is now superseded.** After the feathering fix, the project owner asked the more direct question: could mountains just be **layered** together the same way water is? They can, via `buildBlendedMountainLayer`, which replaced the entire discrete-placement system (`mountainPlacements`, `MOUNTAIN_ICON_SIZES`, `MOUNTAIN_MIN_SPACING`, `pickShapeVariant` are all gone from the code now — `buildSVG` returns a plain `mountainMaskSVG` elevation mask instead). Unlike water's source images (opaque JPEGs meant to tile edge-to-edge), mountain PNGs are irregular shapes on real transparent backgrounds, so the technique needed to be alpha-aware: each of the 4 shape images tiles across the *entire* canvas independently (own random offset + flip, reusing `buildOffsetTiledCanvas`), and the blend only lets a layer compete for a pixel it actually covers (its sharpened weight is zeroed wherever it's transparent there) — color blends among whichever layers *do* cover a given point, alpha stays fully opaque wherever at least one does, transparent only where none do. The result gets a light overall edge feather (reusing the same premultiply/blur/unpremultiply technique as the discrete icons did, factored out into standalone `featherAlphaEdges`) and is clipped to the real mountain-elevation cells via `mountainMaskSVG` with `dest-in` — same masking idiom `grainMasked` already used against `landPng`.
      - **A real bug produced solid black wedges through the mountain mass on the first attempt**: `buildOffsetTiledCanvas`'s base canvas was created with `channels:3` even when a transparent (`alpha:0`) background was requested for the 4-channel mountain path — sharp silently ignores an alpha value on a canvas declared with only 3 channels, so every gap between/around the tiled shapes composited as solid opaque black instead of staying transparent. Fixed by actually creating the canvas with `channels:4` (not just putting alpha in the background object) whenever alpha needs to be preserved.
      - **Once that was fixed, close-up blending looked seamless, but the full zoomed-out map showed a faint but real repeating diagonal pattern** — every layer shared the same tile size, so even with independent offsets and flips, all 4 layers shared one underlying repeat *period*, and the combined result still showed that period's grid structure at a small enough scale to see it. Fixed by giving each layer its own randomized tile size (aspect ratio kept matching the source sprites' own ~1.86, only the scale varies, `0.75x-1.5x` of a base size) — four genuinely different periods overlaid don't share a common repeat interval, so there's no combined pattern left for the eye to catch, the mountain-tuning equivalent of the water fix (which needed color/tone-matching, a structurally different problem, but the same underlying lesson: check the *full* zoomed-out view, not just a close crop, before calling a tiling technique done).
      - **A separate, real jaggedness remained even after both of the above**: the project owner spotted a stair-stepped, zigzag cutoff at the outer boundary between rock and grass in a screenshot. The blended texture's own edges were genuinely smooth (`featherAlphaEdges` handles that) — the jaggedness was coming from `mountainMaskSVG` itself, a raw fill of the actual mountain-elevation cell *polygons*, used completely unblurred as the `dest-in` mask. Since mountain-elevation is a per-cell yes/no classification, the boundary between elevated and non-elevated cells necessarily traces individual Voronoi cell edges exactly — the same category of problem the coastline had before its own distance-based softening. However smooth the texture inside that boundary was, clipping it with a crisp polygon-edge mask reimposed a hard, angular cutoff right at the edge. Fixed with one line: blur the rasterized mask itself (`sigma=18`) before using it as the `dest-in` alpha, so the elevation cutoff fades naturally instead of stair-stepping.
    - **The shore-to-open-ocean water fade also needed to come back more visible** once the real wave texture was blended in on top of it — the project owner asked for the shallow water right at a coastline to clearly read before fading into deeper ocean, and it had gotten too subtle to notice. Two knobs, both already in place from the original painterly-render build, got adjusted rather than anything new being built: `bandThresholds` (the raw distance-from-shore cutoffs `buildWaterBandRaster` interpolates `WATER_BANDS` across) widened back out from `[5,11,19,30]` to `[10,22,38,60]` — it had been pulled in tight earlier in the project specifically because the shallow band covered *too much* area at the wider setting, back when the water was a flat color fill with no texture riding on top; now that a busy wave texture draws the eye on top of it, the color band itself needs more room to actually register. `waterTextureStrength` (how strongly that wave texture blends over the band-color base) was also brought down slightly, `0.55` → `0.4`, so the band's own color shift shows through more instead of getting visually crowded out by ripple detail.
    - **The wider color band alone still wasn't quite what the project owner meant, though — the ask was for a genuinely different *texture* right at the coast** (sandy/mottled shallow water, not just a lighter tint of the same wave pattern), fading into the open-ocean texture set farther out. `assets/map-sprites/` now has a real role split: `water-texture*.jpg` are "deep" open-ocean textures, `water-shallow*.jpg` (one image so far — the sandy/mottled one from the same batch, renamed from `water-texture-4.jpg`) are shore textures. `buildWaterBandRaster` now also returns a raster-resolution "shallow weight" field derived from the exact same distance-from-land data its color bands already use (1 right at the coast, fading to 0 by the last band threshold — so the texture transition lines up with the color transition, not some separately-tuned zone), and `buildBlendedWaterLayer` builds the deep set's multi-layer blend and the shallow set's separately (via a new shared `buildMultiLayerBlend` helper — trivial for a single-image "set", same sharpened-weight-field technique as before for a multi-image one), then mixes the two per-pixel using that shallow-weight field. `listWaterSpritePaths()` split into `listWaterDeepPaths()`/`listWaterShallowPaths()` to match.
    - **The conifer tree icon had the exact same "top-down instruction, side-view shape" mistake mountains started with, caught independently by the project owner testing the prompt directly.** v1's prompt asked for a top-down view but described the shape as a "tall, narrow triangular silhouette" — a shape that only exists looking at a tree from the side — so the generator kept drawing a front-view Christmas-tree silhouette regardless of the stated viewpoint. Fixed the same way as the mountain prompt: describe what a conifer canopy *actually* looks like from directly above (a pointed, radiating star/pinwheel of spikes fanning out from the trunk) instead of reusing a side-view-coded silhouette description. The regenerated art came back as 3 distinct star/pinwheel shapes (one landed on a non-magenta background despite the prompt — keyed out fine anyway by sampling that image's own corner pixel as the background color instead of assuming `#ff00ff`), so conifer trees now pool multiple shapes the same way mountains do: `listConiferSpritePaths()` auto-detects any `tree-conifer*.png`, and each conifer placement rolls a random shape via a re-added `pickShapeVariant` (removed earlier when mountains stopped needing per-instance shape picking, needed again here since trees still use discrete icon placement, unlike mountains' full-canvas blend). Deciduous trees keep a single fixed shape — only the one good image exists for that role.
    - **Water needed more than one flattened image to stop reading as an obvious repeat**, even with the light-blob fix and tile flips — a single source tile, however clean, still tiles as a single source tile. The project owner generated 4 more water images (one had its own large-scale radial vignette — the same flatten-lighting fix applied to all 4 before use). A first attempt at combining them assigned one whole source image per grid *tile* (mirroring the mountain multi-shape approach — `listWaterSpritePaths()` auto-detects any `water-texture.jpg`/`water-texture-2.jpg`/etc.), color-matched to a shared per-channel mean first (each was generated independently and landed at a different overall tone, which on its own showed up as a literal rectangular grid of visible tile boundaries once combined, even though each image was individually clean).

      - A first version of this went very wrong: the composited canvas came back from sharp as 4 channels (RGBA) even though `create({channels:3})` was requested (confirmed directly — `info.channels` was 4, `hasAlpha` true), so the 3-channel stride math reading it back out silently read misaligned bytes, and blending 5 independently-scrambled layers together averaged out to a flat, textureless grey ocean. Fixed with an explicit `.removeAlpha()` before `.raw()`.

Fixed by raising each weight to a high power (`WEIGHT_POWER = 10`) before normalizing, which sharpens the contest so whichever layer is locally highest dominates strongly over a broad area (regions of real texture, with a smooth, not hard-edged, handoff to a different dominant layer elsewhere) instead of an even blur of all five.

- **Procedurally generated per-game kingdoms/borders — idea only, raised by the project owner, refined but not scoped.** Today's kingdom identities, names, and territory come straight from the Azgaar export baked into `travel-graph.json` (`graph.burgs[id].state`/`.race`, see [data-files.md](data-files.md)) — the same political map every character starts from (each character's own *war/conquest progress* is already independent, per [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s Persistence section, but the starting borders/kingdom names themselves are fixed). The idea: reassign which *area* of the map each kingdom controls fresh per new character, so replaying feels different each time, while keeping settlement names and the kingdom-name pool exactly as they are today — nothing needs inventing:
  - **Settlement names never change** — already true today, and stays true; only *which kingdom* a settlement belongs to would vary.
  - **Kingdom names are a fixed, reusable pool, not generated per game** — the same ~24 names (now race-appropriately titled, see [data-files.md](data-files.md)'s note on the Ork/Goblin kingdom rename) get reassigned to different territory each game, rather than needing a name generator. Avoids the current design's problem of a figure ending up in territory that no longer matches them once borders move.
  - **The still-unsolved, genuinely large part**: real settlement/race assignment has to come from *contiguous* region-growing (a flood-fill-style clustering by alliance, similar in spirit to `buildBurgAdjacency`'s own BFS) rather than per-settlement randomness, or the Good/Bad frontier the whole war system leans on would come out as an incoherent checkerboard instead of a real front line. This is the one piece that's still real, unscoped work — everything else above is now a naming/reassignment problem, not a generation problem.
  - Whatever shape this takes, **war must stay restricted to the Good alliance (human/dwarf) vs. the Bad alliance (ork/goblin) exclusively** — never human vs. dwarf or ork vs. goblin — which is already how `ALLIANCE`/the relations-matrix pair loop in `runFactionAITick` works today (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)) and would need to carry over unchanged into any regenerated map.
  - **Dwarves should always end up controlling wherever the mountains are, whatever the region-growing algorithm above assigns elsewhere** — per the project owner, prompted directly by the one-off fix that reassigned today's 17 mountain-sitting settlements to Dwarf kingdoms (see [data-files.md](data-files.md)) after noticing several had ended up Human/Ork/Goblin purely by coincidence of the fixed map's original export. A future per-game generator should treat mountain-elevation terrain as a hard constraint on Dwarf territory assignment, not leave it to chance the way today's one fixed map did.
  - **A related but much bigger idea, raised in the same conversation and explicitly NOT scoped**: making the terrain itself (mountains, coastlines, biomes, rivers — the whole physical geography) vary per game, not just which kingdom controls a fixed geography. Today's terrain is entirely static, baked into `game-map.json`'s one Azgaar export (see [data-files.md](data-files.md)) — every character plays the same physical world, with only *political* control varying under the idea above. Actually varying the terrain would mean generating real new geography per character (running Azgaar itself procedurally, or building a custom terrain generator to replace it) rather than reassigning ownership of a fixed one — a fundamentally bigger undertaking than the region-growing problem already flagged as unscoped above, closer to replacing the world generator entirely than extending this idea.

### Settlements

- **Unique per-settlement art** (a generated picture per visited settlement, replacing the four generic tier illustrations in `TIER_ART` — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)), with graduated variants reacting to war state rather than a single fixed look per tier: a **war/border variant** (fortifications, banners, visible unease) for a settlement whose kingdom is at war *and* sits near enemy-controlled territory — needs the border-detection concept above to know which settlements actually qualify as "on the border" — and a **destroyed/raided variant** for after a settlement actually changes hands. Same tier + state combination should render consistently everywhere that settlement's art shows up (the location-view corner box today, and `showLocationView`'s subtitle already has separate `(captured)` text that a matching art variant would reinforce visually rather than duplicate).
- **Settlement prosperity**, distinct from the population number that already exists — population currently only feeds war attrition/refugees/army strength (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)); Marketplace pricing still only reacts to kingdom-wide war/peace, not a per-settlement prosperity figure.
- **Trade goods / arbitrage economy** — buy-low-sell-high goods (cloth, ore, grain) between settlements. `MARKET_ITEMS` prices are the same everywhere modulo the war multiplier; there's no per-settlement price variation to arbitrage.
- **Horse travel — done.** A purchasable `MARKET_ITEMS` entry (weight `0`, not equippable, just carried like Bedroll) that cuts road-travel `hours` by `HORSE_PACE_MULT` (1.5×) in `computeTravel` — see [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md)'s "Horse and ferry fares" section. Doesn't speed up a sea crossing.

### Character creation & race

- **Character creation at new-game — starting city choice done, race/origin still not built.** characters.html's Create New Character form now includes a **Starting City** picker (`populateStartCitySelect()`) — every Human-race settlement of tier `city` or `capital` from `travel-graph.json` (15 today), capitals sorted first, Bary preselected to match the old hardcoded single start. `freshCharacterData(name, burgId)` threads the choice through. Still not built: choosing a race/origin that determines starting stats and which alliance (Good/Bad) you begin aligned with — "Human" is still the only race a character can ever be, and this picker is deliberately Human-only per the project owner (a fresh character never starts in Dwarf/Ork/Goblin territory), so it doesn't get you any closer to that part of this idea.
- **Race-specific traits** — the tracker's Races data models Human/Dwarf as "stronger equipment, better tactics" and Ork/Goblin as "multiply more quickly," none of which is wired into any gameplay system yet (this would presumably hook into the same Strength/Agility/Intelligence system in the Skills & Progression section above, e.g. as starting stat bonuses).
- **Religion per faction** — lore-only idea, no gameplay hook proposed yet.
- **Difficulty setting — permadeath — done, all three toggles.** Raised by the project owner while scoping mercenary combat (see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Party in combat"), built as a choice made once, at character creation (characters.html's create form, alongside the Starting City picker) — three independent checkboxes rather than one global switch, defaulting to exactly the game's original pre-this-feature behavior (player off, companions/mercenaries on) so no existing character's play changes silently:
  - **Player** — on makes losing a fight game over: `endCombat`'s `'defeat'` branch sets `isDead` on the character (instead of the old unconditional `setHealth(10)` safety net), explicitly awaits one save (unlike the routine keepalive-backed autosave elsewhere — this one-time write is worth the wait), then redirects to characters.html after a beat to read the toast. A dead character's card there shows a "Deceased" badge and no Play button — `playCharacter()` also refuses on the data itself as defense in depth. Off keeps today's behavior exactly (`setHealth(10)`, keep playing).
  - **Companions** — no longer inert: companion combat participation shipped (see "Party / Companions" above and [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Party in combat"), so `permadeathCompanions` (on by default) now gates `allyFalls()` for a recruited companion exactly the way `permadeathMercenaries` already did for mercenaries. Locking the field in early meant this didn't need its own snapshot-shape migration once combat participation actually shipped.
  - **Mercenaries** — on matches the unconditional behavior mercenary combat already shipped with (see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Party in combat"); off is the new case, and lets an easier difficulty turn that off instead of ripping the mechanic out: `mercenaryFalls()` now only removes a mercenary from the roster permanently when this is on — off, they're merely knocked out of the rest of that one fight and roster back in fully healed next time, same as the player's own no-permadeath default.
  Four new snapshot fields (`isDead`, `permadeathPlayer`, `permadeathCompanions`, `permadeathMercenaries`) — see [characters.md](characters.md). Read-only after creation; settings.html shows the chosen values (`#current-difficulty`) so a player can recall them, but there's no way to change them mid-playthrough, deliberately — it's a character-creation choice, not a live setting.

### Hero screen — awaiting a design conversation with the project owner

**Character portrait — a picker, not just a frame.** The project owner raised choosing a character portrait from a list of already-generated art (presumably Character Forge's growing human/dwarf roster — see "Talkable NPCs" above for where that tracker is) as part of rethinking how the Hero screen (character.html) works overall, then asked to talk through the design before any of it gets built — nothing here is scoped yet, deliberately. Distinct from the "Character portrait frame + rune border"/"Character portrait sprites" bullets in the UI Theme section below, which are about styling an empty placeholder slot as part of the blue/gold reskin, not about letting a player actually pick a portrait. Open questions for that conversation: which portraits are selectable (all of Character Forge's roster, or a curated subset), whether the choice is made at character creation or changeable later from the Hero screen itself, and how it interacts with the still-unbuilt race/origin picker above (a race choice would presumably narrow which portraits are offered).

### Combat

- **Pre-fight story beat — done.** Every fight (`startCombat`, the shared entry point every current trigger goes through — ambushes and Defend the Walls, see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)) now opens with one line from `COMBAT_INTRO_LINES`, shown above the existing mechanical "X blocks your path!"/"N enemies block your path!" line in the combat log — a small, scene-agnostic narrative beat so a fight reads as a moment in the player's own story rather than just a stat check. `triggerSiegeDefenseFight` appends its own siege-specific line rather than overwriting the log, so the beat survives there too.
- **Status effects** — bleed, stun, or fatigue as modifiers layered on top of the existing turn-based Attack/Specials/Flee loop (see [combat.md](combat.md)).
- **Combat environment backgrounds — plumbing built, one biome wired in; the rest still needs art.** `#combat-view` can now show a real illustrated scene behind the enemy portrait cards instead of its flat background — see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Environment backdrop" for the actual mechanism (`setCombatBackdrop`, threaded through `startCombat`/`triggerEnemyFight`/`triggerAmbushFight` as an optional `backdrop` argument). Forest (`FOREST_ART`, three images under `assets/biomes/`) is the only one with real art so far, standing in for `triggerAmbushFight`'s plain `'road'` context; the "Combat Backdrops" section of the Scene Backdrops tracker artifact (ask for its link if it's not at hand) tracks prompts for the rest — Plains/Hills/Mountains/Desert/Swamp/Snow/Coast, plus the "Night Camp" and "Under Siege" context cards, none generated yet. Two things this still needs before it covers every fight:
  - *True biome selection* — the live game still has no biome/terrain concept pulled through anywhere; `travel-graph.json` doesn't carry it, and while the raw `assets/game-map.json` Azgaar export does (see [data-files.md](data-files.md)), nothing currently reads it at combat time. Until that data pipeline exists, Forest stays a stand-in for "ambush on the road" regardless of the settlement's actual terrain, rather than a real per-biome pick.
  - *The other contexts* — `'camp'` (Night Camp) and `triggerSiegeDefenseFight` (Under Siege) both still start every fight with no backdrop at all; each needs its own art generated and a one-line change at its `triggerEnemyFight`/`startCombat` call site once it exists, same shape as Forest's own wiring.

### Open design question worth flagging

The tracker's "Skills & Combat" tab describes an older, different idea: a Skyrim/Bannerlord-style skill list trained by *using* it (One-handed Weapons, Two-handed Weapons, Bow & Arrow, Medium/Heavy Armour, Healing, Horse Riding, Trade/Charisma, Cooking/Leadership), with an explicit open question of learn-everything vs. a capped build. That predates and doesn't match the Strength/Agility/Intelligence + skill-point perks/moves system now actually built (see the Skills & Progression section above). Worth a decision from the project owner on whether that older idea is superseded, or meant as a second, complementary "proficiency" layer to add later alongside stats/perks.

### Living world (large, mostly unscoped)

Still just one-line ideas in the tracker, no design decisions made on any of these: marriage/romance, NPC memory (reacting differently based on player actions — ties into the Talkable NPCs idea above), a reputation system, character death with some form of legacy, and NPC children existing in the world. Item rarity/tiers is already tracked above under Equipment (a Sword is always +4, forever). Sieges/garrisons and a pairwise kingdom relations matrix are now built — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md); that file's own "Known simplifications" section tracks what's still cut from that system specifically (no garrison-vs-attacker battle simulation beyond a strength-ratio roll, no diplomatic memory behind Negotiate Peace).

One thing the tracker records as **already decided**, not just an idea: the player is meant to grow strong but never able to solo-topple a city — a support-role cap on player power, worth keeping in mind whenever combat or army-strength numbers get revisited.

## Future ideas from AI-assisted brainstorming (2026-09-09, not from the dev tracker)

Everything below came out of a brainstorming pass with an AI assistant, not `Goblin_War_Tracker.xlsx` — kept in its own section rather than folded into "Future ideas from the dev tracker" above so this file stays honest about where each idea actually came from. Same status as that section otherwise: pure brainstorm, nothing scoped, nothing with a code stub.

### World map & travel

- **Fog of war** — unvisited regions of the map render darkened/hazy until the player has actually traveled near them, rather than the whole map (all settlements, roads, terrain) being visible from a brand-new game. Would need a persisted "revealed" mask keyed off logical-space position, checked and grown as travel updates the player's position — see [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md) for where that position tracking already lives.
- **Off-road landmarks/ruins** — optional points of interest away from the settlement/road network entirely (distinct from the "Points-of-interest markers" idea above, which is about waypoints *on* known routes), each a small self-contained risk/reward beat: a fight, a piece of lore, a one-off loot find. Reaching one would need its own off-road travel affordance, since today's travel is always settlement-to-settlement along `travel-graph.json`'s road/sea graph.
- **Contested bridges/fords** — a chokepoint edge in the road graph that gets toll-taxed or closed outright while the kingdom controlling it is at war, forcing a detour through `buildAdjacency()`'s pathfinding rather than the shortest route always being available. Would tie into the same war-state checks `getBuyPrice`/`isAtWarAt` already do for Marketplace pricing (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)).
- **Paid ferry crossings — done, as a fare on every sea leg rather than only the widest gaps.** `computeTravel` derives `ferryCost` from the route's own sea mileage (`routeBetween`'s new `seaMiles`) and `beginTravel` gates on it — a real affordability gate, unlike Hunger/Thirst's deliberately-never-blocking design — see [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md)'s "Horse and ferry fares" section.

### Settlements & economy

- **Blacksmith — done, via a 4-tier item catalog rather than a per-instance upgrade.** [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s "The Blacksmith" — City/Capital only, a near-duplicate of `showMarketPanel` selling only `shop:'blacksmith'`-tagged `MARKET_ITEMS`: the Steel and Mithril tier of each core Equipment slot, Mithril further gated to Capitals only (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s quality ladder). The idea originally here was upgrading an *already-owned* item's `dmg`/`def` in place; that would have needed inventory items to carry individual identity instead of stacking by name/qty, a bigger rework than this ask called for, so a 4-tier ladder via four item names per slot shipped instead — the full answer to the Equipment section's "no rarity/tier variation" gap above, not just a smaller one.
- **Property ownership** — buy a house in a specific settlement as a permanent base, granting a free-rest perk there (skipping the Tavern's gold cost, though not necessarily its rumour) and maybe a personal storage chest beyond `CARRY_CAPACITY` (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)).
- **Palace loans** — borrow gold at a Capital against future Work income, with real interest accruing over `gameDay`s and a real consequence for defaulting (e.g. that kingdom's services refusing the player until repaid) — a new Palace card alongside the existing Petition for Troops/Negotiate Peace ones (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)).
- **Settlement specialties — a small, scoped slice done; the original idea's fuller version still not built.** `getSettlementDiscount` (index.html) discounts Waterskin at a port settlement and Food at a Village, off data `travel-graph.json` already carries (`b.port`/`b.tier`) — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md). Sits alongside the existing per-kingdom war-price multiplier, doesn't replace it. The original ask here (a mountain Town discounting armor-slot items) is still unbuilt: the live game has no elevation/biome data pulled through yet (same gap noted under "World map & travel" below), so a real mountain check isn't possible without that pipeline first.
- **Gear durability** — equipped items lose a small amount of `dmg`/`def` with combat use, needing an occasional repair fee at the now-real Blacksmith (see above) to restore — a new, ongoing gold sink beyond Food/Waterskin, since equipment today never degrades once bought.

### Combat

- **Named unique bosses** — a one-off chieftain/warlord `ENEMY_VARIANTS`-style entry with its own generated portrait, higher stats than any existing variant, and a guaranteed strong loot roll, offered as the target of a Notable Figure kill-quest (see [quests.md](quests.md)) rather than ever showing up from a random `pickAmbushEnemy` roll — a fight worth traveling for, not one that can just happen to you.
- **A real flee cost — done, exactly as sketched here.** `combatFlee` (see [combat.md](combat.md)) still always succeeds — no roll, no Stamina cost, so a player who's run out of Stamina can never be soft-locked into a fight — but `endCombat`'s `'flee'` branch now adds a flat Hunger/Thirst hit (`FLEE_HUNGER_PENALTY`/`FLEE_THIRST_PENALTY`) on top of the exertion every combat outcome already pays, routed through the same overflow-to-Health floor as everything else in [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section — so fleeing broke stings but never traps the player.

### Progression

- **Prestige / New Game+** — once a character can actually reach max level, let a fresh playthrough carry over a portion of banked skill points or a cosmetic title, rather than leveling being a purely one-shot, one-save arc.
- **Titles on the character sheet — a small, scoped slice done; the fuller Achievements-page idea below still isn't.** Three cosmetic titles (Bandit Hunter, Goblin Bane, Ork Slayer) now show on character.html next to identity once `goblinwar_kills` clears a threshold — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s "Titles" section. Still exactly what it was scoped to be: no stat effect, no per-title icon, no milestone list beyond these three — a lightweight layer distinct from the "Achievements page" idea below, not a first slice of it.
- **A "use it to level it" proficiency layer** — the "Open design question worth flagging" section above already notes the dev tracker's older Skyrim/Bannerlord-style skill-list idea predates and doesn't match the built Strength/Agility/Intelligence + perk-point system. One way to resolve that open question without discarding either idea: layer proficiency-by-use *on top of* the existing system rather than replacing it — e.g. landing enough Sword-equipped attacks in `combatAttack()` slowly unlocks a Swordsmanship perk, entirely additive to the Str/Agi/Int skill points already spent.

### Party & Companions

- **Hireable mercenaries — done, combat participation included now.** A Guard House action (`buildMercenaryHireHTML`/`hireMercenary`, index.html — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) hires from a small fixed `MERCENARY_TYPES` catalog (Hired Sellsword/Mercenary Archer/Veteran Guard) into a new `goblinwar_mercenaries` key, sharing `MAX_PARTY_SIZE` (3) with `goblinwar_party`. Upkeep is charged from `advanceDays(n)` rather than a per-settlement `hasWorkedToday`-style cooldown (that pattern fits a *place* you have to revisit; upkeep needed to keep accruing no matter where the player was) — `chargeMercenaryUpkeep()` bills every elapsed day in one lump sum whenever the calendar advances for any reason (travel, camping, resting), and drops any mercenary the player can't currently afford, with a toast naming who left. Deliberately anonymous per the project owner (no name, just a role) — but each role does have real generic portrait art now under `assets/mercenaries/` (faceless by design: full helm or a shadowed hood, never a face), keeping mercenaries visually and narratively distinct from the named, real-portrait `COMPANIONS` catalog. **Also done:** hired mercenaries fight alongside the player in `combat` — see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Party in combat" — dealing damage automatically each round and able to draw (and die permanently to) an enemy's counter-attack in the player's place.
- **Recruitable companions — recruiting, combat participation, and loyalty are all done now.** Per the project owner: "have them in random cities, but you have to talk to them to hire them — they give you their story and why they want to join, and you decide." Each `COMPANIONS` entry (index.html) is now pinned to a home settlement (`homeBurgId`, spread across different cities than `NOTABLE_FIGURES` — Thrain, a dwarf, in Dwarf territory rather than Human) with a new "A New Face" settlement action opening a conversation panel — their story (`blurb`) plus Recruit/Not Now, not an instant paid hire like a mercenary. See "Party / Companions" above for the full flow. Companions fight exactly like mercenaries do too (see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Party in combat") — same automatic attack-every-round/draw-counters mechanics, own `combatHp`/`combatDmgRange` per entry, and can die permanently gated by `permadeathCompanions`. Loyalty (next bullet) closes out the third and last remaining step this idea always had.
- **Companion loyalty — done.** Per the project owner: a paid mercenary is a simple cash transaction (leaves if upkeep goes unpaid, no feelings involved), but a *recruited* companion carries a real approval meter (`goblinwar_companionLoyalty`, `{companionId: 0-100}`, defaulting to 50 on recruit) nudged by player choices, shown on party.html as a bucketed mood label (Devoted/Content/Wary/Resentful/Hostile) plus a bar. `adjustPartyLoyalty(delta)` (index.html) is the one function every loyalty-moving action calls — every currently-recruited companion shifts by the same amount (no per-companion personality yet, a possible future refinement), and hitting 0 removes that companion from `goblinwar_party` for good: a new departure path distinct from combat death, untouched by `permadeathCompanions` (that toggle only gates dying *in* a fight). Fed from both directions: Aid the Refugees and Negotiate Peace nudge it up; Raid a Village, Incite War, and a world event's uncaring choice (see "World events" below) nudge it down, scaled so a single deliberate act (a raid, inciting a whole war) costs more than one moment of roadside apathy.
- **Raid a Village — done.** The direct opposite of Aid the Refugees, per the project owner: a new base-tier settlement action (`buildRaidVillageHTML`/`acceptRaidVillage`, index.html — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) available at any settlement, any tier, once per day per settlement (same cooldown shape as Work). Pays gold scaled by the settlement's tier, at the cost of a real population hit to it (`setPopulation`, a flat ~3%) and a loyalty hit to the whole party.
- **Incite War — done.** The direct opposite of Negotiate Peace, and the sharpest "helping bad characters" act in the game: a third Palace card (`generateInciteWarOffer`/`acceptInciteWar`, alongside Petition for Troops and Negotiate Peace — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s "Player involvement") that pays the player to provoke a war between the home kingdom and an opposite-alliance kingdom currently at peace with it, using the same strength-ratio math Negotiate Peace prices a truce with, just inverted (a stronger target pays better rather than costing more) — and the single biggest loyalty penalty in the game.
- **World events — done, a small first pass.** A new, independent roll on arrival alongside the existing ambush roll (`rollWorldEvent`/`triggerWorldEvent`/`resolveWorldEvent`, index.html — only checked when the ambush roll already missed, so at most one interruption fires per trip), each picking a random `WORLD_EVENTS` entry and showing a two-choice modal (`#world-event-modal`, structurally a copy of `#whats-new-modal`). Five vignettes to start (a wounded traveler, a merchant under attack by bandits, a begging family, a burning farmstead with someone trapped, a shepherd's flock under attack), each a genuine help-or-walk-away choice trading a small gold/Health/Stamina cost for loyalty and XP, or a small gold gain for a loyalty hit — this is where "letting an NPC come to harm" actually lives in the game now. Plain data (one more array entry, no other code changes to add a sixth), not the fuller "festivals/caravans" world-events brainstorm still listed under "World map & travel" below — this is scoped narrowly to feeding loyalty's negative side, not a general random-encounter system.
- **Companion quests** — one small personal quest per recruited (non-mercenary) companion, reusing the existing Deliver/Kill quest turn-in architecture [quests.md](quests.md) already documents rather than inventing a new quest family.

### Faction war & quests

- **Kingdom elimination — needs real design work, sketched below rather than fully scoped.** Today a kingdom keeps existing in the war/relations matrix forever regardless of how many of its settlements have been captured — `runFactionAITick` still rolls war/peace and siege chances for it every week, and `WAR_ATTRITION_RATE` still bleeds population from its *originally*-owned settlements (`b.state`) even ones it no longer controls, per [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s own "Known simplifications". The idea: once every settlement whose `b.state` is kingdom X has been captured (`getController(burgId) !== b.race` for all of them), treat X as eliminated —
  - Skip it entirely in `runFactionAITick`'s per-kingdom attrition/siege loop and its relations-matrix pair rolls (nothing left to attack from or defend), rather than continuing to simulate a kingdom with zero territory.
  - Give it a distinct one-time announcement, the same way `maybeRunFactionAI()` already summarizes captures in a toast — "the Warg Horde has fallen" reads as a bigger moment than an ordinary settlement-capture toast and probably deserves different treatment (a modal, not just a toast).
  - Its captured settlements simply stay with their conquerors — no separate "kingdom is gone, territory reverts to neutral" step, consistent with `setController` already being one-way with no restore-original-owner action.
  - Open questions this doesn't answer yet: whether an eliminated kingdom's `NOTABLE_FIGURES` entries (if not already gone via their home settlement being captured) should be explicitly retired too; whether losing a whole kingdom should shift `getArmyStrength`-based numbers for the rest of its alliance (one less Bad-alliance kingdom presumably makes the Good alliance's overall position stronger, but nothing today aggregates strength above the single-kingdom level); and whether elimination should ever be reversible if the procedurally-generated-territory idea (see "World map & travel" above) ever lands and a new game redraws the map from scratch anyway.
- **Per-kingdom reputation — all four payoffs are done now.** A second, independent standing number from companion loyalty (`goblinwar_reputation`, `{kingdomName: -100 to 100}`, `adjustReputation`/`getReputation`/`reputationLabel` in index.html — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md) for the full write-up), tracked per kingdom rather than per companion, separate from the live war/peace relation itself (which that file's "Known simplifications" already flags as having no memory of its own). Fed by the same actions that already move companion loyalty — delivery/Notable Figure quest turn-ins, Aid the Refugees, Negotiate Peace, Petition for Troops, a Defend the Walls victory (all gains), Raid a Village and Incite War (losses) — but crediting the *specific* kingdom each action actually concerns rather than every companion at once.
  - **Information — done.** A `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_TRUSTED` pool concats onto `getTownsfolkLines`' existing pool whenever that settlement's controller sits at Friendly or Trusted reputation, the exact same "concat on top" mechanism `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_CAPTURED` already used for a captured settlement.
  - **Equipment — done.** `getReputationDiscount(burgId)` folds into `getBuyPrice` as one more multiplicative factor alongside `getSettlementDiscount`/the `silver_tongue` perk (Trusted 0.85× down to Hostile 1.15×) — since `showBlacksmithPanel` already prices through the same function, both the Marketplace and the Blacksmith got the discount for free, no extra wiring.
  - **Display — done.** Guard House's stats card (`buildGuardHouseStats`) shows a plain "{kingdom}'s opinion of you" row alongside its existing population/army numbers — no new page needed.
  - **Troops — done.** `runFactionAITick()`'s new third pass (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s "The weekly tick") lets a `Trusted`, currently-peaceful kingdom reinforce its own most-threatened ally unprompted, `TRUSTED_REINFORCEMENT_CHANCE` (35%) chance per week — reusing Petition for Troops' exact `findAllyNeedingReinforcement`/`setReinforcement`/`getArmyStrength` call, just tick-triggered instead of player-triggered. Deliberately silent (no toast), consistent with the tick's own "captures only" convention.
  - **War help — done.** `advanceSiege` multiplies a `Trusted` defending kingdom's effective strength by `TRUSTED_DEFENSE_BONUS` (1.15×) before computing that tick's attacker/defender ratio — its own garrison holds a little tougher in the background. Deliberately doesn't touch `startCombat`/`triggerSiegeDefenseFight` at all, so the player's own Defend the Walls fight is completely unaffected — keeping the project's own "player can grow strong but never solo-topple a city" constraint intact from the other direction (a kingdom fighting its own wars better, not the player fighting an easier one).
  Very low standing can already turn a Marketplace/Blacksmith visit pricier via the discount above; a hard Guard House lockout at very low standing is a possible small follow-up, not built.
- **War exhaustion** — the longer a specific war relation has been active, the more it passively pressures both kingdoms toward `WAR_PEACE_CHANCE`'s roll succeeding (or a growing bonus stacked on top of it), so a war doesn't just run indefinitely at the same flat 15%-per-week chance to end — giving the player's own Negotiate Peace option a real ticking clock it's racing against rather than being the only thing that ever ends a war.

### Living world

- **Aging effects** — character.html already tracks age (currently just displayed, no gameplay hook). Crossing age thresholds over a long playthrough could trade off small stats — e.g. reduced Stamina regen, increased Intelligence — rather than age being purely cosmetic forever.
- **Seasons** — a slow calendar-driven cycle (independent of, and slower than, the "Day/night cycle" idea above) tinting the world-raster/biome art and swapping in seasonal settlement flavor lines (a harvest-festival line pool in autumn, etc.) — a longer-timescale companion to weather rather than a replacement for it.
- **Reactive rumors — two slices done now (Tavern Talk's event log, and generated lines in Talk to Townsfolk/Work); the fuller migration this idea originally described is still not built.** Per the project owner, the end state isn't just adding reactive lines alongside the static pools — it's `RUMOURS`/`TOWNSFOLK_LINES_*`/`WORK_LINES` eventually going away *entirely*, replaced by lines generated from real faction-AI state so every flavor line the player reads is describing something that's actually true in their specific game, not a fixed pool of generic color. That's still not what's built: every static pool below is still fully intact, only enriched with lines concatenated on top — no consumer has actually been replaced by a generator function yet.
  - **Tavern Talk — done (previous slice).** A small persisted `goblinwar_recentEvents` log (`recordWorldEvent`/`getRecentEventTexts`, index.html, capped at `RECENT_EVENTS_MAX` = 15, trim-oldest — same shape as `goblinwar_completedQuests`) records two real outcomes as they happen — a settlement actually captured (`runFactionAITick`'s capture branch) and a siege actually repelled (`cancelSiegesBetween`) — and `showTavernPanel` picks from `RUMOURS.concat(getRecentEventTexts())`.
  - **Talk to Townsfolk and Work — done.** `getReactiveTownsfolkLines(burgId)`/`getReactiveWorkLines(burgId)` (index.html) generate lines that name the actual kingdoms involved rather than picking from a fixed pool: Talk to Townsfolk covers an active siege (names the attacker), being at war (names the actual enemy/enemies, via `getEnemiesAtWar`), sitting on the frontline (names the bordering enemy kingdom, via the new `getFrontlineEnemyKingdom` — `isFrontlineBurg` is now just that reduced to a boolean), a captured settlement (names both the original and current kingdom), and a kingdom currently benefiting from Alliance Reinforcement (names who sent the troops, via the new `getReinforcementSource`). Work covers the same siege/war/frontline three, scoped down from Talk to Townsfolk's fuller set since a captured-settlement or reinforcement line reads oddly as "why you got paid to do chores" flavor. Both concatenate onto the existing static pools (`getTownsfolkLines`/`WORK_LINES` respectively) rather than replacing them.

### Meta / UX

- **Achievements page — done, via a Settings menu item rather than a toolbar tab.** [achievements.html](../achievements.html) (see [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md)) is the dedicated completionist checklist this idea asked for: every Capital visited, every `ENEMY_VARIANTS` entry defeated at least once, cumulative kill/quest-completion/level milestones — five categories, each card a progress bar plus an `X / Y` count. Three new cumulative-only `localStorage` keys back the two checklist categories that had no tracking yet (`goblinwar_visitedBurgs`, `goblinwar_defeatedVariants`, `goblinwar_questsCompletedTotal` — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)); the milestone categories (kills, level) reuse data that already existed. Stays separate from the cosmetic Titles line on character.html, per the original idea — both happen to read `goblinwar_kills`, but neither depends on the other.
- **Colorblind-friendly stat pills — done, via text labels rather than a new icon/palette.** Each of the four `.stat-pill`s in `#player-stats` already used a distinct icon shape (heart/bolt/hunger icon/droplet); a small `.stat-tag` label (`HP`/`STA`/`HNG`/`THR`) was added alongside each icon so meaning never depends on shape or hue recognition at all — the most robust fix, not just an alternate palette. See index.html's `#player-stats` markup.
- **Stale-save warning — done, matching this idea's own design.** settings.html now flags it (a warning banner above the current-character card) if the Supabase `characters` row's `updated_at` is meaningfully newer than `goblinwar_localSyncedAt`, a new device-local marker this browser updates on every successful load/save — see [characters.md](characters.md)'s "Stale-save warning" section for the full mechanic.
- **Party tab specifically still felt slow after the fix above — a second, unrelated root cause, also found and fixed.** Per the project owner, Party kept feeling slow even after navigation itself sped up. Profiling on a throttled ~1.5Mbps connection (closer to real mobile conditions than a fast dev machine) with a full 3-mercenary party found the actual cause: `assets/party/`'s and `assets/mercenaries/`'s portrait PNGs were imported at the same 600px-longest-edge convention `assets/enemies/` uses (270-390KB each), even though neither ever renders larger than a 64px thumbnail anywhere in the game (unlike enemy art, shown up to 210px in combat) — three of those loading at once took **~4.9 seconds** on that throttled connection. Downscaled all 6 existing files (3 companions, 3 mercenaries) to a 200px longest edge instead (34-47KB each, comfortably crisp even at 3x device pixel density for a 64px box) — same scenario now loads in **~0.85 seconds**. See [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md) and the root [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)'s asset table for the updated convention; any future companion/mercenary art should be imported at 200px, not 600px.
- **Screen-to-screen navigation felt slow — root cause found and fixed.** Per the project owner, tapping a toolbar icon felt sluggish. Profiling (a scripted browser session against the real Supabase backend, not guesswork) showed the visible page itself was never the bottleneck — the actual cause was `goTo()` (index.html) `await`ing a full autosave PATCH to Supabase (several hundred ms to over a second, real network round trip) before the browser was even allowed to start navigating to the next page, a deliberate choice so the save couldn't be lost to the page unload. The fix keeps that same guarantee without the wait: [assets/auth-client.js](../assets/auth-client.js)'s shared client now sends every request with `keepalive:true` (the fetch spec's own mechanism for a request surviving the page that started it), so `goTo()` fires the save and navigates immediately — verified end-to-end that the save still lands. A second, smaller cost (the active-character existence re-check also run on every page load) is now skipped if it already succeeded within the last minute in the same tab (`sessionStorage`-cached, cleared on a real tab close) — see [accounts.md](accounts.md) for both fixes in full. Deliberately **not** touched: the `?_=` cache-busting on every internal link, which exists to fix a real, already-documented bug (see the root [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)'s Conventions section) — undoing that would trade this speed win for a worse, previously-shipped one.

## Ideas from the project owner's own handwritten notes (undated "TO DO FOR GW" list)

Everything below came from a photographed handwritten list, not `Goblin_War_Tracker.xlsx` or an AI brainstorming pass — kept in its own section per this file's usual practice of staying honest about where each idea actually came from. Pure brainstorm, nothing scoped, nothing with a code stub, same as the two sections above. One item on the original list — showing character age on the character screen — is skipped here because it's **already built**: `character.html`'s info card already has an `#age-value` row reading from `goblinwar_age` (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)).

### World map & travel

- **Further polish on the painterly world-raster render** — the shipped tree/mountain/water/grain art (see "World map rebuilt as a painterly render" above) still isn't where the project owner wants it: trees and mountains need another pass, and city markers need to actually read clearly at a normal zoom level (noted as a Gemini-generation task, presumably new/regenerated marker art rather than a code change). No specifics on what's wrong with each beyond "needs fixing" — worth a follow-up conversation on exactly what reads badly before generating anything.
- **Colored factions/borders on the map** — today's only territory-control visual is `buildFrontlineMarkers()`'s small ring on settlements actually bordering an enemy at war (see "War frontline tracker / territory border detection" above); there's no full political coloring (a tinted fill or border line per kingdom/race showing who controls what at a glance) anywhere in the SVG `#overlay`. Would need `buildBurgAdjacency()`'s existing border graph as the geometry, styled per kingdom rather than just flagged frontline/not.
- **Map terrain burns/degrades closer to the war** — distinct from the "Destroyed/raided art variant" idea already listed under Settlements above (which is about one settlement's own art after *it specifically* changes hands): this is about the surrounding terrain itself in `world-raster.jpg` reading more scorched/damaged the closer a region sits to active fighting, independent of whether any single settlement there has actually been captured yet. Would need some notion of "war intensity by region" derived from the existing frontline/siege data, then a render-time terrain treatment — a real addition to `render-painterly.js`'s pipeline, not a small tweak.
- **Visual port markers on the map** — settlements with `b.port` already get a cheaper Waterskin and a text note inside their location panel (`getSettlementDiscount`, see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s "Settlement specialties"), but nothing shows on the map itself that a given city/town sits on the water. A small icon overlay keyed off the same `b.port` flag, similar in spirit to the existing City/Capital tier markers (`assets/icons/Map/Cities.png`).

### Settlements & Locations

- **A game to play at the Tavern (chess or similar)** — the Tavern (see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)'s "The Tavern") currently offers Rest for the Night, Meal & Drink, and Tavern Talk; a playable minigame would be a fourth, structurally different action — no wagering/reward hooked up yet even as a concept, just the idea.

### Party / Companions

- **Talk to companions at the campfire** — `showCampView`'s camp screen (see [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md)) has no interaction with recruited companions today beyond them being along for the ride; this would be a small conversation beat at camp specifically, distinct from both the full "Talkable NPCs" system (still unscoped, see above) and a companion's one-time recruitment blurb. Probably the most natural first real use of the loyalty system's *reading* — a chance to see how a companion feels about the journey so far, not just a bar on party.html.

### Faction AI, Territory & War Economy

- **Incite War / Negotiate Peace need more weight — a real cost gate, not an instant action.** Both Palace actions resolve immediately today off a strength-ratio price roll (see "Per-kingdom reputation" and "Incite War"/"Negotiate Peace" above) — the ask is for real prerequisites before either is even offered: a minimum reputation/trust threshold with the kingdom in question (the reputation system this would gate on already exists and is fully wired, see above), and a multi-step flow with actual choices along the way ("choosing dialogue options") rather than one Accept button. The dialogue-options half of this overlaps with the still-unscoped "Talkable NPCs"/Hero screen ideas above — this is likely the same underlying need (a real conversation UI) showing up from a second direction.

### Meta / UX

- **Condense Settings' changelog to weekly summaries** — changelog.html (see [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)'s Conventions section) currently lists every individual `CHANGELOG` entry in full, and there are enough of them now (67 as of this writing) that the list is long. The idea: group entries by the week they shipped in settings.html/changelog.html's display, rather than changing how `CHANGELOG` itself is authored (still one entry per player-visible change, three-places-at-once, per the existing convention) — a presentation change over the existing array, not a data-model change.
- **Trust/loyalty/reputation changes need to be visually called out in the moment** — companion loyalty and per-kingdom reputation (both fully built and wired, see "Companion loyalty" and "Per-kingdom reputation" above) only ever show as a static bar/number the player has to go check on party.html or a Guard House visit; nothing calls out *that* a specific action just moved one (e.g. Raid a Village dropping loyalty, Aid the Refugees raising a kingdom's reputation) at the moment it happens. A toast or small floating "+Reputation"/"−Loyalty" indicator right when `adjustPartyLoyalty`/`adjustReputation` fires, similar in spirit to how gold/XP rewards already toast on combat victory.

### Character Creation & Race

- **A "Realistic" difficulty option where a character can die of old age** — ties into two already-built systems rather than needing new ones from scratch: the age-threshold stat-tradeoff idea already listed under Living World above (`goblinwar_age` is tracked and displayed, but has no gameplay hook yet — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)), and the existing three-checkbox permadeath difficulty picker on characters.html's create form (see "Difficulty setting — permadeath" above). A fourth toggle in that same family, gating a genuine old-age-death roll past some age threshold, rather than aging only ever trading off small stats.
