# Roadmap: Stubs Already Scaffolded in the Code

Every "coming soon" placeholder currently in the game, collected in one place so future work has a checklist instead of needing to grep for it. Each entry names the exact file/element so implementing it means replacing that stub, not guessing where it should live.

## Skills & Progression — character.html — in progress

**Built so far:** the whole loop is real. Combat victories grant XP (`ENEMY_VARIANTS`' `xpRange`, rolled in `endCombat`), leveling up grants a skill point plus a small Health/Stamina bump, and character.html lets a player spend each point on Strength/Agility/Intelligence, one of 9 passive perks, or one of 6 active special attacks — all sharing the same `goblinwar_skills` unlock storage and stat-threshold gating. The active moves show up as a "Specials" button in combat (`#combat-specials-panel`, `combatSpecial()` in index.html) alongside Attack/Flee, each usable once per fight. See [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s "Leveling & stats" for exactly where every perk and move hooks in.

The former naming collision is resolved: the active move is "Crushing Blow" (2x damage), distinct from the passive "Power Strike" perk (+2 flat damage, always on).

**Not built yet:** nothing — multi-enemy encounters (see below) closed the last gap here.

**Data model:** five new `localStorage` keys (see [player-state.md](player-state.md) once added) — `goblinwar_level` (default `1`), `goblinwar_xp` (default `0`), `goblinwar_skillPoints` (unspent points, default `0`), `goblinwar_stats` (`{str, agi, int}`, default all `0`), `goblinwar_skills` (`{skillId: true}` for unlocked perks/moves, default `{}`). All five need adding to the character snapshot shape in all three duplicated spots (index.html, settings.html, characters.html — see [characters.md](characters.md)) or leveling resets on save/switch.

XP curve: roughly `xpForLevel(n) = 40 * n^1.5`. Each `ENEMY_VARIANTS` entry (see [combat.md](combat.md)) gets an `xpReward` field alongside its existing `goldRange`, rolled in `endCombat('victory')` the same way gold is. Leveling up grants 1 skill point plus a small flat bump to `maxHealth`/`maxStamina` (e.g. +8/+4), so it feels good even before a point is spent.

**Passive perks** (spend a skill point, gated on a stat threshold so raw stats and perks stay entangled): Power Strike (Str, +2 flat dmg), Pack Mule (Str, +10 carry capacity — see `CARRY_CAPACITY` in [player-state.md](player-state.md)), Iron Skin (Str, −1 flat dmg taken), Evasion (Agi, 15% chance to fully dodge the enemy counter-attack in `combatAttack()`), Second Wind (Agi, 25% chance an attack costs no stamina), Sure Feet (Agi, −10% ambush chance, see `AMBUSH_CHANCE_PER_DAY`/`rollAmbush()` in [combat.md](combat.md)), Silver Tongue (Int, −10% Marketplace buy prices), Scavenger (Int, +20% gold from victories), Field Rations (Int, travel Food cost −10%, see `computeTravel()` in [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md)).

**Active special attacks** — built. Combat no longer stays "just hit Attack": a "Specials" button opens `#combat-specials-panel`, listing whichever of the 6 moves are unlocked, each costing more stamina than a basic Attack and usable once per fight (`combat.usedMoves`, reset in `startCombat`): Crushing Blow (Str, 2x damage to your target), Reckless Swing (Str, 2.5x damage to your target, but that one enemy's counter ignores armor *and* Evasion — see "Multi-enemy encounters" below for why it's scoped to just that enemy), Precise Shot (Agi — since the game has no miss chance to guarantee against, this instead always rolls max damage rather than random), Adrenaline Rush (Agi, a free extra attack with no stamina cost), War Cry (Int, cuts *every* enemy's damage 30% for the rest of the fight, deals no damage itself), Cleave (Str, higher tier — hits every alive enemy for 60% damage each).

**Multi-enemy encounters — built.** Goblin/Ork raiding-party ambushes now spawn 2-3 enemies at once (Bandit ambushes stay solo); `combat.enemies` is an array with click-to-target UI (`#combat-enemies`, `renderEnemies()`), a per-enemy HP bar, auto-retargeting off a killed enemy, and every alive enemy countering each round (`runEnemyCounters`) — the actual difficulty lever that makes a raid harder than a solo fight, not just longer. See [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Multi-enemy encounters" section for the full mechanics and the balance calls made (War Cry/Cleave hit every enemy; Reckless Swing's ignore-armor counter is scoped to only its one target).

**Still deliberately not part of this build:**
- **Talkable NPCs / dialogue** — already captured below as an unscoped idea; explicitly parked, unrelated system (NPC data model, conversation UI, settlement hooks).

**Moved off character.html onto a new "Skills" tab — done.** Per the project owner: Strength/Agility/Intelligence, the 9 perks, and the 6 special attacks now live on [skills.html](secondary-pages.md), a new top-level nav destination on index.html's toolbar alongside Inventory/Party/Hero/Settings, rather than inside the Character sheet. character.html no longer shows any of it — the Level/XP header, Character Stats box, Perks card, and Special Attacks card were removed from character.html entirely and rebuilt on the new page (`renderProgression`/`renderSkillList`, `PERKS`/`MOVES`), leaving character.html back to identity/Health/Stamina/Equipment/Accessories/Purse/basic info. The combat-facing half (`combatSpecial`, `MOVES`, `hasPerk`, etc.) was already in index.html and is unaffected. See [secondary-pages.md](secondary-pages.md) for the full page breakdown.

## Quests — index.html — in progress

**Built so far:** four quest types across two sources — see [quests.md](quests.md) for the full mechanics. The Quest Board (any settlement, a fifth action alongside Inn/Marketplace/Work/Talk to Townsfolk) offers minor delivery jobs, several at once, regenerated deterministically per day rather than stored — a coin flip per slot between Deliver a Message and the heavier-paying Deliver Cargo. A Notable Figure (a new sixth action, but only at the handful of specific settlements one of the 5 named `NOTABLE_FIGURES` calls home — not every City/Capital) offers one important kill-task at a time, capped globally so only one can ever be active: Deal with the Bandits at peace, or Drive Off the Raiders (the actual Goblin/Ork threat, paying more) once that figure's home kingdom is at war — reacting to real game state the same way Marketplace pricing already does. Figures are pinned to their home settlement rather than reassigned daily, and disappear (the action stops appearing, any accepted quest is unaffected) if that settlement is captured by another race — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md). This was a deliberate split from the project owner: important quests come from talking to a character, minor busywork from a board — see "Talkable NPCs" below for how the Notable Figure relates to that still-unscoped idea.

**Not built yet:** the tracker's bigger/structurally-different quest ideas — Protect a Caravan, Destroy a Goblin Nest, Train Villagers, Ask for News (overlaps with Talk to Townsfolk), a Faction Reputation Chain (multi-part, tied to standing with a race), and a fuller Randomised Bounty Board. [quests.md](quests.md)'s "Adding a new quest type" section is the pattern to follow for each — the delivery and kill families already proved it generalizes cleanly (each went from 1 type to 2 without touching the turn-in architecture), these remaining ones are just less well-suited to either existing family. There's also no quest log page yet — the Quest Board's "Your Active Quests" list is the only place to see what's outstanding, and no completed-quest history is kept (a finished quest is just removed from `goblinwar_quests`, not archived).

**Petition for Troops — built, but as a Palace action rather than a `goblinwar_quests` entry.** The idea (travel to a kingdom that isn't at war and convince its royalty to reinforce a threatened ally) turned out to fit the existing Palace flavor-panel better than the Deliver/Kill quest families: the trip to that specific Capital already *is* the "go there" part, and convincing a court is one conversation, not a multi-stage travel-then-turn-in task — so it resolves instantly on accept rather than needing a persisted active quest. See [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s "Alliance reinforcement" section for the full mechanic (`goblinwar_reinforcements`, `findAllyNeedingReinforcement`, `acceptPetition`) and [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md) for where it hooks into the Palace action. Reward is 20-40 gold / 15-25 XP, same seeded-offer pattern as the Quest Board/Notable Figure.

## Equipment — done; Accessories — still a stub

Equipping is real now: six `MARKET_ITEMS` entries (Sword, Shield, Leather Cap, Leather Armour, Leather Boots, Lucky Charm) each carry a `slot` and a `dmg`/`def` combat bonus, `goblinwar_equipped` tracks what's in each of the 6 slots, inventory.html is where you equip/unequip (an Equip/Unequip button per equippable item row), character.html shows what's equipped and lets you unequip from there too, and `combatAttack()` (index.html) actually applies the bonus to damage dealt/taken — see [player-state.md](player-state.md) and [combat.md](combat.md) for the full mechanics.

What's still not built: the **Accessories** grid (character.html, 8 unlabeled slots) is pure decoration — no accessory-type item exists in `MARKET_ITEMS`, nothing points at those slots, tapping one does nothing. Also still missing: any UI hint in the Marketplace itself that an item is equippable *before* you buy it (you only find out on inventory.html), and stacking multiple bonus *sources* per slot (each slot holds exactly one item, as intended, but there's no rarity/tier variation — a Sword is always +4, forever). Selling now exists (see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)) and correctly auto-unequips an item if you sell its last copy — dropping (destroying an item for no gold) still doesn't exist as a separate action.

## Faction AI, territory & war economy — index.html

A real weekly tick now exists — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md) for the full mechanics (kingdoms war/raid/sue-for-peace, settlements can actually change hands, Marketplace prices react to it). Its own "Known simplifications" section is the checklist for this system specifically (binary per-kingdom war state rather than a real relations matrix, raids as a flat coin-flip with no siege/garrison concept, no player involvement in the outcome) — not duplicated here to avoid the two files drifting apart.

## Talk to Townsfolk — index.html — done

A real `'talk'` action now, alongside Marketplace — reuses `showFlavorPanel` (the same pure-flavor panel Temple/Guard House/Palace share, see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)). `getTownsfolkLines(burgId)` assembles the eligible line pool per visit from `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_PEACE`/`_WAR` (via `isAtWarAt`), `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_VILLAGE`/`_CITY` (by `b.tier`), and `TOWNSFOLK_LINES_CAPTURED` (added on top whenever `getController(burgId) !== b.race`) — a settlement-flavored counterpart to the Inn's `RUMOURS`, deliberately scoped as flavor-only, same as the two ideas below it were kept separate from this.

## Talkable NPCs — idea only, nothing built yet

Loose idea, not scoped or scheduled: named NPCs the player can actually talk to, either standing around at a settlement's locations (extending Talk to Townsfolk's flavor text into real characters) or attached to a kingdom's army/military presence (tying into the faction war system — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)). No data model, no UI, no hook into any existing screen yet — this is purely a placeholder so the idea isn't lost, not a spec to build against. Also unscoped and separate: a real "Ask for News" quest type overlapping with Talk to Townsfolk (see the Quests section's "Not built yet" list above) — both were deliberately left out of Talk to Townsfolk's flavor-only build so it stayed small.

The Notable Figure quest-giver (see the Quests section above and [quests.md](quests.md)) is a small, narrow precedent toward this — a named NPC (`NOTABLE_FIGURES`, just `{name, title}`) with a single flavor line and one quest to offer, no portrait, no dialogue tree, no persistence beyond the quest itself. It was scoped deliberately small to unblock "important quests come from a character," not as a first slice of this bigger idea — but if Talkable NPCs ever gets properly scoped, `NOTABLE_FIGURES` and the panel pattern in `showNotableFigurePanel` are a reasonable starting point rather than a clean-sheet design.

**Notable Figure portraits — a smaller, already-scoped task, separate from the full idea above.** All 5 `NOTABLE_FIGURES` (Captain Aldric Vane, Yselle Thorn, Elder Bram Oswick, Scoutmaster Priska Ren, Sir Corwin Hale) already have generation prompts in the Character Forge tracker artifact, marked as implemented in-game but with no portrait yet — `showNotableFigurePanel` could show one once art exists, no dialogue system required.

What already exists toward the bigger idea: a growing roster of human and dwarf character art (knights, mages, rangers, dwarven warriors) cataloged with generation prompts in the "Character Forge" tracker artifact, separate from this repo — ask for its link if it's not at hand. Those entries are intentionally left unchecked for "Asset implemented in game," since none of them are wired into anything here yet. When this idea gets scoped for real, start there for character looks rather than commissioning fresh art.

**Natasha and Sophie — two named characters already designed, per the project owner.** Natasha is a princess of one of the major human kingdoms, meant to be a real talkable character once this system exists. Sophie is Natasha's companion, a large mythical dog, meant to appear alongside her in dialogue scenes. Both have portrait art in Character Forge now — Sophie has two expressions (Friendly/Neutral), the first entry there to use Character Forge's new `expressions` field (see below). Neither is wired into anything in the game yet — this is purely so the idea isn't lost before Talkable NPCs gets properly scoped, same as everything else in this section.

**Character Forge now supports more than one piece of art per character.** Per the project owner, future character art will include sprite sheets (separate frames for talk/dialogue scenes and combat), not just a single static portrait. Character Forge's tracker artifact was extended for this: each entry can carry an optional `expressions` array of `{label, image}` alongside its main portrait, rendered as a row of small labeled thumbnails on the card. Sophie's Friendly/Neutral pair is the first use of it — the pattern is there for combat poses or dialogue frames once those get generated.

## Party / Companions — party.html

The entire page is one `.coming-soon` block: *"No followers yet. ## UI Theme — pixel-art blue/gold reskin — idea only, nothing built yet

Loose idea from the project owner, not scoped or scheduled: replace the current dark-parchment palette (see [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)'s Conventions section) with a pixel-art "royal blue and gold" fantasy RPG look — reference is a mobile hero-sheet screen with a deep blue gradient background, thick ornate gold pixel-art panel borders, segmented pixel Health/Stamina bars, a level badge, a race pill, and locked/unlocked perk cards with an "UNLOCK" button. Nothing built yet; this is a placeholder so the idea and its art requirements aren't lost. Generation prompts for each art asset below are tracked in the "Item Forge" tracker artifact, under its own "UI Theme" section — ask for its link if it's not at hand.

This would touch every page, not just character.html, since the dark-parchment palette is deliberately shared across all of them per the Conventions section.

**Art that would need to be created** — none of it exists in `assets/` today; the current UI is CSS-only, with no custom art beyond enemy portraits and icons:

- **9-slice pixel border frame** — the ornate gold panel border seen around every card (hero sheet, perk cards, stat box), needed as a border-image so CSS panels can use it instead of a plain `border` rule.
- **Segmented Health & Stamina bar sprites** — pixel-block fill textures (red for Health, cyan for Stamina) to replace the current smooth CSS gradient bars.
- **Level badge frame** — the square gold-bordered box that holds the level number (top-left of the hero sheet).
- **Race/class pill background** — the small rounded pill behind the race name ("HUMAN," green, in the reference) — would need one per race once [character creation](#character-creation--race) exists.
- **Character portrait frame + rune border** — the dashed rune-bordered portrait slot ("CHARACTER PORTRAIT – COMING SOON" in the reference) plus a faded heraldic crest/shield watermark behind it.
- **Character portrait sprites** — an actual pixel-art portrait per race to fill that slot, replacing the placeholder text.
- **Perk & special-attack icons** — 9 perk icons + 6 special-attack icons (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s "Leveling & stats") for the Skills tab's cards, which are text-only today.
- **"Unlock" button skin** — the gold gradient pixel-art button used on locked perk/move cards.
- **Top-bar sword icon** — the small angled sword glyph next to "BACK TO WORLD MAP".
- **Pixel-art background texture** — the subtle dark-blue dither/checker pattern behind the whole page.
- **A pixel-style display font** for headers ("CHARACTER", "HERO SHEET") — the game currently ships no custom webfont at all.

## Smaller/implicit gaps (not marked with a UI stub, but visible from the code)

- **Waterskin and Bedroll do nothing.** Food is consumed by travel now, but the other two `DEFAULT_INVENTORY` items are pure flavor/weight — inventory.html has no click handler on rows, so nothing ever uses them.
- **No character customization.** character.html hardcodes "The Traveler" / "Human" / "Kingdom of Bary" — see "Character creation & race" below, where this ties into a bigger idea from the dev tracker.
- **Three enemy races with several variants each now** (Bandit; Goblin Raider/Shieldbearer/Skirmisher/Archer; Ork Raider/Berserker/Warlord — see [combat.md](combat.md)), but which *race* shows up is still a simple rule (`pickAmbushEnemy`) with no scaling by settlement tier, distance from the frontline, or how long a kingdom's been at war, and there's still no fourth "wildlife"/"monster" category distinct from humanoid raiders.
- **Three more variants already prompted, not yet added.** Goblin Chieftain, Goblin Shaman, and Ork Shaman all have generation prompts in the Character Forge tracker artifact but no `ENEMY_VARIANTS` entry — per [combat.md](combat.md)'s own note, adding one is "just one more array entry plus its PNG in `assets/enemies/`, no other code changes," so this is likely the cheapest content addition available in the game right now.
- **No random encounters while simply walking around** — only the two fixed ambush rolls (on arrival, on overnight camp) exist; there's no per-day-of-travel "something happens" table beyond bandits.

## Future ideas from the dev tracker (not yet scoped into stubs)

The rest of this file tracks placeholders already visible in the code. Everything below instead comes from `Goblin_War_Tracker.xlsx` (the Google Sheet dev tracker, in the project's Drive folder) — pure brainstorm, none of it has a code stub yet. The tracker itself was last edited 2026-09-02 and has drifted from the real codebase since (it still lists things like the Marketplace, camping/provisions, and settlement population as "Not Started" when they're actually built — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md), [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)), so this list is a cross-checked, de-duplicated pull of what's genuinely still unbuilt, not a copy-paste of the sheet.

### World map & travel

- **Weather affects travel** — rain/snow/fog slowing travel speed and reducing map visibility. No weather concept exists anywhere in [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md) today.
- **Points-of-interest markers** — non-settlement waypoints (ruins, camps, river crossings) with their own hover labels and quest hooks, distinct from settlement dots.
- **Day/night cycle** — the calendar (`gameDay`) is a flat day counter with no hour-of-day clock stored anywhere (see [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md)'s "Hours vs. the calendar"), so there's no time-of-day to tie visuals, NPC availability, or ambush odds to yet.
- **Random world events** — festivals, plagues, or trade caravans spawning near settlements as short-lived opportunities/threats. Distinct from the existing raid/ambush system (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)), which is kingdom-vs-kingdom conquest, not a local one-off event.
- **Visible armies on the map** — today `getArmyStrength(kingdom)` (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)) is purely an internal number used to weight raid outcomes; nothing renders a moving army or shows who owns it.
- **World map rebuilt as a pixel-art tileset** — idea only, tied to the UI Theme reskin above: regenerate `assets/world-raster.jpg` to match the new blue/gold pixel-art style. Since it'll be generated in Gemini rather than commissioned as one image, and Gemini can't produce the full 10240×5108 raster in a single pass, it needs generating as a grid of tiles that line up at the edges (roads/coastlines/rivers continuing across seams) and stitching back into one image afterward — `assets/game-map.json`'s settlement coordinates need to still line up post-stitch. Tracked as a prompt card in the Item Forge tracker artifact's "World Map" section.
- **War frontline tracker / territory border detection** — a visual read of how the Good/Bad alliance line has shifted from sieges and battles won. The underlying per-settlement territory-control data already exists (`goblinwar_territoryControl`), but nothing aggregates or draws it as a frontline, and there's no real border/adjacency concept at all — [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s `attemptRaid` already flags this gap directly (it substitutes proximity-to-centroid for a real border when picking raid targets, "the game doesn't have kingdom border data"). A "which settlements border an enemy-controlled settlement" query is the prerequisite both for drawing a real frontline and for the settlement-art idea just below. Would also sharpen **Alliance reinforcement** (now built — see the Quests section above and [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md) — but currently picks *which* ally to help by least-reinforced rather than by proximity/actual border).

### Settlements

- **Unique per-settlement art** (a generated picture per visited settlement, replacing the four generic tier illustrations in `TIER_ART` — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)), with graduated variants reacting to war state rather than a single fixed look per tier: a **war/border variant** (fortifications, banners, visible unease) for a settlement whose kingdom is at war *and* sits near enemy-controlled territory — needs the border-detection concept above to know which settlements actually qualify as "on the border" — and a **destroyed/raided variant** for after a settlement actually changes hands. Same tier + state combination should render consistently everywhere that settlement's art shows up (the location-view corner box today, and `showLocationView`'s subtitle already has separate `(captured)` text that a matching art variant would reinforce visually rather than duplicate).
- **Settlement prosperity**, distinct from the population number that already exists — population currently only feeds war attrition/refugees/army strength (see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)); Marketplace pricing still only reacts to kingdom-wide war/peace, not a per-settlement prosperity figure.
- **Trade goods / arbitrage economy** — buy-low-sell-high goods (cloth, ore, grain) between settlements. `MARKET_ITEMS` prices are the same everywhere modulo the war multiplier; there's no per-settlement price variation to arbitrage.
- **Horse travel** — a purchasable Marketplace item for faster overland pace, alongside the existing road/sea travel modeled in [travel-and-map.md](travel-and-map.md).

### Character creation & race

- **Character creation at new-game** — choosing a race/origin that determines starting stats and which alliance (Good/Bad) you begin aligned with, instead of the hardcoded "Human" / "Kingdom of Bary" in character.html.
- **Race-specific traits** — the tracker's Races data models Human/Dwarf as "stronger equipment, better tactics" and Ork/Goblin as "multiply more quickly," none of which is wired into any gameplay system yet (this would presumably hook into the same Strength/Agility/Intelligence system in the Skills & Progression section above, e.g. as starting stat bonuses).
- **Religion per faction** — lore-only idea, no gameplay hook proposed yet.

### Combat

- **Status effects** — bleed, stun, or fatigue as modifiers layered on top of the existing turn-based Attack/Specials/Flee loop (see [combat.md](combat.md)).

### Open design question worth flagging

The tracker's "Skills & Combat" tab describes an older, different idea: a Skyrim/Bannerlord-style skill list trained by *using* it (One-handed Weapons, Two-handed Weapons, Bow & Arrow, Medium/Heavy Armour, Healing, Horse Riding, Trade/Charisma, Cooking/Leadership), with an explicit open question of learn-everything vs. a capped build. That predates and doesn't match the Strength/Agility/Intelligence + skill-point perks/moves system now actually built (see the Skills & Progression section above). Worth a decision from the project owner on whether that older idea is superseded, or meant as a second, complementary "proficiency" layer to add later alongside stats/perks.

### Living world (large, mostly unscoped)

Still just one-line ideas in the tracker, no design decisions made on any of these: marriage/romance, NPC memory (reacting differently based on player actions — ties into the Talkable NPCs idea above), a reputation system, character death with some form of legacy, and NPC children existing in the world. Item rarity/tiers is already tracked above under Equipment (a Sword is always +4, forever). Sieges/garrisons and a real kingdom relations matrix are already tracked in [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s "Known simplifications."

One thing the tracker records as **already decided**, not just an idea: the player is meant to grow strong but never able to solo-topple a city — a support-role cap on player power, worth keeping in mind whenever combat or army-strength numbers get revisited.
