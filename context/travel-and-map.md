# Travel & Map (index.html)

The overworld screen. This is the largest and most complex system in the game — everything else (combat, resting, save state) hangs off of it.

## Two coordinate spaces

The world data and the rendered image use different scales, and the code converts between them constantly:

- **Logical space** (`LOGICAL_W=2560, LOGICAL_H=1277`) — the coordinate system every settlement, road point, and the SVG `#overlay` viewBox use. This is what `travel-graph.json` stores `x`/`y` in.
- **Raster/stage space** (`STAGE_W=10240, STAGE_H=5108`) — the actual pixel size of `world-raster.jpg` and `#map-stage`, exactly 4× logical space.

`STAGE_W/LOGICAL_W` (4) is the conversion factor, used everywhere the camera needs to place something in screen pixels from a logical `x,y`.

## Camera (pan/zoom)

- `view = { scale, tx, ty }` is the single source of truth for the camera. `applyView()` (after `clampView()` keeps it in bounds — can't zoom out past fitting the map, can't zoom in past `maxScale=3.0`) writes `translate(tx,ty) scale(view.scale)` to `#map-stage`'s own transform; `#map-img` and `#overlay` are both plain children of `#map-stage` and scale for free via that one transform, no per-element work needed. The map art is a deliberately pixelated render at its baseline zoom (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "World map rebuilt as a painterly render"), but three earlier attempts at forcing that pixelation to stay crisp on zoom-in (a conditional `image-rendering:pixelated` threshold, an unconditional one, then real width/height resizing of `#map-img` instead of a transform) were all abandoned as unreliable — a `will-change:transform`-promoted layer gets rasterized once and then GPU-resampled by the compositor regardless of any `image-rendering` hint on its descendants. The simpler, reliable choice: don't fight it — up close the map just softens via the browser's normal smooth upscaling instead of staying blocky, since the pixel-art look is really about the overview zoom, not the zoomed-in one.
- `centerOnLogical(lx, ly, scale)` is the one function that points the camera at a logical coordinate — used on load, on recenter, and (critically) every frame during travel animation.
- Panning/pinch-zoom is handled with raw Pointer Events (`pointerdown`/`pointermove`/`pointerup`) rather than touch/mouse-specific listeners, so it works the same on desktop and mobile. A single pointer that doesn't move more than 6px counts as a tap (`handleTap`), not a drag.

## The road/sea graph

`travel-graph.json` models the world as a graph of map **cells** (not just settlements) — every point along a drawn road, trail, or sea route is a node, so junctions and mid-route camping work naturally. See [data-files.md](data-files.md) for the exact JSON shape.

- `buildAdjacency()` turns the flat `edges` array into a `cellId -> [{to, mi, edge, forward}]` map, once, at load.
- `cellRoute(fromCell, toCell)` is a Dijkstra shortest-path search over that adjacency map using a hand-rolled binary `MinHeap` (a plain sorted array was too slow across thousands of cells). Returns `{miles, edges}` or `null` if there's no path at all.
- `computeTravel(toId)` wraps `cellRoute` into game terms: raw miles are first divided by `DISTANCE_SCALE` (`5`) — the world data's mileage is realistic-scale and would otherwise make a cross-map trip take dozens of in-game days, so this compresses every distance down before anything else touches it. From there: miles → **hours** (`Math.max(1, Math.ceil(miles/ROAD_PACE))`, `ROAD_PACE=40`/day-equivalent — doubled from `20` per the project owner, see "Pace tuned down further" below) — trips are timed in hours, not days, so even a settlement "2 days" away by the old realistic pacing now reads as roughly an hour and rarely dents the calendar at all. `hoursToHungerGain(hours)`/`hoursToThirstGain(hours)` and `hoursToDaysElapsed(hours)` (all near `computeTravel`) derive Hunger/Thirst gain and calendar advancement from that hour count separately — see "Hours vs. the calendar" below and [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section. If no route exists (rare — most of the ~800 settlements are connected), it falls back to a straight-line "cross-country" estimate at `OFFROAD_PACE=24`/day-equivalent instead of blocking travel entirely (also scaled by `DISTANCE_SCALE`).
- **Pace tuned down further — done.** Per the project owner, trips were still eating too much of the calendar even after the initial hours-based rework above: sampled against the real `travel-graph.json` data, a typical cross-map trip landed around a full in-game day, and even a "next village over" hop routinely cost 1-4 hours — enough that a normal session of settlement-hopping (Quest Board, Marketplace, a delivery run) could burn several calendar days without the player doing anything that felt like "days." `ROAD_PACE`/`OFFROAD_PACE` were both doubled (`20`→`40`, `12`→`24`) so the same distance now costs roughly half the hours — the sampled cross-map median dropped from ~26.5 hours to ~13.5. Nothing else about the pacing model changed: Hunger/Thirst gain and `gameDay`'s own rollover are still driven directly off these hours (see "Calendar and the clock" below), so this isn't just a cosmetic speedup — days and months genuinely accrue slower for the same amount of play, which was the actual ask. First-pass number, easy to retune further if it still feels fast.
- `formatDuration(hours)` renders that count for display — `"N hours"` under a day, `"N days M hours"` above it — used in the info-card quote and the Travel button label (`Travel Here (Nh)`).
- `computeReachable(fromCell)` is a separate, distance-ignorant flood fill used only once at load, to detect if the player's saved location got stranded with zero road connections (falls back to Bary, burg id `"5"`, if so).
- `buildBurgAdjacency()` (index.html, called right after `buildAdjacency()`) derives a *settlement*-level border graph on top of this cell-level one, for the faction/war system — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md). Neither `edges` nor `adjacency` connect burgs to each other directly (most cells aren't a settlement at all), so for each burg it BFSes outward through `adjacency` and stops each branch at the first cell that's home to some *other* burg, caching the result as `burgId -> [burgId, ...]` in a separate `burgAdjacency` map. Not used for player travel/pathing at all — `cellRoute`/`computeTravel` above are untouched by this.
- **A second consumer of this same pathfinding, besides the player.** `computeDistanceBetween(burgIdA, burgIdB)` (originally just for a quest offer's travel-time estimate) and `buildRoutePolyline` are also what a marching `goblinwar_armies` entry uses to compute its own route and on-screen position — see [factions-and-territory.md](factions-and-territory.md)'s "Armies march before a siege starts". Same road/sea graph, same Dijkstra search, no separate pathing system for armies.

## Where you are: settlement vs. camp

`currentBurg` (a burg id string, persisted as `goblinwar_currentBurg`) is the player's last-arrived-at settlement. But mid-journey the player can stop or camp somewhere that isn't a named settlement — `campPos = {x, y, cell, name}` holds that in-between position (snapped to the nearest real point on the road graph). Three helpers abstract over both cases so the rest of the code never has to branch on it:

- `getCurrentCell()`, `getCurrentXY()`, `getCurrentDisplayName()` — all check `campPos` first, fall back to `graph.burgs[currentBurg]`.

`campPos` is cleared (`= null`) only on a completed arrival (`beginTravel`'s `onDone`), never on rest — resting in camp keeps you on the road, it doesn't teleport you to a settlement.

## Travel animation

`beginTravel(destId)` is the entry point (called from the info-card's Travel button, or "Continue" in the camp view). It:

1. Computes the route via `computeTravel`. There's no affordability gate anymore — travel is never blocked by Food/Waterskin item count (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section for why).
2. Shows the `#travel-controls` bar (Stop / Set Up Camp).
3. Hands off to `animateTravel(fromPos, toId, edgeSteps, ...)`, which:
   - Builds one flat polyline (`buildRoutePolyline`) by concatenating each edge's real point list (`edge.pts`) in the correct direction — so the marker visibly follows actual drawn roads, not a straight line (unless there's no route at all).
   - Walks that polyline at **constant linear speed** (no easing) over a duration proportional to (already-scaled) distance (`Math.max(4000, 2000 + miles*20)` ms) — deliberately unhurried, like a Bannerlord-style overworld crossing, not a quick dart. This is independent of the hours/Hunger/Thirst/calendar math above — animation length is purely about watching the journey happen, not simulated at all against in-game time.
   - On every animation frame, calls `centerOnLogical(pos.x, pos.y, view.scale)` — recomputed from the **current** `view.scale` each time, not a value cached at journey start. This is what keeps the character centered under the camera even if the player pinch-zooms or scrolls mid-journey; an earlier version cached the camera math once and zooming mid-travel caused visible drift.
4. On arrival: sets `currentBurg`, advances the clock (`advanceTime(t.hours)` — see "Calendar and the clock" below), raises Hunger and Thirst (`addHunger(t.hungerGain)`/`addThirst(t.thirstGain)` — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)), and rolls a bandit ambush chance (`rollAmbush(t.hours, startHour)`, converted to days internally and scaled up for however much of the trip fell during night hours — see [combat.md](combat.md)) before opening the location view. If the ambush roll misses, a second independent roll (`rollWorldEvent()`, a flat `WORLD_EVENT_CHANCE` — no day/night scaling, deliberately simpler than the ambush roll) can instead pop up `#world-event-modal` (`triggerWorldEvent`) — a small two-choice vignette (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "World events") — before the location view opens. At most one interruption (a fight or an event) ever fires per arrival; a miss on both just opens the location view as before.

`stopTravel()` and `campMidTravel()` both cancel the in-flight animation (`controller.cancelled = true; cancelAnimationFrame(...)`) and snap the player to `activeTravel.lastSnap` — the most recent `{x, y, cell}` the animation reported via its `onProgress` callback, i.e. wherever they actually were when interrupted, not back at the start. `campMidTravel` additionally derives `partialHours` from `activeTravel.totalHours * frac` (the fraction of the journey completed), advances the clock and raises Hunger/Thirst from that via `advanceTime`/`hoursToHungerGain`/`hoursToThirstGain` — the same real-hours math a completed arrival uses — and opens the camp screen showing `formatDuration(partialHours)` passed.

**The header's Hunger/Thirst numbers — and the clock — move live during the animation, not just at arrival.** Hunger/Thirst are still only ever actually applied (`addHunger`/`addThirst`) at arrival or at a mid-journey interruption (unchanged from above — nothing about when the real change lands has changed), and the real clock (`gameHour`) only actually moves via `advanceTime` at those same points — but leaving `#stat-hunger`/`#stat-thirst`/the clock frozen at their pre-travel values for the whole animation, then jumping straight to the post-trip numbers, made a trip look free (and timeless) until it suddenly wasn't. `beginTravel` now captures `startHunger`/`startThirst`/`startHour` before departing, and the same `onProgress` callback that updates `activeTravel.lastSnap` each frame also calls `updateLiveHungerThirstDisplay(...)` and `updateLiveClockDisplay(...)`, which repaint the Hunger/Thirst pills and the header clock with a smooth linear interpolation that lands exactly on the real post-arrival values at `frac=1`, so there's no discontinuity with the `addHunger`/`addThirst`/`advanceTime` calls that follow. This is purely a display (Hunger/Thirst capped at 100 even though the real calls can still register genuine overflow past that) — it never touches the real meters or clock itself, so it can't cause a double application. The one place this needed a matching fix: `stopTravel()` applies no Hunger/Thirst gain or clock advancement at all ("No time cost"), so it now calls both `refreshPlayerStatUI()` and `refreshCalendarUI()` on cancel to restore the true, untouched numbers — otherwise the live displays would be left showing values that were never actually applied.

## Horse (faster road travel) and ferry fares (paid sea crossings)

Two small additions to `computeTravel(toId)`, both derived from `routeBetween`'s route rather than needing new world data:

- **Horse** — a `MARKET_ITEMS` entry (index.html, weight `0`, no `slot` — not equippable, just a carried possession like Bedroll) that speeds up road travel. `computeTravel` checks `getItem('Horse')`: whenever the route is `viaRoad` and has no sea leg (`!bySea`), the trip's `hours` is divided by `HORSE_PACE_MULT` (`1.5`) before Hunger/Thirst gain is derived from it — a horse doesn't swim, so it never touches a sea crossing's pace. The returned `mounted` flag drives a small "(riding — faster pace)" note in the info-card's travel quote (`onMapTap`).
- **Ferry fares** — `routeBetween` now also returns `seaMiles` (the route's sea-`kind` edges' own mileage, summed and scaled by `DISTANCE_SCALE` same as `miles`), and `computeTravel` derives `ferryCost` from it: `Math.max(FERRY_MIN_FARE, Math.round(seaMiles * FERRY_GOLD_PER_MILE))` whenever `seaMiles > 0`, else `0`. Unlike Hunger/Thirst (deliberately never a travel gate — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)), this **is** a real affordability gate: `beginTravel` checks `playerGold` against `t.ferryCost` and refuses (toast, no travel) if short, otherwise deducts it via `setGold` before the journey animation starts. The info-card shows the fare up front (`Travel Here (Nh, Fg)`) so it's never a surprise at departure. A cancelled/interrupted crossing (`stopTravel`/`campMidTravel`) doesn't refund an already-paid fare.

## Calendar and the clock

Two stored time values now, not one: `gameDay` (key `goblinwar_gameDay`, a whole-number day counter, unchanged in meaning) and `gameHour` (key `goblinwar_gameHour`, a real hour-of-day, `0` up to but not including `24`, float-precision so a clock reading like "2:30 PM" is exact, not rounded to the hour). `DAYS_PER_MONTH=30`, `MONTHS_PER_YEAR=12` are fixed constants used to derive day/month/year for display; `timeStr(hour)` formats `gameHour` as a 12-hour clock string. Both — plus `refreshCalendarUI()`, the render function — are duplicated in character.html and settings.html rather than shared, since there's no module system (see root [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)); those two pages are static snapshots on load, not live tickers, so they just read the stored value once.

**`advanceTime(hours, quiet)` is the single source of truth for time passing, real hours not whole days.** Per the project owner, the old model (`hoursToDaysElapsed(hours) = Math.floor(hours/HOURS_PER_DAY)`, only moving `gameDay` once a trip's hours crossed a full 24-hour boundary) meant a short hop was free of any calendar cost at all — there was no hour-of-day clock stored anywhere to move. `advanceTime` replaces that everywhere calendar time passes (travel arrival, `campMidTravel`, `waitTick` — see below): it adds `hours` straight onto `gameHour`, and only when that total actually crosses `24` does it roll the excess into `gameDay` and fire the once-per-day side effects (`chargeMercenaryUpkeep()`, `maybeRunFactionAI()`) — skipped entirely when `quiet` is true, which only `waitTick` ever passes (see "Waiting at a location" below). A 3-hour trip now visibly moves the clock a few hours; a 20-hour trip visibly crosses into the next day partway through, both correctly reflected the moment they land, live during travel too (see above).

**Resting always wakes the player at a fixed hour, not "whatever time plus 24."** `restThroughNight()` (Tavern's "Rest for the Night" and Camp's own rest button, both previously a bare `advanceDays(1)`) sets `gameHour` straight to `WAKE_HOUR` (`6`) and `gameDay += 1`, regardless of what hour the player fell asleep at — sleeping through the night wakes you at dawn, it doesn't just add exactly one day's worth of hours to whatever moment you dozed off. Runs the same once-per-day side effects `advanceTime`'s non-quiet path does, since resting is a real way a whole day passes.

Hunger/Thirst still use their own matching helpers, unaffected by any of this: `hoursToHungerGain(hours) = Math.max(2, Math.round((hours/HOURS_PER_DAY)*HUNGER_PER_DAY))` and `hoursToThirstGain` the same shape at its own rate — see [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section for the full mechanic (including what happens if either maxes out).

## Day/night cycle

Per the project owner: a real clock existing at all (above) is what actually unblocked this — see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Day/night cycle" idea, which had explicitly been waiting on exactly this. Two things read `gameHour`, both driven by one function, `updateNightOverlay(hour)` (called from `refreshCalendarUI` for the real clock, and from `updateLiveClockDisplay` during travel for the live preview):

- **`nightFactor(hour)`** returns `0` (full daylight, 8:00-18:00) to `1` (deepest night, 21:00-6:00), ramped linearly through dawn (6:00-8:00) and dusk (18:00-21:00) rather than an instant cutoff, so both effects below ease in and out instead of snapping.
- **`#night-overlay`**, a `position:absolute; inset:0` div layered in `#map-viewport` alongside (and below) the existing `#ui-vignette`, gets its opacity set via a `--night-opacity` CSS custom property (`nightFactor(hour) * MAX_NIGHT_OPACITY`, `0.5` at full night) with a `1.2s` CSS transition doing the actual smoothing — a dark navy (`#0a1230`) wash over the whole viewport that stays put regardless of pan/zoom, same reasoning as the vignette it sits next to. Deliberately not opaque even at its darkest — this is a mood/lighting cue, not a "you can't see the map at night" mechanic.
- **The header clock badge's icon** swaps between a sun and a crescent-moon `<svg>` at the `nightFactor(hour) >= 0.5` threshold — toggled via `style.display`, not the `hidden` attribute; `hidden`'s `display:none` UA rule didn't actually suppress these inline SVG children in testing even though the attribute itself was being set correctly, so the toggle sidesteps that quirk entirely rather than chasing the root cause.

**Gameplay reacts to it now too, in two places — done.** Per the project owner: `rollAmbush` scales ambush risk up for however much of a trip falls during night hours (see [combat.md](combat.md)'s "Where fights are triggered" for the exact math), and the Work action is unavailable at night (`isNightNow()`, same `nightFactor(gameHour) >= 0.5` threshold the map tint uses) — disabled with an explanation ("Nobody's hiring after dark — come back in daylight") the same way the existing once-per-day cooldown already disabled it, and `doWork()` itself refuses defensively too, not just the button. Still not built: anything beyond those two — settlement/NPC availability more broadly, enemy composition, etc. are all still exactly what they were before any of this shipped.

## Tap-to-select and the info card

Every settlement gets an invisible circular `<circle class="hitzone">` sized by tier (`HIT_R`), built once in `buildHitzones()`. Tapping one calls `onMapTap(burgId)`, which populates and opens `#info-card` with one of two states: "you are here" (already at that burg), or a travel quote (miles/`formatDuration(hours)`/Hunger & Thirst cost, plus a route preview drawn along the same polyline logic as the travel animation) with the Travel button always enabled, labeled `Travel Here (Nh)` — there's no affordability gate to block it, per [player-state.md](player-state.md)'s Hunger & Thirst section.

## Roads, settlement dots, labels, and city icons

`world-raster.jpg` used to be Azgaar's own export, with roads, settlement pips, and place names all baked directly into its pixels. The painterly re-render (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "World map rebuilt as a painterly render") has none of that baked in — it's terrain only — so all of it is now real SVG built once at load, in the `#overlay`'s `#roads`/`#border-markers`/`#settlement-dots`/`#labels`/`#city-icons` groups (in that DOM order, so later groups paint over earlier ones):

- **`buildRoads()`** draws every `road`- and `trail`-kind edge from `travel-graph.json` — but not one `<path>` per edge. Every individual edge is just a straight 2-point segment between two adjacent cells (confirmed by inspecting the data: all 385 road edges have exactly 2 points), so smoothing an edge on its own is a no-op — the jagged look players actually see is the *macro* shape formed by chaining many short straight edges together across the network. `buildEdgeChains(kinds)` walks the road/trail graph first: starting from every junction or dead-end cell (anything whose edge-degree isn't exactly 2), it follows each attached edge through however many degree-2 "pass-through" cells come next — concatenating their points, dropping the duplicate shared point at each join — until hitting the next junction or dead-end, and calls that whole run one chain (plus a second pass for any leftover pure loop with no junction anywhere on it). `kinds` can be a single kind or an array; `buildRoads()` passes `['road','trail']` together as one merged network, so a path that happens to switch from `road` to `trail` partway doesn't show an artificial break at that boundary. Each chain is then Chaikin-smoothed exactly like a river (`chaikinSmooth`/`smoothedPathD`, the same technique and the same reasoning as `render-painterly.js`'s river rendering — interpolation-only, so it can't cusp on a sharp turn the way Catmull-Rom would), then rendered with a worn-dirt-path look — a darker earthen edge stroke (`.road-road-edge`) drawn first for *every* chain, then a lighter dusty-tan fill stroke (`.road-road-fill`) on top for every chain, as two full passes rather than edge-then-fill per chain in turn, so one chain's fill never gets covered by a different chain's edge at a junction (the same halo-then-centerline trick rivers use).
- **`buildBorderMarkers()`** — the political border overlay, drawn just above roads so settlement dots/labels/icons still paint cleanly over it. A first version drew one line per `buildBurgAdjacency()` edge crossing the Good/Bad alliance line — shipped, then replaced after rendering it against the live data showed why it was wrong: that graph fans out far past a settlement's real neighbors before hitting the next burg across open terrain (fine for `isFrontlineBurg`'s yes/no check, wrong for a literal line per edge — 2302 edges over 426 burgs produced a dense, unreadable tangle covering most of the continent, not a border). The real version instead classifies a `TERRITORY_CONTOUR_COLS × TERRITORY_CONTOUR_ROWS` (160×80) grid over logical space by each point's *nearest burg's current controlling alliance* — a Voronoi-by-alliance field — then traces the boundary between Good- and Bad-dominant cells with marching squares (`CONTOUR_EDGE_TABLE`), the same technique a real elevation contour/isoline uses. Renders as one continuous dashed line, including small closed loops around an isolated pocket of the other alliance's territory — confirmed by rendering it against the live `travel-graph.json` data before shipping (~30ms for the full grid, negligible next to the tick it's rebuilt alongside). Same-alliance boundaries (Human/Dwarf, Ork/Goblin) never produce a crossing at all under *this* single Good/Bad field, since both map to the same sign — a real gap a later rewrite had to fix, see "Rebuilt a third time" further down. The contour geometry was correct from the start, but a single warm-toned stroke all but disappeared against `world-raster.jpg`'s busy painterly terrain (grain texture, tree sprites, mountain shading) — the first render check used a blank background, which hid the problem, and a player reported seeing no border at all once it actually shipped. The line now draws as two full passes, a dark outline stroke then a bright dashed fill on top — the same halo-then-centerline technique `buildRoads()`/rivers already use, confirmed this time by rendering it directly over the real raster image. Rebuilt on load and after the weekly faction tick (`maybeRunFactionAI`), since that's the only place territory control (`setController`, via a siege capture) ever actually changes. See [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)'s "Colored factions/borders on the map" for the full design history, including why this is a computed contour rather than a territory fill (no per-settlement polygon exists in `travel-graph.json` to shade instead).

  **At-war segments get a second, louder style — done.** Per the project owner, a border that reads identically whether the two sides are at peace or actively fighting undersold the war system. Every cell the contour crosses checks `getRelation` between the two kingdoms that meet there, and a segment whose two kingdoms are at war renders thicker and solid (no dash — a front line, not an administrative one) in the same danger-red already used for a captured settlement's `(captured)` tag (`#e2857f`), drawn in its own pass after every peace segment so an at-war stretch always reads on top where the two meet. (Exactly how "the two kingdoms that meet at this cell" gets determined changed with the later rewrites below — see "Rebuilt a third time" for the current mechanism — but the war-vs-peace styling split itself has held steady since this entry.) Verified against the real data with a seeded war relation before shipping, same harness as every border check before it — worth noting the first attempt at that check picked two kingdoms whose territories, it turned out, don't actually share a contour border at all (their centroids are close, but a third kingdom's territory sits between them) — confirmed by directly querying which kingdom pairs the grid actually produces a crossing for, rather than assuming from centroid proximity.

  **Peace segments colored per kingdom, and bumped up overall — done.** Per the project owner, the border should read as *whose* border, matching the same `kingdomColor()` every settlement name and territory label already uses, not one flat cream line everywhere; separately, the border in general needed to read as more apparent. Both widths were bumped (outline `4.2`→`5.0`/war `5.4`→`6.0`; fill `1.8`→`2.4`/war `2.6`→`3.2`) and the peace dash tightened (`5,4`→`7,3`, denser) for more visual weight generally.

  A first version of the per-kingdom coloring split each segment at its own midpoint, one half per side's `kingdomColor()` — shipped, then the player asked why the border looked "multi colored": a kingdom with several different neighbors got a different color on its own half of the line every time the neighbor across from it changed, reading as an alternating "candy cane" rather than one coherent line per kingdom. A second version kept the original Good/Bad contour (one shared boundary between the two alliances) but drew it as two parallel offset lines, one per kingdom's color — closer, but the player then sent screenshots showing a plain black line with *no* color at all in some places, and colors still changing repeatedly along one edge in others. The actual root cause, only visible once traced against real screenshots rather than a synthetic test: the whole system only ever traced the boundary between Good-alliance and Bad-alliance territory — between two kingdoms on the *same* side (Human vs Dwarf, or one Human kingdom vs another) it drew nothing at all, which is the bare black-outline gap (a road or coastline showing through with no border drawn over it), and it's also why the visible line still changed kingdom colors partway — it was only ever *the* Good/Bad line, which can border several different kingdoms along its own length on either side.

  **Rebuilt a third time to trace every kingdom's own complete border, not just the Good/Bad line — done.** "Give each kingdom its own border" meant literally that. A single binary sign field can't represent 24 kingdoms' boundaries at once, so `buildBorderMarkers()` now runs the same marching-squares trace (`CONTOUR_EDGE_TABLE`, `interp` — unchanged) once *per kingdom* that currently holds any territory, each time treating just that one kingdom as the "positive" region and everyone else as "negative" against the same nearest-burg `kingdomAt` grid (the expensive part — nearest-burg-per-grid-point — still runs exactly once; the 24 extra passes are cheap table lookups over that already-computed grid, ~130ms total measured against the real data, still a one-time cost on load/weekly-tick, not a per-frame one). Two adjacent kingdoms' own traces would otherwise land on the exact same pixels at a simple two-kingdom edge (marching squares' interpolation is symmetric under sign flip), so each kingdom's own line still gets the offset treatment from the previous version (`BORDER_PARALLEL_OFFSET`, `1.9` logical units into its own territory, direction tested per segment against that cell's own-kingdom corners rather than assumed from point ordering) — now driven by "am I this kingdom" instead of "which side of Good/Bad am I."

  Generalizing to all 24 kingdoms surfaced a second real bug before it could ship broken a third time: nearest-burg classification covers the *entire* map, open ocean included, all the way to its edges — harmless for a Good/Bad field (only ever a couple of huge regions), but tracing one contour per kingdom turned every stretch of empty sea into real Voronoi-cell boundaries between whichever kingdoms' coastal settlements happened to be nearest, radiating out as long straight lines toward the map corners — confirmed by rendering this exact version against the real data before the fix, not caught by inspection alone. `MAX_TERRITORY_DISTANCE` (`140` logical units, sampled against the real data: the single worst-case nearest-neighbor gap between any two settlements is ~99, so 140 stays clear of fragmenting real, sparsely-connected territory while still cutting off well short of open ocean) caps how far from its own nearest settlement a grid point can be and still count as anyone's territory; beyond it a point is unclaimed, which reads as "not this kingdom" for every kingdom's own trace alike, so a kingdom's line closes around a real bubble of influence near its own settlements instead of extending indefinitely. War segments (`getRelation` between a kingdom and whichever specific neighbor borders it at that cell) get the same offset-per-kingdom treatment, just in the shared thicker solid danger-red instead of that kingdom's own color — so a war edge between two kingdoms now shows as two offset red lines rather than kingdom colors, still visually distinct from an ordinary peacetime border. Verified this version the same way as every one before it, directly against the real map art and data — confirming every stretch of every kingdom's own border now shows in one consistent color regardless of which kingdom (same alliance or not) sits on the other side, and that the ocean-spoke bug is gone.

  **Rebuilt a fourth time to keep the border on land — done.** `MAX_TERRITORY_DISTANCE` fixed the ocean-*spoke* bug (long straight lines radiating to the map corners) but is a pure distance cutoff with no idea where the actual coastline is, so a coastal settlement's own territory bubble still pushed the border line out over open water toward that same 140-unit cap wherever the coast curved inward — the project owner's next report ("keep the borders on the land mass too, don't go out into the ocean"). `travel-graph.json` has no per-cell land/water polygon to check against, so the only real source of truth is the rendered map art itself. `ensureBorderWaterMask()` (called lazily from `buildBorderMarkers()`, result cached in `borderWaterMask`) loads `assets/world-raster.jpg` into an offscreen canvas, draws it downscaled directly to the contour grid's own resolution (161×81, matching `TERRITORY_CONTOUR_COLS/ROWS`), and classifies every pixel `isWater = (blue - red) > 5`. Downscaling *before* sampling isn't incidental: a single full-resolution pixel is noisy against this map's painterly terrain texture (grain/tree-sprite shading can sample blue-dominant even on land), but averaged down to grid resolution the noise washes out. Calibrated directly against the real image with Python/PIL before writing the JS version — the downscaled `(blue-red) > 5` rule separated all 426 real burgs (including all 76 coastal ports specifically, the highest-risk case) from open-ocean sample points with zero misclassifications and comfortable margins on both sides (land: red beats blue by 16+; water: blue beats red by 19-27). The grid classification loop now requires a point pass *both* the existing distance cutoff *and* `!isWater` to count as anyone's territory. Verified with a standalone Playwright harness against the real `travel-graph.json`/`world-raster.jpg`, screenshotted at both the full map and several zoomed-in coastal stretches — confirmed borders traced tight against the coastline instead of bulging into the water at every one of them, and shipped.

  **Rebuilt a fifth time — the shipped version above was correct in isolation but still wrong live, and a player screenshot proved it.** Image decode can't happen synchronously, so the original version of this fix drew the border once immediately using the old distance-cutoff-only math while `assets/world-raster.jpg` was still decoding for the mask, on the reasoning that a briefly-wrong border was "better than no border at all" — but that first render *is* the exact pre-mask ocean-crossing bug, not some close-enough placeholder, and every offline verification pass had explicitly awaited the mask before its one screenshot, so this was never actually exercised until a real player, on a real device, sent a screenshot of dead-straight lines slicing across a lake — indistinguishable from the bug the mask was supposed to have fixed. Re-sampling that exact spot's grid cells directly against the real asset (`assets/world-raster.jpg` downscaled the same way `ensureBorderWaterMask()` does) confirmed the mask classifies it correctly once loaded — the bug was purely in the timing, not the classification. `buildBorderMarkers()` now returns without drawing anything at all while `borderWaterMask` is null and no *terminal* failure (`borderWaterMaskFailed`) has been recorded yet, letting the pending promise's own callback do the only draw once the real mask is in hand — a border briefly absent on a slow connection instead of briefly wrong. A genuine load failure (a thrown canvas read, e.g. some tainted-canvas edge case, or the image itself failing to fetch) is tracked separately via `borderWaterMaskFailed` so that case still falls through to the old distance-only math rather than leaving the border permanently empty — a `null` mask alone no longer means "give up," only "still waiting." Verified this exact regression and its fix with an updated harness that calls `buildBorderMarkers()` *before* awaiting the mask (matching real cold-load conditions instead of the old test's own mask-first shortcut) — confirmed zero lines drawn while pending, then the correct coastline-hugging render once the mask resolves, at the same real map location (near Bary, the lake toward Sodoy) the player's screenshot showed broken.

  Roads and trails render *identically* now — two earlier passes tried keeping trails visually distinct as "minor paths" (first a thin dashed single-tone stroke, then just a more visible version of the same), but per the project owner both read as "this settlement isn't really connected" even where a trail plainly did connect it (confirmed by inspecting the data: only 6 of 796 settlements have no road/trail edge at all — those are sea-only or fully isolated; everything else genuinely has a path). Every real connection gets the full road treatment now, no separate `.road-trail` styling left. `sea` edges are still not drawn at all — a line across open water mostly restates what the water itself already implies, unlike a road with no other visual cue.
- **`buildSettlementDots()`** draws a small filled circle (`DOT_R`) at every Village/Town's exact point — the only thing that marks their location at all now that there's no baked pip. City/Capital tier is deliberately skipped here: they get the castle icon instead (below), not a dot underneath one.
- **`buildLabels()`** draws one `<text class="map-label tier-{tier}">` per burg (`LABEL_FONT_SIZE`/`LABEL_OFFSET_Y`, offset *below* the point), for all 796 settlements, always — an earlier pass scaled Capital labels up to a much larger size and hid Town/Village labels entirely below a zoom threshold, which per the project owner read as inconsistent (a few huge names, most just missing) rather than genuinely decluttered. Sizes stay small and close together across every tier so the map remains legible with literally every name showing at once — declutter comes from the small uniform text itself, not from selectively hiding settlements — but were bumped up a second time (`4`-`6`, from `3`-`4.5`; `LABEL_OFFSET_Y` and `.map-label`'s own stroke-width scaled up to match) per the project owner, still too hard to read at the first size.

  **Colored by controlling kingdom — done.** Per the project owner, each settlement's own name now renders in its controlling kingdom's color (`kingdomColor(getControllerKingdom(id))`, set via `t.style.fill`) instead of one flat gold for every label. `kingdomColor()` (right above `buildLabels()`) assigns one stable HSL hue per kingdom, evenly spaced around the wheel by that kingdom's index in `ALL_KINGDOMS`, fixed saturation/lightness for a readable pastel regardless of hue — legibility against the busy terrain comes from `.map-label`'s existing dark stroke outline (the same halo trick as everywhere else on this map), not from the hue itself, so no `buildBorderMarkers()`-style visibility fix was needed here. One real trap found and fixed while building this: `.map-label`'s CSS used to declare `fill:var(--gold-lt)` directly, and a CSS class rule beats a plain SVG `fill` *attribute* regardless of selector specificity — confirmed empirically (an isolated test: a `fill="red"` attribute rendered gold under a bare `.cls{fill:gold}` rule) before trusting the fix. Setting `.style.fill` (inline style, which always wins) instead of `.setAttribute('fill', …)`, and dropping the now-redundant CSS declaration, was the actual fix — the first version used `.setAttribute`, which the CSS class silently overrode back to gold, caught in this session's own pre-ship render check rather than shipped broken like the border line's first two rounds were.

  **Kingdom territory-name labels — done.** Per the project owner, a kingdom's own name now floats over its territory when the map is zoomed out, the way a real political map labels a country rather than only its cities. `buildKingdomTerritoryLabels()`/`positionKingdomLabels()` (index.html, right after `buildLabels()`) place one label per kingdom that currently controls at least one settlement, at the population-weighted centroid of everything it holds (`getControllerKingdom`, not original `b.state`) — weighted so the label lands near a kingdom's real population center (its capital and major cities) rather than getting dragged toward a cluster of minor villages. Colored via the same `kingdomColor()` as the settlement labels, so the two visually tie together.

  **Sized to territory, with collision avoidance — done.** Per the project owner, a flat font size for every kingdom read as clutter, especially where two small, close kingdoms' names collided. Font size now scales with each kingdom's own population-weighted spread — `sqrt` of the weighted variance of its controlled burgs' positions around their centroid, a "typical territory radius" in logical units, cheap to derive from data already gathered for the centroid — mapped through `KINGDOM_LABEL_REF_RADIUS` (`300`, sampled against the real `travel-graph.json` data so the single biggest kingdom's radius lands close to it) to a `KINGDOM_LABEL_MIN_PX`–`MAX_PX` (`10`–`26`) range, so a one-city kingdom reads small and a kingdom spread across dozens of settlements reads large. Sizing alone didn't fully stop nearby small kingdoms' names from overlapping, so `positionKingdomLabels()` also runs a lightweight collision-avoidance pass (`KINGDOM_LABEL_SEPARATION_PASSES`, `6` iterations) whenever the layer is actually shown: each label starts at its ideal centroid screen position (each one's rendered box size measured once via `getBoundingClientRect()` when built), then any two whose padded bounding boxes actually overlap get pushed apart along the line between their centers — split evenly, along whichever axis needs less movement to clear — a standard, cheap force-relaxation label layout, not true bin-packing. Recomputed from scratch on every call (from each label's *ideal* position, not the previous frame's already-nudged one), so it stays stable and deterministic under repeated pan/zoom instead of drifting further with each frame. Both the sizing and the separation were confirmed together by rendering the exact shipped code against the real data before shipping — visibly fixed the worst offenders (two adjacent small principalities whose names fully overlapped before this).

  Deliberately **not** part of the scaled `#overlay` SVG every other map element lives in — a new `#kingdom-labels-layer` div, a plain sibling of `#map-stage` inside `#map-viewport`, positioned in screen space and repositioned on every pan/zoom via `positionKingdomLabels()` (called from `applyView()`). That's a real architectural exception, not a stylistic one: `clampView()`'s `minScale` (the "fit the whole map" zoom level, now cached in `mapMinScale`) is directly proportional to the viewport's own CSS width, so at the exact zoom level these labels are meant to show, a fixed *logical* font size (the convention every other label here uses) would render at wildly different, often illegible, CSS pixel sizes across a phone versus a desktop. A fixed *screen*-space font size sidesteps that. Shown only while `view.scale <= mapMinScale * KINGDOM_LABEL_ZOOM_MULT` (`1.4`, checked in `positionKingdomLabels()`, toggling `.show` on the layer for a CSS opacity fade rather than a hard cut) — verified against the real `travel-graph.json`/`world-raster.jpg` at both a genuinely-zoomed-out and a zoomed-in `view.scale` before shipping, not just checked in isolation. Built once on load and rebuilt after the weekly faction tick, same as `buildBorderMarkers()`, since that's the only place territory control changes.

  Known simplification, same shape as `buildBorderMarkers()`'s own: one centroid per kingdom, not one per contiguous landmass — a kingdom holding a scattered exclave (a captured settlement far from its core territory) pulls its one label somewhat toward that exclave rather than getting a second label placed over it. `travel-graph.json` has no per-settlement polygon to place a label "inside" a shape properly; a population-weighted centroid is the simple, honest version of this the data supports. Dense clusters of small, closely-spaced kingdoms can also produce overlapping labels (no collision-avoidance layout) — acceptable for a first pass, not addressed.
- **`buildCityIcons()`** draws a castle-medallion image (`CITY_ICON_HREF`, `assets/icons/Map/Cities.png` — a 1024×848 coin-style icon, transparent outside the circle) centered exactly on City/Capital settlements' own point, `20`-wide for capitals vs. `14`-wide for cities — the icon *is* their marker, not an addition sitting above a separate dot. `CITY_ICON_OFFSET_Y` went `-11` → `-6` → `0` across three passes as the reasons for offsetting it at all (clearing Azgaar's baked label, then clearing a dot that no longer exists for this tier) both went away. The larger old offsets were also why so many icons went missing entirely: `buildCityIcons()` skips drawing an icon if its position would land within `CITY_ICON_MIN_CLEARANCE` (now `7`, was `12`) of *any* other settlement's point, and the bigger the offset, the more often that false-triggered in dense clusters — Bary's own icon was one of the ones silently disappearing before this got fixed.

## Waiting at a location

A second fixed control, `#wait-controls` (bottom-left, alongside `#recenter-btn`/`#camp-here-btn`, hidden together with `#camp-here-btn` while `#travel-controls` is showing), lets the player pass time without traveling anywhere — Play, Pause, and a 3× speed toggle. Per the project owner, this is deliberately a **safe** fast-forward, distinct from both travel and camping:

- **It still raises Hunger/Thirst, at the same effective rate travel does — and the real clock moves live, every tick, not just on day boundaries.** `waitTick()` (fired every `WAIT_TICK_MS` = 1 real second by `startWaiting()`'s `setInterval`) accrues `WAIT_HOURS_PER_TICK` (2) in-game hours per tick (×3 while the speed toggle is on): each tick recomputes `hoursToHungerGain`/`hoursToThirstGain` against the *running total* of hours waited so far, applying only the delta since the last tick — the same incremental-delta pattern the travel display uses, just applied as real `addHunger`/`addThirst` calls here instead of a display interpolation, since there's no fixed trip length to interpolate toward — and calls `advanceTime(delta, true)` with that same per-tick delta, which is a real (non-preview) clock advance, unlike travel's live display. So watching the map while waiting genuinely shows the clock ticking forward and the night overlay shifting in real time, not just a jump whenever a day happens to roll over. No affordability gate — waiting can continue indefinitely; ignoring the meters just costs Health via their overflow handling (see [player-state.md](player-state.md)).
- **It never rolls an ambush and never runs the weekly faction AI tick** — `waitTick()` always passes `quiet:true` to `advanceTime`, which skips `chargeMercenaryUpkeep()`/`maybeRunFactionAI()` even on a day it crosses, and never calls `rollAmbush` at all. This is the one deliberate difference from every other way time passes (travel, camping, resting at the Inn all go through `advanceTime`/`restThroughNight` non-quiet) — the project owner wanted waiting to be a pure convenience, not a way to (accidentally or deliberately) trigger events faster or more safely than normal play.
- **No direct Health/Stamina effect** — waiting doesn't drain or regenerate either one itself (resting stays the Tavern/Camp's own role); the only way waiting affects Health is indirectly, through Hunger/Thirst overflow if left unmanaged long enough.
- **Stopped automatically, not just by Pause**, whenever the player leaves the open map for something else that owns the calendar/screen itself — `stopWaiting()` is called at the top of `showLocationView`, `showCampView`, and `beginTravel`, so waiting can never keep silently ticking behind another screen.
- Not persisted — `waitState` is a transient, real-time-only object (same as `activeTravel`); closing the tab mid-wait simply loses whatever partial hour/Food progress hadn't crossed a whole-unit boundary yet, same as travel would.

## Camping from the map

Beyond interrupting an active journey, a fixed "MAKE CAMP" button (`#camp-here-btn`, stacked above the recenter button, hidden while `#travel-controls` is showing) lets the player camp on the spot at any time — see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md) for the standalone camp flow. Resting always fully restores Health and Stamina (a little more than full Stamina if a Bedroll is carried, see [player-state.md](player-state.md)) but doesn't touch Hunger/Thirst — those only come down by eating/drinking (inventory.html) or a Pub's Meal & Drink (see [locations-and-camp.md](locations-and-camp.md)).
